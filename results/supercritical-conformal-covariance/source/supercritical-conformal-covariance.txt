                    Conformal covariance of the supercritical
                       Liouville quantum gravity metric


                                 Revised draft — October 1, 2026


                                                  Abstract
         We prove conformal covariance for the canonical supercritical Liouville quantum gravity
      metric. The replacement for uniform convergence of metrics is a local gluing argument at
      endpoints surrounded by short loops. The proof uses lower-semicontinuous distance functions,
      compact confinement, local annular estimates for supercritical metrics, and a countable-scale
      endpoint regularity condition. No subcritical metric-convergence theorem is used.


1    Statement and conventions
Fix ξ > ξcrit and write Q = Q(ξ) ∈ (0, 2). Throughout, D denotes one fixed normalization of the
canonical metric assignment of Ding–Gwynne [1]. Metrics are allowed to take the value +∞. If
W ⊂ C is open, Dh (x, y; W ) denotes the intrinsic distance obtained by infimizing the Dh -length
over paths contained in W ; the infimum over an empty family of finite-length paths is +∞.

Theorem 1.1 (Conformal covariance). Let U, V ⊂ C be domains and let ϕ : U → V be a
deterministic conformal bijection. Let h be a GFF plus a continuous function on V , in the local
sense in which the canonical metric is defined. Then almost surely, simultaneously for all u, v ∈ U ,

                             Dh (ϕ(u), ϕ(v); V ) = Dh◦ϕ+Q log |ϕ′ | (u, v; U ).                       (1)

The probability-one event is allowed to depend on ϕ.

   Thus the assertion includes singular endpoints and the multiplicative constant is exactly one.
We make no assertion here about one event holding simultaneously for all conformal maps.
   For the proof it is more convenient to use the forward convention. Thus h is a field on U and

                                     hϕ = h ◦ ϕ−1 + Q log |(ϕ−1 )′ |,                                 (2)

and we write
                       d(u, v) = Dh (u, v; U ),     e(u, v) = Dhϕ (ϕ(u), ϕ(v); V ).
We first take h to be the restriction of a whole-plane GFF to U .
   Write Br (z) = {w : |w − z| < r} and Aa,b (z) = Bb (z) \ Ba (z). For a metric D, D(around A)
denotes the infimum of lengths of loops in A separating its two boundary components, and D(across A)
denotes its crossing distance. Boundary endpoints are interpreted using paths in the closed annulus.
The internal metric on a closed set is defined using paths contained in that set.
   We use the following established inputs.

1.1   Compact path compactness
We record one consequence that will be used repeatedly.

Lemma 1.2 (Compact path compactness). Let K be a compact subset of a domain on which the
field is locally a whole-plane GFF plus a continuous function. If Pn : [0, 1] → K are paths with
uniformly bounded metric lengths and are parametrized at constant metric speed, then (Pn ) has a
Euclidean-uniformly convergent subsequence. If Pn → P uniformly, then

                                  len(P ; Dh ) ≤ lim inf len(Pn ; Dh ).
                                                  n→∞

Consequently the metric intrinsic to a fixed Euclidean compact set is Euclidean lower semicontinuous.

Proof. Cover K by finitely many coordinate neighborhoods Wj with Wj contained in slightly larger
neighborhoods Wj′ . On Wj′ write the field as a whole-plane GFF plus a continuous correction and
extend the correction continuously, with a cutoff, to the plane. Locality identifies the lengths of
subpaths contained in Wj with the corresponding ambient lengths. The inverse-Hölder estimate for
the ambient metric gives a common Euclidean modulus of continuity for constant-speed paths of
bounded metric length; a finite cover yields equicontinuity on [0, 1]. Arzelà–Ascoli gives a uniformly
convergent subsequence.
    For lower semicontinuity, subdivide [0, 1] so that each limiting subpath lies in one Wj . Uniform
convergence puts the corresponding approximating subpaths in Wj′ for large n. Ambient lower
semicontinuity on each piece gives the desired inequality after summing over the subdivision and
then refining it. Applying the same argument to near-minimizing paths in a fixed compact set
proves lower semicontinuity of its intrinsic metric.

3      The tangent limit in the lower-semicontinuous topology
For nonnegative lower-semicontinuous functions, we use epigraph convergence: fn → f if

    (i) f (x) ≤ lim inf n fn (xn ) whenever xn → x;

 (ii) for each x there exists xn → x with fn (xn ) → f (x).

For distance functions, x is a pair of Euclidean points. We metrize local epigraph convergence using
closed epigraphs on a countable exhaustion of C2 × [0, ∞]. This gives a Polish realization of the
relevant space of extended-valued lower-semicontinuous functions.

Lemma 3.1 (Closed-set metric continuity under total variation). Fix closed disks K ⋐ K ′ . Let
gn , g be random generalized functions on a neighborhood of K ′ which can be coupled so that gn = g
on K ′ with probability tending to one. Let fn , f be continuous functions on K ′ with ∥fn − f ∥∞ → 0
in probability. Then the intrinsic metrics on K associated with gn + fn converge jointly with the
fields, in probability in the local epigraph topology, to the intrinsic metric associated with g + f .

Proof. On the event gn = g on K ′ , locality identifies the metric functionals on K before adding the
continuous corrections. Simultaneous Weyl scaling gives, for every path P ⊂ K,

                 e−ξ∥fn −f ∥∞ len(P ; Dg+f ) ≤ len(P ; Dg+fn ) ≤ eξ∥fn −f ∥∞ len(P ; Dg+f ).

After infimizing, the same comparison holds for the intrinsic distances on K. Since these intrinsic
distance functions are lower semicontinuous by Lemma 1.2, uniform multiplicative convergence on
finite values implies epigraph convergence, with +∞ values preserved as well.

Lemma 3.2 (Expanding confinement). Let h be a whole-plane GFF. For every fixed R < ∞ and
every δ > 0, there exists S > R such that with probability at least 1 − δ, every Dh -distance between
points of BR is unchanged when paths are restricted to BS .

Proof. Choose a large annular scale. By the short-loop barrier construction in Ding–Gwynne [1,
Lemma 2.11 and its proof], with probability tending to one as the outer scale increases there exists
a loop separating BR from infinity whose Dh -length is strictly less than half the cost of crossing a
farther surrounding annulus. Any path between two points of BR that exits that outer annulus
hits the loop before and after the excursion; replacing the excursion by the shorter arc of the loop
strictly decreases its length. Hence no distance-minimizing sequence needs to exit the outer disk.
The same argument is valid when one or both distances are infinite.

4.1   Measurability of failing regular pairs
Lemma 4.2 (Universal measurability). Fix a compact annulus A, constants M, b > 0, and η > 0.
On the Polish product space consisting of two lower-semicontinuous length-distance functions on
A, together with endpoints and a continuous path, the set of tuples for which the path witnesses a
dyadically regular pair of source distance at most M , endpoint separation at least b, and comparison
failure
                                        e(u, v) ≥ (1 + η)d(u, v)

is Borel after the path is included as a coordinate. Its projection to the two metric coordinates
is analytic and hence universally measurable. On that projected event there exists a universally
measurable choice of witnessing endpoints; a witnessing path may also be selected, but it will not be
needed in the probabilistic subsequence argument.

Proof. Use C([0, 1], A) with the uniform topology. Endpoint conditions, Euclidean containment con-
ditions, and membership in the countable family of dyadic annuli are Borel. For lower-semicontinuous
length metrics, inequalities involving distances between specified endpoints are Borel under epigraph
convergence. Path-length sublevel conditions are Borel after expressing length as the supremum over
polygonal partitions with rational times; the geodesic condition is the conjunction of the endpoint
condition with equality between path length and endpoint distance. The regularity conditions (7)–(8)
involve only countably many dyadic scales and therefore remain Borel. Hence the full witnessing set
is Borel.
    A projection of a Borel subset of a product of Polish spaces is analytic. Analytic sets are
universally measurable, and the Jankov–von Neumann selection theorem supplies a universally
measurable selector on the projected event. Projecting the selector further gives universally
measurable witnessing endpoints in the compact endpoint set. Completing the underlying probability
space therefore permits these endpoint variables to be adjoined to subsequence representations
without any separate tightness issue for the path coordinate. Equivalently, one may formulate the
argument throughout with outer probabilities.

7    Local field variants and the inverse map
Lemma 7.1 (Transfer under local continuous corrections). Assume the one-sided inequality of
Proposition 6.1 has been proved for restrictions of a whole-plane GFF. Then the same inequality
holds for every field which is locally a GFF plus a continuous function.

Proof. Fix disks B ⋐ B ′ ⋐ U . Decompose the given local field on B ′ as a zero-boundary GFF plus
its harmonic part and the prescribed continuous correction. Couple the zero-boundary component
with the restriction of a whole-plane GFF. On the smaller disk B, the difference of the two fields
is a random continuous function: it is the difference of their harmonic parts plus the prescribed
continuous term.
    The one-sided inequality for the whole-plane field holds on a probability-one event. Simultaneous
continuous Weyl scaling holds, on another probability-one event, for every continuous correction at
once. Hence adding the random continuous difference transfers the inequality pathwise to the given
local field on B. Under the conformal map, the same correction is composed with the inverse map,
so corresponding path weights agree exactly.
    Choose a countable disk cover with rational centers and radii, each disk compactly contained in
a slightly larger one, and intersect the corresponding probability-one events. Any finite-length path
has compact image and can be subdivided into finitely many pieces lying in members of this cover.
Adding the local inequalities over the subdivision and then infimizing gives the one-sided inequality
for the intrinsic metrics of the full domain, simultaneously for all endpoint pairs.
