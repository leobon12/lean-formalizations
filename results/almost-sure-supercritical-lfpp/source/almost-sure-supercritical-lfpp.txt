               Almost sure convergence of supercritical
                      Liouville first passage percolation
                                          Research manuscript

                                             October 1, 2026


                                                 Abstract
          We prove almost sure convergence of heat-kernel Liouville first passage percolation in the
      supercritical phase, in the topology on lower semicontinuous metrics and with the usual median
      crossing normalization. The main estimate compares distances between shrinking endpoint
      neighborhoods with a multiplicative constant arbitrarily close to one and polynomially small
      failure probability. To improve the comparison constant, we use separating paths whose lengths
      are measured in the other metric, together with a covering argument in the time parameter of a
      geodesic. The estimate is uniform over geodesics contained in a fixed Euclidean ball. Rational-
      circle distances identify the constant and provide the normalizer control needed to pass from a
      polynomial mesh to all positive real cutoffs.

1.1   Notation and imported results

Remark 3.3. The universal endpoint statements above can be treated as measurable events, without
a separability assumption in the Euclidean topology for the metric itself. Here are details. On a
fixed closed annulus, the internal metrics are lower semicontinuous: parameterize paths of bounded
length at constant speed, use the local inverse Hölder bound to obtain Euclidean equicontinuity,
and pass to a subsequence. Lower semicontinuity bounds the length of the limit by the lower limit
of the lengths. More precisely, for a length bound T , use the compact family of paths on [0, 1]
satisfying d(P (s), P (t)) ≤ T |s − t|. These conditions are closed by lower semicontinuity and can be
checked at rational times. Every path of length at most T has such a parameterization. Evaluation
of path length is the supremum over rational partitions, hence is jointly measurable. Projection
over the compact path families gives a measurable random internal lower semicontinuous metric;
one may localize on countably many bounds for the inverse Hölder constants. For smooth LFPP
the same argument uses bounds on its positive density on the annulus. Locality on a closed annulus
follows by computing path lengths with the internal metric on a slightly larger open annulus: its
boundary is at positive D-distance from the smaller annulus, so suﬀiciently short pieces of any
rectifiable path have the same internal and ambient distance.
    For any lower semicontinuous g, g [η] is upper semicontinuous: any point witnessing a strict
upper bound continues to witness it after a suﬀiciently small change of the center. The inequalities
of Lemma 3.2 are therefore measurable maximum conditions on a compact truncated epigraph of
the source internal metric. Alternatively, their failure sets are projections of jointly Borel sets with
compact sections after specifying a positive rational strict gap. These observations also justify the
probabilistic use of Lemma 3.1. Locality can be taken with respect to the completed field sigma
algebra.

7    Proof of the main theorem
Proof of Theorem 1.1. For each integer j ≥ 1 and integer R ≥ 1, apply Proposition 5.1 with
u = 1/j. Choose a polynomial mesh n−a(j,R) on which the failure probabilities are summable.
There is one probability-one event on which all these mesh comparisons hold eventually, all compact
field comparisons in Lemma 2.1 hold, and Lemma 6.2 holds. Work on this event. Lemma 6.1 is
deterministic and is available for each of the countably many mesh exponents.
    We first prove (1.2). Take arbitrary εn → 0 and (zn , wn ) → (z, w). If the lower limit of
their distances is infinite there is nothing to prove. Otherwise pass to a subsequence on which
the distances are bounded and converge to their finite lower limit. The relevant ordinary LFPP
geodesics exist and are confined to a random compact set by Lemma 6.2. For a fixed j, choose a
large integer R which also contains all bounded-cost localized paths starting near these endpoints;
this is possible by the same lemma. Transfer the ordinary geodesic paths to the adjacent cutoff of
the mesh for (j, R) using compact field comparison and Lemma 6.1. Their localized mesh lengths

are at most (1 + o(1))Xεn (zn , wn ). The localized mesh minimizing geodesics also lie in BR (0). The
reverse comparison (5.2) gives radii ρn → 0 such that

                       D(Bρn (zn ), Bρn (wn )) ≤ (1 + 1/j)(1 + o(1))Xεn (zn , wn ).

Lower semicontinuity implies that the lower limit of the left side is at least D(z, w). Now let j → ∞.
Since the probability-one event did not depend on the endpoint sequences, this proves the required
lower-limit assertion simultaneously for all such sequences.
    For recovery, fix (z, w) with D(z, w) < ∞. The diagonal case z = w is immediate. Otherwise
choose a D-geodesic in some BR (0). For each j, the forward comparison on the mesh for (j, R)
provides endpoints approaching z, w whose localized distances are at most (1 + 1/j)D(z, w) + o(1).
Bounded-cost minimizing paths between these endpoints are confined to a fixed larger compact
set by Lemma 6.2. Compact field comparison and Lemma 6.1 transfer them to every intervening
ordinary cutoff. Thus, for each j, choose bj > 0 so that at every real cutoff ε < bj there are
endpoints within 1/j of z, w whose ordinary normalized distance is at most (1 + 2/j)D(z, w) + 1/j.
Decrease these thresholds so that bj ↓ 0. For small ε, let j(ε) be the largest j such that ε < bj ,
and choose the corresponding endpoints. Then j(ε) → ∞, giving recovery through all cutoffs.
No intersection over uncountably many endpoint-dependent probability-one events is taken: these
choices are deterministic arguments on the one event fixed at the start of the proof. The already
proved lower bound gives the matching lower limit of the recovery values.
    If D(z, w) = ∞, the lower-limit assertion applied to the constant pair forces Xε (z, w) → ∞,
so that pair itself is a recovery pair. These are exactly the two defining conditions of the lower
semicontinuous topology. The coupling and normalization have remained those of (1.1) throughout.



A Audit record
This appendix records sequential self-audits, not independent referee reports. It concerns the
argument in this manuscript, including the new deterministic contraction, rather than an attempted
direct application of a subcritical theorem.

Round 1: source scope and deterministic geometry
The initial draft used an open source annulus whose boundary contained the selected endpoints.
It was replaced by V = {1/2 ≤ |z| ≤ 3}. An unnecessary claim that a separating path could
be made simple was removed; the joining argument uses its parametrized image directly. The
distinction between ordinary and localized rational-circle convergence was repaired by Lemma 2.2.
Root-scale units are now explicit, and A is chosen before the error tolerance. The cited annulus
concentration, fixed-constant comparison, geodesic existence, and scaling statements were checked
in their supercritical scope.

Round 2: locality, dependencies, and constants
The localized field was checked against the precise cutoff definition in [2, Section 2.3]: it is the choice
q = 1, so Proposition 3.6 applies directly. The source domains, target domains, and convolution
dependence neighborhoods all lie inside the root annulus Ar/16,32r . Measurability of the uncountable
endpoint comparisons is addressed in Remark 3.3; no density of finite-distance points in the metric
is assumed. The annulus separation ratio and lattice union bound were checked explicitly. The
fraction λ is fixed before the error tolerance, and every iteration uses only finitely many exponent

                                                    13
bands. The output-ball constant was made uniformly 513 to remove a strict-boundary ambiguity.
The dependency chain is fixed-constant comparison, confinement in probability, localized circle
convergence, contraction, near-one comparison, normalizer interpolation, and finally almost sure
confinement. In particular, the normalizer argument does not use almost sure metric convergence.

Round 3: exact conclusion and source recheck
The final pass checked the proof backwards from the two defining conditions of the lower semi-
continuous topology. Bounded-cost paths are confined before any compact field interpolation is
used. The escape proof stops paths at their first exit and therefore does not assume the confine-
ment it proves. The final probability-one event involves only countably many mesh exponents and
Euclidean disks; the endpoint sequences are arbitrary after that event is fixed. Recovery through
every real cutoff is now given by an explicit diagonal choice. Infinite distances are handled by the
lower-limit condition, with no subtraction of infinities. The normalizer is the same unit-square
crossing median at every step. The fixed-annulus LFPP tightness input was checked directly in [6,
Proposition 4.1], and the field bound was attributed directly to [2, Lemma 2.3]. A locality over-
statement in the separating-path lemma was corrected: its event is asserted only for the truncated
metric Yδ,L , with the dependence-radius condition stated explicitly. Ordinary LFPP is used there
only to bound marginal probabilities. This pass also made explicit why the closed-domain path
lengths remain local and why the joining argument never assigns a finite metric cost to an arbitrary
Euclidean arc. No unresolved step was identified in these three self-audits; they are not a substitute
for independent mathematical review.
