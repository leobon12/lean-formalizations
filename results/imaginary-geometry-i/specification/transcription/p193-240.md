### 6.2 Existence and continuity of Loewner driving functions

The purpose of this subsection is to establish the existence and continuity of the Loewner driving function of $\eta_{\theta_1}$, viewed as a path in the right connected component of $\mathbf{H} \setminus \eta_{\theta_2}$. We will also describe related results which hold in the setting of multiple flow lines and counterflow lines. We begin with the following proposition, which gives criteria which imply that a continuous curve $\eta$ in $\overline{\mathbf{H}}$ starting from $0$ has a continuous Loewner driving function.

<a id="prop-6.12"></a>
**Proposition 6.12.** *Suppose that $T \in (0, \infty]$. Let $\eta \colon [0, T) \to \overline{\mathbf{H}}$ be a continuous, non-crossing curve with $\eta(0) = 0$. Assume $\eta$ satisfies the following: for every $t \in (0, T)$,*

*(a) $\eta((t, T))$ is contained in the closure of the unbounded connected component of $\mathbf{H} \setminus \eta((0, t))$ and*

<!-- page 211 -->

*(b) $\eta^{-1}(\eta([0, t]) \cup \mathbf{R})$ has empty interior in $(t, T)$.*

*For each $t > 0$, let $g_t$ be the conformal map which takes the unbounded connected component of $\mathbf{H} \setminus \eta([0, t])$ to $\mathbf{H}$ with $\lim_{z \to \infty} |g_t(z) - z| = 0$. After reparameterization, $(g_t)$ solves the Loewner equation*

$$ \partial_t g_t(z) = \frac{2}{g_t(z) - U_t}, \qquad g_0(z) = z, $$

*with continuous driving function $U_t$.*

Roughly speaking, the first hypothesis states that $\eta$ never enters a loop consisting of either its own range or part of $\partial\mathbf{H}$ upon closing it. The second condition intuitively means that $\eta$ traces neither $\partial\mathbf{H}$ nor itself. **Precise convention for non-crossing in this criterion.** A path in $\overline{\mathbf{H}}$ is called non-crossing here if, on every compact parameter interval $[0, S]$, it is the uniform limit of injective continuous paths $\gamma_n : [0, S] \to \overline{\mathbf{H}}$ with $\gamma_n(0) = 0$ and $\gamma_n((0, S]) \subset \mathbf{H}$. The order of traversal is part of this condition. An assertion merely about the absence of transversal intersections of the range would not specify it. In particular, the condition is not inferred from local connectedness of the hull boundary.

<a id="prop-6.12-proof"></a>
*Proof of Proposition 6.12 under the stated non-crossing convention.* Write $K_t$ for the filling of $\eta([0, t])$ in $\mathbf{H}$, and $D_t = \mathbf{H} \setminus K_t$. We first check strict increase, as required by the deterministic theorem we shall apply. Suppose that $K_s = K_t$ for some $0 \le s < t < T$. Choose $r$ strictly between $s$ and $t$. For every $u \in (r, t)$, hypothesis (a) and the equality of the hulls put $\eta(u)$ in $K_r \cap \overline{D_r}$. The boundary of a filled trace is contained in its trace together with $\mathbf{R}$; hence

$$ \eta((r, t)) \subset \eta([0, r]) \cup \mathbf{R}. $$

This contradicts (b) at time $r$. Thus $K_s \subsetneq K_t$ whenever $s < t$.

Yuan's deterministic characterization of Loewner traces [Yua22, Theorem 1.1, (iii)$\Rightarrow$(i)] now applies: the curve is continuous, its filled hulls are strictly increasing, and the non-crossing convention supplies the required uniform simple approximations. It gives the local growth property. Consequently half-plane capacity is a continuous strictly increasing parameter, and the Loewner transform is continuous. Parametrizing by $\operatorname{hcap}(K_t)/2$ yields the displayed Loewner equation with initial condition $g_0(z) = z$.

The cited implication is a deterministic conformal-topological result; it uses no imaginary-geometry theorem. Its finite-interval form is obtained by restricting to any $[0, S]$ with $S < T$; the resulting drivers agree on overlaps by uniqueness of the hydrodynamically normalized maps. This proves the assertion up to its terminal capacity time. $\square$

Each component of the open parameter set $\beta^{-1}(\mathbb{H})$ is consequently a crosscut whose two real endpoints bound a base interval, and these base intervals have disjoint interiors in their traversal order. There are at most countably many such crosscuts, since their open parameter intervals are disjoint. Thus the logarithm of the derivative, normalized at infinity, has imaginary part exactly zero at every such point. The assertion continues to the completed crosscut: in a neighborhood of $x$ the maps and their derivatives converge by analytic reflection, since that neighborhood is separated from the closed hull. $$ \operatorname{Im} \log g_K'(x) = 0 \quad (x \in I). $$

**Aux. lemma 46** (Factorization of component field–path pairs). *Let $A$ be a random environment. Given $A$, let $(X_j)_{j \ge 1}$ be a countable family of independent random variables with fixed laws $\nu_j$. Suppose that, conditionally on $A$ and the entire family $(X_j)$, the variables $(H_j)_{j \ge 1}$ are independent, with conditional law $K_j(X_j, dH_j)$ for each $j$; the kernels $K_j$ do not depend on $A$. <!-- page 223 -->

*law is a kernel $L_A$ and which is independent of all the $H_j$ under this same conditioning. Then, conditionally on $A$, the pairs $(X_j, H_j)$ are independent with laws*

$$ \nu_j(dX_j) K_j(X_j, dH_j), \tag{CPF1} $$

<a id="aux-46-proof"></a>
*Proof.* Take bounded measurable functions $F_j(X_j, H_j)$ for finitely many indices and a bounded measurable function $F_0(H_0)$. First condition on $A$ and all the $X_j$. The conditional expectation of the product is

$$ L_A F_0 \prod_j \int F_j(X_j, h) K_j(X_j, dh). $$

$$ L_A F_0 \prod_j \int\!\!\int F_j(x, h) K_j(x, dh) \nu_j(dx). $$

**Aux. lemma 47** (Brownian driving noise after retaining the hull germ). *Let $(h, W, V)$ have the original optional hull specification, with its own driving filtration $(\mathcal{F}_t)$ and lifetime $T_*$. $$ \mathbb{E}[YF \mid \mathcal{F}_\tau] = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{F}_\tau]\, \mathbb{E}[F \mid \mathcal{F}_\tau]. \tag{GB1} $$

*Only weak uniqueness of the integrated SDE, including its admissible entrance states, is needed here. The same factorization holds when $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ is replaced by the sigma-algebra of the recorded stopped driving history, including $\tau$. This latter form is useful at announced pocket births and does not require identifying that sigma-algebra with a right augmentation.*

<a id="aux-47-proof"></a>
*Proof.* The sigma-algebras $\mathcal{G}_t^0$ are increasing. Indeed, the old hull is a function of the later recorded history and is contained in the later hull. The field restriction to each shrinking neighborhood of the old hull is consequently measurable from the corresponding restriction near the later hull. Passing to the canonical germs gives the inclusion, with the histories retained. Equivalently, one may use monotone finite covers and the identity $Q_{C_1} Q_{C_2} = Q_{C_1}$ when $C_1 \subseteq C_2$. The same inclusion holds at ordered own-filtration stops.

Fix a deterministic capacity horizon $N < \infty$ and first stop at a bounded announcing time $\sigma < T_*$. All identities below use the stopped history through $t \wedge \sigma$, $0 \le t \le N$. Choose $z = iY$ and a normalized nonnegative radial smooth test function $\phi$ supported on a disk about $z$ which lies wholly above height $2\sqrt{N}$. The deterministic Loewner hull-height bound keeps this disk outside every hull being considered.

The history-marked local-set law identifies the conditional mean outside the hull with the canonical harmonic function $M_t$, even after its germ is retained. Harmonic averaging therefore gives

$$ \mathbb{E}[(h, \phi) \mid \mathcal{G}^0_{t \wedge \sigma}] = M_{t \wedge \sigma}(z). \tag{GB2} $$

The right side is a square-integrable martingale in this stopped germ filtration. Completion changes no conditional identity.

The integrated SDE and the already established harmonic-mean Itô calculation give, in the own filtration,

$$ dM_t(z) = k_t(z)\, dB_t, \qquad k_t(z) = 2 \operatorname{Im} \frac{1}{g_t(z) - W_t} < 0. \tag{GB3} $$

is a continuous local martingale in the enlarged germ filtration. It equals $B_{t \wedge \sigma}$. Splitting a bounded test of the raw germ at $s$ into these two events proves this assertion. Its continuous martingale version again passes to the usual augmentation.

for deterministic smooth compactly supported tests. Domination by $G_{\mathbb{H}}$ justifies the Green pairings, including signed tests, since its logarithmic diagonal singularity is locally integrable. Equivalently this is the Brownian point-polarity argument in **Aux.** lemma 9.

**Aux. lemma 50** (Covariance exhaustion across a continuous trace). *Fix a finite-force integrated driving law, with any parameter $\kappa > 0$, coupled with $h = h^0 + m$, where $h^0$ is a zero-boundary GFF in $\mathbb{H}$ and $m$ is deterministic and is the canonical initial harmonic mean for these force data.
