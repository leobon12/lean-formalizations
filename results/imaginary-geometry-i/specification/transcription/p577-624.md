**Convention S1** (one-sided completion for a possibly noninjective history). Let $A$ be the compact range of a finite continuous own Loewner history from $0$, and let $B$ be a finite simple Loewner prefix from $0$. The one-sided predicate means that $B$ has a proper continuous injective extension $\widehat{\beta}$ in $\overline{\mathbb{H}}$ from $0$ to infinity whose full closed right Jordan side contains $A$. The right side is defined by adjoining the lower vertical ray at $0$ and using the orientation from $0$ along $\widehat{\beta}$ to infinity. No injective extension of $A$ is required. An extension is used only as a pathwise geometric witness, never as additional Gaussian conditioning data.

This predicate is universally measurable. Code its one additional proper curve by an injective continuous sphere-valued path ending at infinity and agreeing with the specified second prefix, exactly as in W0. Injectivity is the countable compact-separation condition; agreement with the prefix is Borel. Closed-right containment of the first continuous range is Borel by the same winding-index test, applied at its rational parameter values. Existence of this one curve code is therefore analytic. Both the predicate and its complement are universally measurable. No measurable choice of a witnessing extension is required.

Every such proper injective witness has the current-target growth property. If a future portion entered a bounded component cut off by an earlier prefix, it could reach infinity only by meeting that earlier prefix again or leaving the closed upper half-plane. An untraced interior point of a filled real base has only the bounded incident upper component; moving along that base to an exterior component would pass one of its already traced endpoints. Thus real contacts do not provide an exception. In particular each new real point of the witness is outside its earlier filled real base. This geometric target-growth fact is available before the gap argument below. Strict capacity and a continuous driver are asserted only on actual available portions without a real subarc, not on an artificial real-ray completion.

In particular every negative real point off $\widehat{\beta}$ lies strictly on its left: it can be joined through the lower-left half-plane to the left side of the lower vertical ray without crossing the Jordan curve. For example, the interior below a leftward upper semicircle from $0$ to a negative real endpoint is on the *left* of that oriented arc, not its right. An artificial completion is permitted to have real subarcs. The actual available prefix $B$ has none. In particular an outward negative-real ray may be used after the actual path has stopped; no stochastic evolution along that ray is asserted.

**Lemma S2** (one-sided hull restriction). Assume the setting of S1, and assume the inverse map of $D_A = \mathbb{H} \setminus \operatorname{fill}_{\mathbb{H}}(A)$ has its continuous finite-fiber extension. Write

$$ L = \min(A \cap \mathbb{R}), \qquad L \le 0. $$

For $L < 0$, let $t_L$ be the unique time at which the proper witness $\widehat{\beta}$ visits $L$; this visit exists. If $L = 0$, put $t_L = 0$. Then

$$ \widehat{\beta}([0, t_L]) \subseteq \operatorname{fill}_{\mathbb{H}}(A), \qquad \widehat{\beta}((t_L, \infty)) \subseteq \overline{D_A}. \tag{S2.1} $$

Every interior point of the second range which is off $A$ lies in $D_A$. The post-$t_L$ arc has a continuous injective proper lift in $\overline{\mathbb{H}}$ under $g_A$, starting from the left original-real base prime end $g_A(L^-)$ and ending at infinity. It uses the unique prime end selected by its free left collar at every old-range contact. On any available interval with no shared open subarc with $A$, its relative capacity is continuous and strictly increasing, and its lifted curve has a continuous Loewner driver. These assertions restrict to the actual finite prefix $B$, even when its endpoint precedes $t_L$.

Indeed their difference is the closed walk $a_L - \widehat{\beta}[0, t_L]$, contained in the closed right Jordan side. Its winding is zero on the connected unbounded left side.

The right-hand winding in (S2.2) equals $1$ in a sufficiently small left collar of any interior point, or any rightward real portion, of $\widehat{\beta}(0, t_L)$. One direct justification removes all accidental real contacts of this finite simple arc. Apply the vertical interiorizing homeomorphism of G10 with displacement at most $\epsilon$, vanishing only at the two endpoints $0, L$. The perturbed simple arc has no other real point. Thus these left-collar points have nonzero winding around a closed walk in $A \cup \mathbb{R}$. They cannot be connected to infinity inside $\mathbb{H} \setminus A$, so they belong to its filled part. This proves the first inclusion in (S2.1). When $L = 0$ that inclusion is immediate.

**No second swallowed portion.** We use the following elementary left-collar incidence fact for a proper simple Loewner arc from a real seed. After its first visit to a negative record point $L$, every component of its open left side intersected with $\mathbb{H}$ which has that later arc on its boundary either is unbounded or has an original-real base interval strictly to the left of $L$. A bounded component on the left has one of these gaps as an original-real boundary interval. At a real boundary point off the arc, an upper half-disc supplies the required open gap. The simple subarc joining the gap endpoints, together with that real interval, is its positively oriented Jordan boundary; intermediate real contacts outside the gap do not change this boundary orientation. The right gap endpoint is visited before the left endpoint. By the own target- component property, this latter endpoint is a new negative record: a new real point inside the already filled real base could not be visited. If the component has any post-$t_L$ arc on its boundary, its later endpoint is strictly below $L$. Since $L$ itself is a real contact, it cannot be inside this gap, so the entire gap is to its left. The same statement for a boundary portion approached by infinitely many real contacts follows from these countable open gaps and continuity. A component without a finite real gap is the unbounded one. This argument does not assume finitely many real contacts.

Otherwise it reaches an original-real gap below $L$. A sufficiently small upper half-disc there is in $D_A$: the real base of the compact filled hull of $A$ starts at $L$. The same collar component is therefore in $D_A$. An interior witness point off $A$ has a small neighborhood missing $A$ and is in this component as well. At a point of $A$ the

These local limits give the continuous lift. It is injective because two equal lifted points would have the same physical image and the witness is injective. It is proper because $g_A$ fixes infinity and the physical witness is proper. A simple proper lifted arc cannot enter a pocket filled by one of its own earlier prefixes: it could then leave only by revisiting that prefix or crossing the real boundary, contrary to injectivity and its proper target at infinity. Thus it has the current-target growth property. On an actual available interval the physical path has no original-real subarc. Aux. 44 and the continuous-driver criterion of Proposition 6.12 now supply the relative Loewner driver and the stated adapted capacity clock for an actual available prefix. $\square$

**Theorem S7** (the full finite-angle upper-barrier induction). For each prescribed finite angle schedule and its prescribed own stopping rules, use the full available-time conventions of Proposition 7.11. Assume $|\theta_i - \theta_j| < 2\lambda/\chi$ and $\vartheta > \max_i \theta_i$. Then every finite available physical history of the angle-varying flow satisfies the one-sided predicate S1 with every finite available prefix of the constant upper flow. This is the precise stopped-order formulation used here for a possibly self-touching first history. The assertion is almost sure for that prescribed schedule; no uncountable simultaneous family of schedules or stopping rules is claimed. Every actual switch and every original continuation threshold is retained, including a switch at an old-range or original-real point.

<a id="thm-S7-proof"></a>
*Proof.* Induct on the finite number of switches. First dispose of zero histories. If the upper available path is the singleton original seed, including an immediate continuation threshold, complete it by the outward negative-real ray; its closed right side contains all of $\overline{\mathbb{H}}$. If only the first path is a singleton, it is the common seed of any proper completion of the upper prefix. These cases require no nonempty-seed theorem. For two nonempty initial paths the constant-angle base uses W5 and S6, whose middle interval is empty for the common original seed. Retaining the upper proper completion gives S1. Assume the assertion for the stage before its next own switch, and let $A$ be that actual finite switched history. S0 supplies its entire own conditional word and finite-fiber inverse chart. If $A$ is still the singleton seed, delete the preceding zero-duration pieces and use the constant-angle base for the first nonempty piece. If the next prescribed angle has an inadmissible outgoing seed group, that piece has only its zero-duration terminal history. It adds no range to $A$, so the one-sided assertion is unchanged; no positive continuation through that threshold is asserted. The same is true of a prescribed zero-length stop. In the remaining case the new seed groups are admissible. The induction hypothesis supplies the one-sided witnesses for $A$ and every available upper prefix. Their existence is only a pathwise geometric input to S2–S4; no selected witness is retained in a Gaussian sigma-algebra.

## 18 A common separator for two finite angle schedules

This section supplies the common separator needed to apply S0–S7 to two variable-angle histories, including a finite intermediate constant-path threshold. All paths start at the same original real seed, translated to $0$, in one finite-word field. <a id="lem-S8"></a>
**Lemma S8** (containment at the terminal point of a constant barrier). Let $C$ be a nonempty maximal constant-angle flow of angle $\varphi$, with finite continuation threshold and endpoint $q > 0$. Let $\delta$ be a finite own angle schedule whose angles $\psi_j$ satisfy

## 19 A strict barrier from two ordered boundary seeds

**Theorem A4d** (the distinct-seed barrier needed in the critical component). Let $2 < \kappa < 4$, put $\Lambda = \kappa\lambda/4$, and fix $u > 0$. Let the boundary heights of the canonical field in $\mathbb{H}$ be

## 20 A critical seed limit with an admissible merged group

This section treats a singular limit at seed weight $-2$. It is separate from DS1–DS4, which require uniformly admissible uncut groups. Only the statements explicitly proved below are used; convergence of driving functions alone is not promoted to convergence of complete traces or to a frontier theorem.

<a id="lem-CS1"></a>
**Lemma CS1** (collapse to the next boundary jump). Fix $2 < \kappa < 4$ and $u > 0$. For sufficiently small $\varepsilon > 0$, start a chordal finite-force process at $u$ with the following data:

$$ \begin{array}{c|ccc} \text{mark} & u^- & 0 & u^+ \\ \hline \text{weight} & -2 + \varepsilon & \kappa - 2 & -\kappa/2 - \varepsilon. \end{array} \tag{CS1.1} $$

Let $T_\varepsilon$ be the first swallowing time of the separated mark $0$, and let $q_\varepsilon$ be the actual terminal point of this initial segment. Then $T_\varepsilon < \infty$ almost surely, and

$$ T_\varepsilon = O_{\mathbb{P}}(\varepsilon^2), \qquad q_\varepsilon \longrightarrow 0, \qquad d_{\mathrm{H}}\bigl(\gamma_\varepsilon[0, T_\varepsilon], [0, u]\bigr) \longrightarrow 0 \quad \text{in probability}. \tag{CS1.2} $$

Here the Hausdorff distance is Euclidean. If $V^2$ denotes the separated left coordinate and $V^R$ the original right coordinate, then also

$$ |V^2_{T_\varepsilon}| + |V^R_{T_\varepsilon} - u| = O_{\mathbb{P}}(\varepsilon). \tag{CS1.3} $$

The initial hulls have capacity tending to zero; their mapping-out maps tend locally uniformly to the identity on $\mathbb{H}$.

<a id="lem-CS1-proof"></a>
*Proof: an integrated two-coordinate comparison.* Put

$$ r_1 = -2 + \varepsilon, \quad R = \kappa - 2, \quad r_R = -\kappa/2 - \varepsilon, \quad S = r_1 + R, $$
<!-- page 590 -->
and, before $T_\varepsilon$, write

$$ X = W - V^1, \quad Y = W - V^2, \quad Z = V^R - W, \quad D = Y - X = V^1 - V^2, \quad A = Y - (1 - \varepsilon/2)D = X + \frac{\varepsilon D}{2}. \tag{CS1.4} $$

The initial values are $X = Z = 0$, $Y = D = u$ and $A = \varepsilon u/2$. The integrated equations give

$$ dD = -\frac{2D}{XY}\,dt, \qquad dA = \sqrt{\kappa}\,dB + \frac{S + 2}{Y}\,dt - \frac{r_R}{Z}\,dt, \tag{CS1.5} $$

and

$$ dZ = -\sqrt{\kappa}\,dB + \left(-\frac{r_1}{X} - \frac{R}{Y}\right)dt + \frac{r_R + 2}{Z}\,dt. \tag{CS1.6} $$

All identities use the integrable singular integrals at admissible nearest-seed collisions. The two left marks stay distinct while $Y > 0$: on a compact interval where $Y$ is bounded below, the integrated $1/X$ term is finite, so the difference equation keeps $D > 0$. Thus $A > 0$ until the first outer collision.

Choose $\varepsilon_0 > 0$ small enough that, for $0 < \varepsilon \le \varepsilon_0$,

$$ -2 < S < \kappa/2 - 2 < 0, \qquad -2 < r_R < 0. \tag{CS1.7} $$

Since $A \le Y$, the $A$ drift is at most $(S + 2)/A - r_R/Z$. The other drift has the reverse inequality:

$$ -\frac{r_1}{X} - \frac{R}{Y} \ge -\frac{S}{A}. \tag{CS1.8} $$

For an explicit check, put $t = X/Y$ and $a = A/Y = t + \varepsilon(1 - t)/2$. Multiplication by $Ya$ reduces the difference in (CS1.8) to

$$ (1 - t)(1 - \varepsilon/2)\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{t} + R\right) \ge 0. \tag{CS1.9} $$

The endpoint $t = 0$ is read through the integrated inequality.

On the same Brownian motion run the opposite two-force gap system

$$ \begin{aligned} d\overline{A} &= \sqrt{\kappa}\,dB + \frac{S + 2}{\overline{A}}\,dt - \frac{r_R}{\overline{Z}}\,dt, \\ d\overline{Z} &= -\sqrt{\kappa}\,dB - \frac{S}{\overline{A}}\,dt + \frac{r_R + 2}{\overline{Z}}\,dt, \end{aligned} \tag{CS1.10} $$

starting from $(\varepsilon u/2, 0)$. An integrated comparison in the order $(A, Z) \preceq (\overline{A}, \overline{Z})$ meaning $A \le \overline{A}$ and $Z \ge \overline{Z}$ gives

$$ A \le \overline{A}, \qquad Z \ge \overline{Z} \tag{CS1.11} $$

up to the first left collision of either system. Here are details that include repeated right-seed collisions. Set $a = S + 2$, $b = -r_R$, $c = -S$, $e = r_R + 2$, all positive. Stop while $A, \overline{A} \ge \delta > 0$ and all remaining finite coordinates are bounded. The two differences $p = A - \overline{A}$ and $q = \overline{Z} - Z$ are continuous finite-variation functions because their Brownian terms cancel. Let $P = p^+$, $Q = q^+$, and put

$$ J_t = \int_0^t \left(\frac{1}{Z_s} - \frac{1}{\overline{Z}_s}\right)^+ ds = \int_0^t \frac{q_s^+}{Z_s \overline{Z}_s}\,ds. \tag{CS1.11a} $$

The second expression is read off the zero-time collision sets. The first expression makes its integrability explicit: both reciprocal gaps have locally integrable singular integrals in the admissible entrance convention. The positive-part formula for continuous finite variation, (CS1.8), and $A \le Y$ give

$$ P_t \le bJ_t, \qquad Q_t + eJ_t \le c\delta^{-2} \int_0^t P_s\,ds. \tag{CS1.11b} $$
