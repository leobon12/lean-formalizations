**Lemma 6.2.** *Suppose that $\eta_1, \ldots, \eta_k$ are continuous paths with deterministic starting points, determined almost surely by $h$. Assume that they are local explorations in the following history-sensitive sense. For every deterministic open set $U$ and every stopping time $T$ of $\eta_i$, put $E = \{\eta_i([0,T]) \cap U = \varnothing\}$. Conditional on $h_{U^c}$, the pair consisting of $\mathbf{1}_E$ and the stopped history $\eta_i|_{[0,T]}$ on $E$ is independent of $h_U$; on $E^c$ the history is replaced by a fixed cemetery symbol. Here $h_U$ and $h_{U^c}$ are the orthogonal GFF projections onto $H(U)$ and $H^\perp(U)$.*

*Let $\tau_1$ be a stopping time of $\eta_1$. Inductively, for $2 \le j \le k$, let $\tau_j$ be a stopping time for the filtration generated by $\eta_1|_{[0,\tau_1]}, \ldots, \eta_{j-1}|_{[0,\tau_{j-1}]}$ and $(\eta_j(s) : s \le t)$. Then $\bigcup_{i=1}^k \eta_i([0,\tau_i])$ is a local set of $h$. More precisely, the union together with the ordered tuple of stopped histories satisfies the same history-sensitive locality condition.*

<a id="lem-6.2-proof"></a>
*Proof.* Fix a deterministic open set $U$. If $U$ contains a starting point, the union necessarily intersects $U$, so the locality criterion is immediate. Thus assume $U$ avoids all starting points. For each $i$, let $T_i^U = \inf\{t \ge 0 : \eta_i(t) \in U\}$. By continuity, the path through $T_i^U$ avoids $U$ (with the usual stopped-path convention if $T_i^U = \infty$). The hypothesis, applied at $T_i^U$, says that the entire stopped history is conditionally independent of $h_U$ given $h_{U^c}$. It is also determined by $(h_U, h_{U^c})$. Consequently it is determined by $h_{U^c}$ alone. This uses the elementary fact that a random variable which is measurable with respect to $(X, Y)$ and conditionally independent of $X$ given $Y$ is $Y$-measurable, up to completion.

We induct on the number of explored paths, retaining the ordered tuple of stopped histories. On the event that the first $j-1$ stopped paths avoid $U$, the induction hypothesis says that this event and their entire ordered histories are determined by $h_{U^c}$. Given those histories, the comparison $\tau_j \le T_j^U$ is determined by the history of $\eta_j$ through $T_j^U$, because $\tau_j$ is a stopping time for the indicated enlarged filtration. Thus it too is determined by $h_{U^c}$. On this event the stopped history through $\tau_j$ is determined by the same information. Induction gives that the event

$$\left\{\bigcup_{i=1}^k \eta_i([0,\tau_i]) \cap U = \varnothing\right\}$$

and, on this event, the entire ordered tuple of stopped histories, are determined by $h_{U^c}$. This proves the asserted history-sensitive locality. Characterization (i) in Lemma 3.6 proves locality. $\square$

*Qualification concerning applications.* The strengthened assumption concerns the whole stopped curve, including any information conveyed by its order or parametrization. In applications to SLE explorations it must be supplied by the conditional-on-the-stopped-curve GFF coupling, together with field determinacy; locality of the unmarked range by itself is insufficient. For simple paths with a fixed canonical parametrization, the stopped path is recoverable from its oriented range. For nonsimple counterflow paths, verify this property for the marked range history. Theorem 1.1 gives a conditional-on-history GFF law outside the filled hull; extending that statement to the range and all complementary components requires the corresponding decorated local-set argument. It must not be inferred solely from locality of the unmarked range.

*A sufficient condition in terms of ranges.* The history-sensitive assumption follows from the original two assumptions (range-locality at every path stopping time and field determinacy) if, in addition, the increasing family of stopped ranges determines the parametrized path. In particular, this is true for a continuous path whose range strictly increases on every nontrivial time interval before its lifetime. Indeed, for every positive rational $s$, the range through $s \wedge T_i^U$ is local, field-determined, and disjoint from $U$, and is therefore $h_{U^c}$-measurable by Lemma 3.6. The family of these ranges reconstructs the stopped path: for rational $a < t < b$ tending to $t$, any point in the nonempty difference between the ranges through $b$ and $a$ tends to $\eta_i(t)$, by continuity. After the last growth time the stopped path is constant. A Loewner trace with its fixed capacity parametrization has the required strict range growth, since a constant range on an interval would give a constant hull and hence constant capacity. Thus, whenever stopped-range locality is available for the SLE exploration, this additional explicit hypothesis suffices for its applications here.

We next prove the following simple lemma, which says that if $X, Y$ are independent, $Y$ is Gaussian, and $X + Y$ is Gaussian, then $X$ is Gaussian as well.

<a id="lem-6.3"></a>
**Lemma 6.3.** *Suppose that $X, Y$ are independent random variables such that $Y \sim N(\mu_Y, \sigma_Y^2)$ and $Z := X + Y \sim N(\mu_Z, \sigma_Z^2)$. Then $X \sim N(\mu_X, \sigma_X^2)$ where $\mu_X = \mu_Z - \mu_Y$ and $\sigma_X^2 = \sigma_Z^2 - \sigma_Y^2$.*

<a id="lem-6.3-proof"></a>
*Proof.* The proof follows from the calculus of characteristic functions. Indeed, we know that

$$\mathbf{E}[e^{i\lambda Y}] = e^{i\lambda\mu_Y - \lambda^2\sigma_Y^2/2} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf{E}[e^{i\lambda Z}] = e^{i\lambda\mu_Z - \lambda^2\sigma_Z^2/2}.$$

Since $X$ is independent of $Y$, we have

$$\mathbf{E}[e^{i\lambda Z}] = \mathbf{E}[e^{i\lambda X}]\mathbf{E}[e^{i\lambda Y}],$$

which allows us to solve for $\mathbf{E}[e^{i\lambda X}]$ to see that

$$\mathbf{E}[e^{i\lambda X}] = e^{i\lambda(\mu_Z - \mu_Y) - \lambda^2(\sigma_Z^2 - \sigma_Y^2)/2}.$$

$\square$

Suppose that $D \subseteq \mathbf{C}$ is a non-trivial simply connected domain and that $z \in D$ is fixed. Recall that the conformal radius $C(z; D)$ is the quantity $|\varphi'(0)|$ where $\varphi$ is a conformal transformation which takes the unit disk $\mathbf{D}$ to $D$ with $\varphi(0) = z$. By the Koebe-1/4 Theorem [Law05, Theorem 3.16], the ratio between the conformal radius of $z$ and the distance of $z$ to $\partial D$ is contained in $[\frac{1}{4}, 4]$. <a id="lem-6.4"></a>
**Lemma 6.4.** *Suppose that $D \subseteq \mathbf{C}$ is a non-trivial, simply connected domain. Let $h$ be a GFF on $D$ and fix $z \in D$. Suppose that $A$ is a local set for $h$ such that $D \setminus A$ is simply connected and $C(z; D \setminus A)$ is almost surely constant and positive. Then $\mathcal{C}_A(z)$ is distributed as a Gaussian random variable with mean $\mathcal{C}(z)$ and variance $\log C(z; D) - \log C(z; D \setminus A)$.*

<a id="lem-6.4-proof"></a>
*Proof.* The Koebe 1/4 theorem [Law05, Theorem 3.16] implies there exists non-random $\epsilon > 0$ such that, almost surely, $B(z, 2\epsilon) \subseteq D \setminus A$. Let $h_\epsilon$ denote the average of $h$ on $\partial B(z, \epsilon)$ (the construction and properties of the circle average process are explained in detail in [DS11, Section 3]). By [DS11, Proposition 3.2], we know that $h_\epsilon(z) \sim N(\mathcal{C}(z), -\log\epsilon + \log C(z; D))$. Since $A$ is a local set for $h$, we can write $h = h_1 + h_2$ where $h_1$ is harmonic on $D \setminus A$ and conditional on $(A, h_1)$ (equivalently, on the full local-set information $\mathcal{A}$), the law of $h_2$ is that of a zero-boundary GFF on $D \setminus A$. Since $h_1$ is harmonic in $D \setminus A$, we note that $h_1(z)$ is equal to the average of its values on $\partial B(z, \epsilon)$. Moreover, we have that $\mathbf{E}[h_\epsilon(z) \,|\, \mathcal{A}] = h_1(z)$. Consequently, we have that $h_\epsilon(z) - \mathbf{E}[h_\epsilon(z) \,|\, \mathcal{A}]$ is equal to the average of $h_2$ on $\partial B(z, \epsilon)$. Therefore it follows that $h_\epsilon(z) - \mathbf{E}[h_\epsilon(z) \,|\, \mathcal{A}]$ given $\mathcal{A}$ follows the $N(0, -\log\epsilon + \log C(z; D \setminus A))$ distribution. This implies that

$$h_\epsilon(z) - \mathbf{E}[h_\epsilon(z) \,|\, \mathcal{A}] \sim N(0, -\log\epsilon + \log C(z; D \setminus A))$$

(no longer conditioning on $\mathcal{A}$; recall that $C(z; D \setminus A)$ is almost surely constant). Since $\mathbf{E}[h_\epsilon(z) \,|\, \mathcal{A}]$ is independent of $h_\epsilon(z) - \mathbf{E}[h_\epsilon(z) \,|\, \mathcal{A}]$ (as the former is determined by $\mathcal{A}$ and the latter is independent of $\mathcal{A}$), Lemma 6.3 implies that

$$\mathbf{E}[h_\epsilon(z) \,|\, \mathcal{A}] \sim N(\mathcal{C}(z), \log C(z; D) - \log C(z; D \setminus A)).$$

Since $\mathcal{C}_A(z)$ is harmonic in $D \setminus A$, we know that $\mathcal{C}_A(z) = \mathbf{E}[h_\epsilon(z) \,|\, \mathcal{A}]$ almost surely, which proves the lemma. $\square$

**Aux. lemma 28** (Uniqueness with finitely many exceptional prime ends)**.** *Let $C$ be a bounded Jordan domain, let $f : \mathbb{D} \to C$ be conformal, and let $H$ be harmonic in $C$. Suppose that, for every $\epsilon > 0$,*

$$|H(z)| \le C_\epsilon\bigl(1 + \operatorname{dist}(z, \partial C)^{-\epsilon}\bigr).$$

*Let $\varphi : C \to \mathbb{H}$ be conformal, let $g$ be a bounded harmonic function in $\mathbb{H}$, and fix a real $\chi$ and a continuous branch of $\arg\varphi'$ in $C$. Put $F_C = g \circ \varphi - \chi\arg\varphi'$. If $H - F_C$ tends to zero continuously at every boundary point of $C$ except a finite set, then $H = F_C$ in $C$. The same conclusion holds when $C$ is merely simply connected, provided the boundary condition is interpreted in the disk and the exceptional set consists of finitely many prime ends.*

<a id="aux-28-proof"></a>
*Proof.* The Koebe quarter and derivative distortion theorems give, for $|\zeta| = r$,

$$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{dist}(f(\zeta), \partial C) &\ge \frac{1 - r^2}{4}|f'(\zeta)| \\
&\ge \frac{|f'(0)|}{4}\frac{(1 - r)^2}{(1 + r)^2} \ge \frac{|f'(0)|}{16}(1 - r)^2.
\end{aligned}$$

Consequently $H \circ f = O((1 - r)^{-2\epsilon})$, uniformly in the argument of $\zeta$, for every $\epsilon > 0$.

We also need to control the argument, rather than only the modulus, of a conformal derivative. The standard second-coefficient estimate for a normalized univalent function gives

$$\left|(1 - |\zeta|^2)\frac{f''(\zeta)}{2f'(\zeta)} - \overline{\zeta}\right| \le 2.$$

For completeness, apply $|a_2| \le 2$ to the normalized function obtained by composing $f$ with the disk automorphism $w \mapsto (\zeta + w)/(1 + \overline{\zeta}w)$; its second coefficient is the quantity

<!-- page 163 -->
inside the absolute value. In particular $|f''/f'|(\zeta) \le 6/(1 - |\zeta|^2)$. Integrating a branch of $\log f'$ along radii yields

$$|\arg f'(\zeta) - \arg f'(0)| \le 3\log\frac{1 + r}{1 - r}.$$

Since $m = \varphi \circ f$ is a disk-to-half-plane Möbius map, $\arg m'$ is bounded on the disk. The chain rule therefore shows

$$\arg\varphi' \circ f = O\!\left(1 + \log\frac{1}{1 - r}\right).$$

It follows that the harmonic function $u = (H - F_C) \circ f$ satisfies

$$\sup_{|\zeta| = r}|u(\zeta)| \le c(1 - r)^{-\alpha}$$

for some $0 < \alpha < 1$, and tends continuously to zero at every point of the unit circle outside a finite set $E$.

Here is a direct boundary-distribution argument completing the uniqueness step. Write the harmonic Fourier expansion

$$u(re^{it}) = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} c_n r^{|n|} e^{int}.$$

Taking $r = 1 - 1/|n|$ in the coefficient bound, for $|n| \ge 2$, shows $|c_n| = O(|n|^\alpha)$. Thus these coefficients define a distribution $T$ on the circle, and $u$ is its Poisson extension. The radial functions $u(re^{it})$ converge to $T$ in the distributional sense. On every compact subarc outside $E$ the convergence to zero is uniform, so $T$ is supported on $E$. A distribution supported at finitely many points is a finite linear combination of point masses and their derivatives: this follows from its finite order, since at each support point its action on a test function depends only on a finite Taylor jet.

If this combination were nonzero, choose a support point at which the highest nonzero derivative has order $m \ge 0$. At angular distance $c(1 - r)$ from that point, the Poisson extension of this derivative is $(1 - r)^{-m-1}$ times a nonzero function of $c$, to leading order. This follows by differentiating the scaling limit

$$(1 - r)P_r(c(1 - r)) \longrightarrow \frac{2}{1 + c^2}$$

for the Poisson kernel $P_r(t) = (1 - r^2)/(1 - 2r\cos t + r^2)$. Choose $c$ where the relevant derivative of $2/(1 + c^2)$ is nonzero. Lower derivatives at this point are smaller in order, and derivatives at the other, separated support points are $O(1 - r)$. Thus $\sup_{|\zeta| = r}|u(\zeta)| \ge c_0(1 - r)^{-m-1}$ for $r$ sufficiently close to one, contradicting $\alpha < 1$. Hence $T = 0$ and $u = 0$. $\square$

**Aux. lemma 29** (Uniqueness with finitely many exceptional physical points)**.** *Let $O$ be a bounded simply connected domain with locally connected boundary, and let $f : \mathbb{D} \to O$ be conformal. Let $P$ be a finite subset of $\partial O$. Suppose that $u$ is harmonic in $\mathbb{D}$, that $u \in L^2(\mathbb{D})$, and that $u$ has continuous boundary value zero at every point of*

$$\partial\mathbb{D} \setminus f^{-1}(P).$$

*Here $f$ denotes also its continuous extension to the closed disk. Then $u = 0$.*

*In particular, let $H$ be harmonic in $O$ and satisfy, for every $\epsilon > 0$,*

$$|H(z)| \le C_\epsilon\bigl(1 + \operatorname{dist}(z, \partial O)^{-\epsilon}\bigr).$$

*Let $\varphi : O \to \mathbb{H}$ be conformal, let $g$ be a bounded harmonic function in $\mathbb{H}$, and fix a continuous branch of $\arg\varphi'$ in $O$. Put*

$$F = g \circ \varphi - \chi\arg\varphi'.$$

*If $(H - F) \circ f$ has continuous boundary value zero outside $f^{-1}(P)$, then $H = F$. There is no assumption that the number of prime ends over a point of $P$ is finite.*

<a id="aux-29-proof"></a>
*Proof.* We use the Fourier definition

$$\|v\|^2_{H^{1/2}(\partial\mathbb{D})} = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}}(1 + |n|)|\widehat{v}(n)|^2.$$

The elementary disk trace estimate is

<a id="id-PU1"></a>
$$\|\operatorname{Tr} v\|^2_{H^{1/2}} \le C\left(\|v\|^2_{L^2(\mathbb{D})} + \int_{\mathbb{D}}|\nabla v|^2\right). \tag{PU1}$$

For clarity, it follows by taking angular Fourier coefficients $a_n(r)$ of a smooth $v$. For $n \ne 0$, completing the square gives

$$|n|\,|a_n(1)|^2 \le \int_0^1\left(|a_n'(r)|^2 + \frac{n^2}{r^2}|a_n(r)|^2\right) r\,dr.$$

The zero coefficient is controlled by the one-dimensional trace inequality on $[1/2, 1]$, using both its squared value and squared derivative on that interval. Summation proves (PU1) for smooth functions; density extends it to $H^1(\mathbb{D})$. If an $H^1$ function is continuous on the closed disk, its trace is its ordinary boundary restriction, by uniform radial convergence.

By adding one boundary point if necessary, we may assume that $P$ is nonempty. Write $P = \{p_1, \ldots, p_N\}$, omitting repetitions. Fix $R > 0$. For $0 < r < R$, define on the plane

$$b_{j,r}(z) = \begin{cases} 1, & |z - p_j| \le r, \\[2pt] \dfrac{\log(R/|z - p_j|)}{\log(R/r)}, & r < |z - p_j| < R, \\[2pt] 0, & |z - p_j| \ge R, \end{cases} \qquad b_r = \max_{1 \le j \le N} b_{j,r}.$$

<!-- page 165 -->
These are Lipschitz functions for each fixed $r$. Direct integration of the radial derivative gives

$$\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\nabla b_{j,r}|^2 = \frac{2\pi}{\log(R/r)}, \qquad \int_{\mathbb{C}}|\nabla b_r|^2 \le \frac{2\pi N}{\log(R/r)}.$$

For the second inequality, the gradient of the maximum agrees almost everywhere with one of the gradients at a maximizing index; on a tie set the same estimate follows from the Sobolev chain rule for maximum. Set $v_r = b_r \circ f$. Conformal invariance of the Dirichlet integral gives

$$\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\nabla v_r|^2 = \int_O|\nabla b_r|^2 \le \frac{2\pi N}{\log(R/r)} \longrightarrow 0.$$

Also $0 \le v_r \le 1$ and $v_r(\zeta) \to 0$ at every interior point: $f(\zeta)$ is not a boundary point $p_j$. Dominated convergence therefore gives $\|v_r\|_{L^2(\mathbb{D})} \to 0$. The continuous boundary functions $\psi_r = v_r|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}$ satisfy

<a id="id-PU2"></a>
$$\|\psi_r\|_{H^{1/2}} \longrightarrow 0, \qquad \psi_r = 1 \text{ on an open neighborhood of } E = f^{-1}(P). \tag{PU2}$$

The neighborhood assertion uses continuity of the boundary extension of $f$ and the fact that $b_r$ is one on an open neighborhood of $P$.

We will use smooth cutoffs, so that no pairing with a merely continuous test function is required. The set $E$ is compact. Convolve each $\psi_r$ on the circle with a nonnegative smooth approximate identity whose support is smaller than half the distance from $E$ to the complement of a neighborhood on which $\psi_r = 1$. The result $\widetilde{\psi}_r$ is smooth, equals one near $E$, and satisfies

<a id="id-PU3"></a>
$$\|\widetilde{\psi}_r\|_{H^{1/2}} \le \|\psi_r\|_{H^{1/2}} \longrightarrow 0. \tag{PU3}$$

The norm inequality follows coefficient by coefficient, since the Fourier multipliers of this convolution have absolute value at most one. Thus (PU3) establishes directly the only capacity fact needed below; no assertion about the cardinality of $E$ is involved.

Write the harmonic Fourier expansion

$$u(\rho e^{it}) = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} c_n\rho^{|n|}e^{int}.$$

Parseval's identity and integration with the area element $\rho\,d\rho\,dt$ give

<a id="id-PU4"></a>
$$\|u\|^2_{L^2(\mathbb{D})} = \pi\sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}}\frac{|c_n|^2}{|n| + 1}. \tag{PU4}$$

Consequently the coefficients $c_n$ define a distribution $T \in H^{-1/2}(\partial\mathbb{D})$ whose Poisson extension is $u$. The radial restrictions of $u$ converge to $T$ in distributions; this follows by applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the weighted coefficient sum in (PU4) against the rapidly decreasing Fourier coefficients of a smooth test function. Since the boundary

<!-- page 166 -->
convergence of $u$ is locally uniform on every arc disjoint from $E$, the distribution $T$ is supported on $E$.

For a smooth test function $a$ on the circle, support on $E$ and (PU3) give

$$\langle T, a\rangle = \langle T, a\widetilde{\psi}_r\rangle, \qquad |\langle T, a\widetilde{\psi}_r\rangle| \le \|T\|_{H^{-1/2}}\,\|a\widetilde{\psi}_r\|_{H^{1/2}} \longrightarrow 0.$$

Multiplication by a fixed smooth $a$ is bounded on $H^{1/2}$. For example this follows immediately from the equivalent norm

$$\|v\|^2_{L^2} + \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{|v(e^{it}) - v(e^{is})|^2}{|e^{it} - e^{is}|^2}\,dt\,ds$$

by writing the increment of $av$ as the sum of an increment of $v$ times $a$ and an increment of $a$ times $v$. The second increment is bounded by a constant times $|e^{it} - e^{is}|$. The norm equivalence itself follows by applying the double integral to the Fourier modes and using orthogonality; its weight is a constant multiple of $|n|$. Thus $T$ vanishes on every smooth test function, and $u = 0$.

For the final assertion, the Koebe quarter theorem and derivative distortion estimate used in **Aux.** lemma 28 give

$$\operatorname{dist}(f(\zeta), \partial O) \ge \frac{|f'(0)|}{16}(1 - |\zeta|)^2.$$

Hence $H \circ f = O((1 - |\zeta|)^{-2\epsilon})$ for every $\epsilon > 0$. The logarithmic derivative estimate in that same lemma gives

$$F \circ f = O\!\left(1 + \log\frac{1}{1 - |\zeta|}\right).$$

Choose $\epsilon < 1/4$. Both bounds are square integrable against disk area near the unit circle. Thus $(H - F) \circ f \in L^2(\mathbb{D})$, and the first part applies. $\square$

As the original target is outside $C$, this would swallow $C$ at that time. The original Loewner trace could not have an interior tip in $C$ afterward. At time $T(x_0)$ its tip is $x_0 \notin C$ as well. This proves (JP1).

The common contacts of the two flow paths have the same chronological order. If distinct common points $p, q$ had opposite orders, reverse first-visit order on the two

<!-- page 190 -->
paths would give $T(p) \le T(q) \le T(p)$. These first visits are finite unless a point is the terminal target, which cannot have opposite order to a distinct point. Equality of finite first-visit times would make the continuous counterflow occupy two distinct points at one time, a contradiction. In particular a portion of one flow before $x_0$ cannot touch an open selected side of the other flow after $x_0$: that contact and $x_0$ would have opposite orders. This also excludes extra exterior contacts with those sides, which would not be excluded by the Jordan pocket description alone.

Let $u_i$ be the first parameter at which $\alpha_i$ meets $R_s$. The first contact exists, since its terminal endpoint is the counterflow's starting point. Reverse first-visit order and (JP1) give

$$\alpha_i^{-1}(x_0) < u_i \le \alpha_i^{-1}(y_0), \qquad \alpha_i([u_i, \mathrm{end}]) \subset R_s. \tag{JP2}$$

If $x_0$ is the original flow seed, interpret its parameter as zero. The open incoming prefixes lie in $D_s$, since a prefix from the original target cannot enter a swallowed component before meeting its boundary trace. In particular $x_0 \in D_s$ unless it is that original target prime end.

Map $D_s$ to the half-plane with the original target at infinity. The first-exit prime-end lemma gives continuous simple lifts of the incoming prefixes, ending at real points $q_1, q_2$. Their two subarcs from $x_0$ to those attachments have disjoint open interiors, by the stated Jordan-pocket hypothesis. Earlier portions of either path are outside $C$, and later portions are contained in $R_s$ by (JP2). The only relative flow boundary pieces incident to $C \cap D_s$ are therefore these two incoming subarcs.

The active inverse map is continuous at the driver $W_s$. Since the physical tip is strictly inside $C$, an upper half-neighborhood of $W_s$ is mapped into $C \cap D_s$. It follows that $q_1 \ne q_2$: if the two attachments coincided, the two disjoint incoming subarcs would form a Jordan loop meeting the real boundary only at that common point. When $x_0$ is interior, the earlier incoming prefix from the original target to $x_0$ lies outside $C$ and selects the exterior side of this loop as the side outside $C$. The $C$-side is therefore its interior, which could not contain a half-neighborhood of the distinct real point $W_s$. The case in which $x_0$ is the original target is the same ordered two-crosscut picture in the real-circle compactification.

Consequently the two incoming subarcs and the real-circle interval from $q_1$ to $q_2$ facing $W_s$ bound a Jordan face. Its physical inverse is the remaining component of $C \setminus R_s$ incident to $x_0$ and the current tip. To determine the correct interval when $x_0$ is interior, the earlier incoming prefix connects $x_0$ to the original target without entering $C$. Hence the selected face is the side of the $q_1$-to-$q_2$ crosscut opposite that target. This also proves that it is the prescribed central component, rather than a different component cut off by the old counterflow.

Condition on the counterflow history and its germ and use the full marked relative union decomposition (OC2). All later flow tails are in $R_s$, by (JP2); no independence is inferred from that containment. On each open incoming side away from its angle-change

marks, an earlier stopped single-piece coupling and the nested boundary comparison give its usual height and winding correction. Compactness, injectivity, and the disjoint open sides give the required positive separation from other flow pieces there. On each open old real interval, zero-boundary decay for the relative local set preserves the stopped counterflow mean. The only exceptions in the selected Jordan face are its two attachments, the point over $x_0$, the old finite boundary marks including the driver, and the finitely many angle changes on its two incoming sides. The initial conditional mean is bounded in the fixed counterflow coordinate. The subpower estimate and finite-prime-end uniqueness therefore identify the claimed harmonic mean, exactly as in **Aux.** lemma 32.

Finally, if the current tip at $t_0$ is strictly in $C$, choose a rational interval around $t_0$ on which the counterflow stays in $C$ at positive distance from both full flow paths. The two incoming prefixes, their attachments, and all their angle-change marks are fixed on this interval. Their images remain separated from the driver and are transported continuously by the Loewner ordinary differential equation. The Jordan-face and Radó continuity argument following (OC3) therefore applies with these finitely many additional marks. The raw-range Green kernels and actual conditional means have the same continuity already proved there. Equality at all rational original times consequently extends to every strict-interior time, and the marked stopping law gives the stated enlarged stopping-time conclusion. Nothing in this argument uses a global order of the two paths after $y_0$, or asserts a limit through a time when the current tip is on their boundary. $\square$
