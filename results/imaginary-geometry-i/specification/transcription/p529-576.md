**Lemma W0b** (no common open arc on an ordered earlier prefix). For $\theta_1 \neq \theta_2$, almost surely no pair of original finite prefixes which admits the ordered proper completions of W0 contains a common nontrivial open subarc. The conclusion is used only on such earlier ordered prefixes.

<a id="id-W0b-proof"></a>
*Proof.* The contact-order theorem for the proper completions makes any common arc cooriented. Choose a compact subarc in its interior which lies in $\mathbb{H}$, and choose rational original own stopping times for both paths strictly after that compact subarc but still within the common open arc. This is possible because a nontrivial real subarc would have zero capacity. Injectivity and compactness give positive physical clearance between the chosen compact subarc and every portion of either retained history outside the common open arc.

Use the marked union at this rational stopping pair. Its common right-hand side is exposed to the joint unbounded component. One direct verification is to use the ordered proper completion of the right-hand path: its right collar avoids the other path by Aux. lemma 83. The common arc continues past the compact piece to the current common portion, so that collar is the common right-side collar, rather than an incident bounded pocket. The physical clearance just noted gives the corresponding untouched collar when the joint domain is compared with either individual stopped domain.

The nested marked Gaussian boundary comparison identifies the union germ on this collar with the germ of each individual path. Crucially, both transitions are the hydrodynamically normalized maps removing the other retained hull from the respective own chart. On their common connected untouched real base intervals, Aux. lemma 57 gives the analytic-logarithm lift exactly zero, normalized at infinity. Thus the two heights in the same inherited joint chart are, without an unspecified integer winding,

$$ \lambda - \theta_1\chi \quad\text{and}\quad \lambda - \theta_2\chi. $$

They are the boundary value of the same harmonic mean, so they are equal. Since $\chi > 0$, this contradicts $\theta_1 \neq \theta_2$.

All marked-kernel and boundary-decay identities are intersected over the countable family of rational own stopping pairs before the compact common arc is selected. Proper completions are used only for pathwise collar clearance; neither a chosen completion nor a future order event is added to the conditioning mark. $\square$

**Lemma W1** (incoming rational cuts for a backwards contact). Let $\alpha : [0, A] \to \overline{\mathbb{H}}$ and $\beta : [0, t] \to \overline{\mathbb{H}}$ be continuous injective Loewner prefixes, with their available finite endpoints included. Suppose $\mathcal{E}(\alpha([0, A]), \beta([0, v]))$ holds for every $v < t$. Suppose

$$ p = \alpha(a) = \beta(t), \qquad q_0 = \alpha(b_0) = \beta(v_0), \qquad 0 < a < b_0 \le A, \quad v_0 < t. $$

Assume that there is no common open subarc before $t$, and that $p$ is not an original seed. There are rational own parameters $r < a$ and $s < t$, with interior, distinct current tips, and an earlier contact $q = \alpha(b) = \beta(v)$, such that

$$ r < a < b, \qquad s < v < t, $$

and the following properties hold.

1. The old pair $\alpha([0, r]), \beta([0, s])$ has ordered proper completions. Each current tip is outside the other individual filled hull; hence both are active in their joint unbounded component.

2. The fresh portions $\alpha([r, b])$ and $\beta([s, t])$ avoid both old prefixes after their respective seeds.

<!-- page 530 -->

3. Their first contact in the first traversal order is $p$, whereas their first contact in the second traversal order is $q$. Equivalently, they contain the two contacts $p, q$, and
$$ \alpha([r, a]) \cap \beta([s, v]) = \varnothing. \tag{W1.1} $$

The same conclusion holds with the paths and left/right orientations exchanged.

<a id="id-W1-proof"></a>
*Proof.* All contacts with second parameter strictly below $t$ have increasing coordinates, by the proper-completion boundary-order theorem. The new point $p$ does not belong to an earlier second prefix, by injectivity of $\beta$. Moreover its first parameter $a$ is separated from the earlier contact set. Indeed, earlier contacts with first parameters tending to $a$ would have second parameters tending to $t$, by continuity and the inverse continuity of the compact simple second arc. Eventually those second parameters would exceed $v_0$, so increasing contact order would force their first parameters to be at least $b_0 > a$, a contradiction.

Let $(a_-, v_-)$ be the last earlier contact with first parameter below $a$, if one exists, and let $(b, v)$ be the first earlier contact with first parameter above $a$. The latter exists since $(b_0, v_0)$ does. These extrema are attained. For the lower one all relevant second parameters are at most $v_0 < t$, so compactness applies. For the upper one, a purported minimizing sequence with second parameter tending to $t$ would have physical limit $p$ and first parameter tending to $a$, contrary to the separation just proved. Increasing order now gives a matched contact-free gap

$$ (a_-, b) \quad\text{and}\quad (v_-, v), $$

with $a$ strictly inside the first interval. If the lower contact is absent, use the initial seeds as the lower ends of these intervals.

Choose $r \in (a_-, a)$ and $s \in (v_-, v)$ rational at interior trace points. Such choices exist because a nontrivial real subarc would have zero capacity. There are no earlier contacts inside either matched gap. The only contacts of $\alpha([r, b])$ with $\beta([s, t])$ are therefore $p$ and $q$. Their order is as displayed, which proves (W1.1). The same increasing contact relation shows that the first fresh portion does not hit the old second prefix, and the second fresh portion does not hit the old first prefix. Each avoids its own old prefix by injectivity.

The point $p$ is a future unvisited point of $\beta$ after $s$, and $q$ is a future unvisited point of $\alpha$ after $r$. Such a point is outside the older individual filled hull. For an interior point this is the defining target-component property together with old-range avoidance. For a real point, an untraced point in the old filled real base has an upper half-disc inside that old hull and cannot be a new continuous Loewner endpoint; the extreme old real endpoints lie on the old trace and are excluded by injectivity. Thus an available new real endpoint is on an untouched real ray, with its surviving incident upper collar.

The connected fresh arc from $\alpha(r)$ to $p$ avoids the second old range. It cannot pass from the interior of the second filled hull to its exterior without hitting that range; a real point outside the range has only one incident upper component. Since its terminal point is outside that hull, $\alpha(r)$ is outside as well. Apply the same argument to the second fresh arc from $\beta(s)$ to $q$. The old pair has ordered proper completions by restriction of a pair with second parameter strictly between $s$ and $t$. Aux. lemma 90 therefore gives joint activity of both interior tips. The outer-boundary orientation of Aux. lemma 85, or its disjoint-prefix version, puts their prime ends in the order $w_\beta < w_\alpha$. $\square$

Suppose its two gap arcs bound a Jordan region $V$ which is filled at that contact. <a id="id-W3-proof"></a>
*Proof.* Choose rational interior cuts inside the very gap which bounds $V$. The two fresh arcs through $q$ are disjoint before $q$ and avoid both old retained prefixes. The unvisited endpoint $q$ gives the two opposite-hull exclusions exactly as in W1, including its surviving incident collar if $q$ is real. **Lemma W4** (extension without a new mutual contact). Suppose $\mathcal{E}(\alpha([0, a]), \beta([0, b]))$ holds. Extend one or both original simple Loewner prefixes, within their available lifetimes, without adding any new mutual contact. At the last old common point, every suffix which was a singleton must either remain a singleton or have a positive initial portion certified to lie in the old joint unbounded component, with its prescribed relative cyclic order. Then the enlarged pair still satisfies $\mathcal{E}$. When both old suffixes were already nontrivial, their initial order is fixed and no extra certification is needed.

<a id="id-W4-proof"></a>
*Proof.* If the original prefixes have no common point, neither do the enlarged prefixes. The distinct-seed disjoint-arc completion theorem G13a applies. For a common initial seed use G13, with the unchanged initial branch order.

Otherwise the original contact relation is increasing and has a last common point $q$. It remains the last common point after extension. The original proper ordered completions have increasing contact order, so their unused tails after $q$ avoid both prefixes through $q$. Properness puts those tails in the unbounded component $D_q$ of that common-prefix union. In particular $q$ is active, and the original finite suffixes lie in $\overline{D_q}$, with the correct initial relative order.

Each extended suffix still avoids the common-prefix union, by own injectivity and the assumption of no new mutual contact. Connectedness keeps it in $\overline{D_q}$, using the additionally certified initial portion for a newly nontrivial suffix: an original-real point outside the old union has only one incident upper component, so it cannot conceal a passage between components. The fixed-hull restriction argument gives its continuous simple relative Loewner trace, including original-real contacts by reflection. The two relative suffixes have no common point after their seed. An already nontrivial suffix retains its initial branch; any newly nontrivial suffix has the prescribed order by the additional hypothesis. G13 supplies ordered proper extensions of them in the relative half-plane. If one suffix is a singleton, extend it along any proper completion of the other suffix; the resulting shared tail has either required closed-side order. If both are singletons, use a common vertical ray.

If a geometric extension has unwanted additional real contacts, apply the vertical interiorizing homeomorphism of G10, fixing both finite relative suffixes and their seed. Every real point fixed for this purpose is either the seed or a genuine untouched-original-real contact of a retained suffix. All other extension points on the real line are pushed into the open half-plane. The modified extensions therefore pull back without hitting the old common-prefix union after $q$. Glue them there using the ordered boundary orientation supplied by the original completions. They are the required proper completions of the enlarged original pair. $\square$

**Lemma W2a** (a reversed frontier pair is also excluded). Suppose that, for continuous increasing clocks $A(u), B(u)$, every pair of prefixes at $u < \tau$ has ordered proper completions. Consider contacts in the rectangle $[0, A(\tau)] \times [0, B(\tau)]$. The new contacts on its top or right edge cannot reverse the increasing order of the contacts in its strict interior, nor can the two new edge contacts be distinct and reverse one another.

<a id="id-W2a-proof"></a>
*Proof.* The strict-interior relation is increasing. A new edge contact backwards relative to an earlier strict-interior contact is treated by W1–W2, using only that earlier relation to choose the cuts. Both cuts are strictly below the frontier, so their ordered completions are available. The two fresh histories contain the two new contacts but have no contact in the rectangle below $(a, b)$. The activity and retained-law proof of W2 applies unchanged, and Lemma 7.7 contradicts this empty lower rectangle. No contact on the frontier has been used as an own stopping rule. $\square$

**Lemma W6** (contact order through finite terminal endpoints). Let two maximal canonical finite-word flows from one real seed have distinct angles and admissible initial seed groups. Their finite common points occur in increasing parameter order, including a common point at a finite continuation threshold of either path.

<a id="id-W6-proof"></a>
*Proof.* Label the lower-angle curve $\alpha$ and the higher-angle curve $\beta$. Every pair of strict prefixes has ordered proper completions by W5. The contact-order theorem G5 for these completions therefore orders every contact whose two parameters are strictly below their lifetimes.

Suppose a reversed pair remained after adjoining finite endpoints. First consider a contact $p = (a, T_\beta)$ at the second path's threshold which reverses a strict contact $q_0 = (b, v)$, so $a < b < T_\alpha$ and $v < T_\beta$. Truncate the first path at the *strict* parameter $b$. W5 then gives $\mathcal{E}(\alpha([0, b]), \beta([0, t]))$ for every $t < T_\beta$. Thus W1 applies with its full displayed hypothesis, without ever asserting completion of the first path at its threshold. The earlier contact set has a matched gap around $a$, its rational cuts are strict, and W2 excludes the empty lower rectangle. Reflect this argument for a contact at $T_\alpha$ reversing a strict earlier contact.

The only remaining possibility is a reversed pair

$$ p = (a, T_\beta), \qquad q = (T_\alpha, b), \qquad a < T_\alpha, \quad b < T_\beta, $$

**Lemma DS1** (uniform inverse-gap occupation). Fix $\kappa > 0$, a finite number of force labels, a time bound $T$, and a uniform bound on the absolute force weights. On each side order the nonnegative driver

$$ dX_1 = -\sqrt{\kappa}\, dB + b_X\, dt. $$

$$ \frac{2}{X_1} + \sum_{i=1}^{m_0} \frac{\rho_i}{X_i} = \sum_{i<m_0} (S_i + 2)\left(\frac{1}{X_i} - \frac{1}{X_{i+1}}\right) + \frac{S_{m_0} + 2}{X_{m_0}} \ge \frac{\epsilon}{X_1}. \tag{DS1.4} $$

**Lemma CP0** (a finite threshold for the three-negative-weight word). Let $0 < \kappa < 4$, $x < 0$, and consider the integrated process with left weights $r_1$ at $0^-$ and $r_2$ at $x$, and right weight $r_R$ at $0^+$. Suppose

$$ -2 < r_1 < 0, \qquad r_2 < 0, \qquad -2 < r_R < 0, \qquad S = r_1 + r_2 \le -2. \tag{CP0.1} $$

Then its continuation threshold is finite almost surely. If also $S > d_\kappa = \kappa/2 - 4$, its continuous terminal trace lies strictly to the left of $x$.

<a id="id-CP0-proof"></a>
*Proof.* Up to the threshold put

$$ X = W - V^{1,L}, \quad Y = W - V^{2,L}, \quad Z = V^{1,R} - W, \quad D = Y + Z, \quad U = Y/D, \quad du = dt/D^2. $$

Here $0 \le X \le Y$, $Y > 0$, and $D > 0$ from time zero. An admissible nearest-seed collision uses the integrated convention and has zero Lebesgue measure. Direct differentiation gives

$$ dU = \sqrt{\kappa}\, dB_u - \left\{ \frac{-S - 2}{U} + \frac{r_R + 2}{1 - U} + (-r_1)\left(\frac{D}{X} - \frac{1}{U}\right) \right\} du, \qquad \frac{d \log D}{du} = \frac{2}{U(1 - U)}. \tag{CP0.2} $$

Every term in braces is nonnegative. The expressions involving a zero nearest gap are interpreted through their integrable singular integrals. The initial value $U = 1$ is the admissible right-seed entrance.

Write the nonnegative drift integral in the first equation as $A_u$. If the total angular lifetime were infinite, then $U_u \le U_0 + \sqrt{\kappa} B_u - (r_R + 2)u$ would contradict $U_u \ge 0$ and $B_u/u \to 0$. Its angular lifetime $u_*$ is therefore finite. On every event $u_* < L$, the identity $A_u = U_0 + \sqrt{\kappa} B_u - U_u$ bounds this increasing process by a finite Brownian supremum. It has a finite limit. In particular

$$ \int_0^{u_*} \frac{du}{1 - U_u} < \infty. \tag{CP0.3} $$

The Brownian motion and hence $U$ have continuous terminal limits.

One must also control $1/U$ when $S = -2$, since its coefficient in (CP0.2) then vanishes. Fix $r < 1/8$. Choose bounded, nonnegative, smooth increasing concave functions $f_\nu$ on $[0, 1]$ whose derivatives vanish above $2r$, and for $0 \le v \le r$ satisfy

$$ f'_\nu(v) = -\log(v + \nu) - 1, \qquad f''_\nu(v) = -(v + \nu)^{-1}, \qquad 0 < \nu < r. $$

A smooth decreasing interpolation of the positive first derivative on $[r, 2r]$ gives such functions with uniformly bounded values. The nonpositive drift of $U$ and concavity make the entire Itô drift of $f_\nu(U)$ nonpositive, and on $U < r$ it is at most $-\kappa/(2(U + \nu))$. Apply Itô's formula at bounded angular stops and then exhaust the lifetime. For each fixed $\nu$ the martingale has zero expectation after the usual localization; boundedness and nonnegativity of $f_\nu$ give

$$ \mathbb{E}\int_0^{u_*} \frac{\mathbf{1}_{\{U_u < r\}}}{U_u + \nu}\, du \le C. $$

Monotone convergence proves integrability of $1/U$ near zero. Away from zero it follows from $u_* < \infty$. Together with (CP0.3), this shows that $\log D$ has a finite terminal limit. Consequently

$$ T_* = \int_0^{u_*} D_u^2\, du < \infty. $$
