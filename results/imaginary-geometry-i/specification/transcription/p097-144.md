**Aux. lemma 19** (History marking before a predictable lifetime)**.** *The history-marking and conditional-mean conclusions of **Aux.** lemma 18 remain valid before a lifetime $T_* > 0$ if the following localized hypotheses hold. The hulls have capacity $2t$ for $t < T_*$, their histories are recorded in the usual filtration, and $K_S$ is local at every stopping time $S < T_*$ almost surely. There are increasing finite stopping times $\sigma_n < T_*$ with $\sigma_n \uparrow T_*$, and the hulls are right-continuous in the sense $\bigcap_{s>t,\, s<T_*} K_s = K_t$ for $t < T_*$. Then the conclusion holds at every almost surely finite stopping time $\tau < T_*$.*

<a id="aux-19-proof"></a>
*Proof.* We first establish a marked assertion at a deterministic time $t$, on the event that the evolution is still alive. Fix $F \in \mathcal{F}_t$. For each $n$ put $S_0 = t \wedge \sigma_n$ and define

$$
S_1 = \begin{cases} \sigma_n, & \sigma_n \le t, \\ t, & \sigma_n > t \text{ and } F, \\ (t+1) \wedge \sigma_{n+1}, & \sigma_n > t \text{ and } F^{c}. \end{cases}
$$

These are stopping times strictly before $T_*$, bounded by $t + 1$. For $u < t$, the sublevel event for $S_1$ is $\{\sigma_n \le u\}$; for $t \le u < t + 1$, the two remaining branches are decided by $\mathcal{F}_t$ and by $\{\sigma_{n+1} \le u\}$. On the last branch $S_1 > t$, because $\sigma_{n+1} \ge \sigma_n > t$.

Fix a deterministic open set $U$. The local-set avoidance criterion, with the entire set label retained, says that each of

$$
\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbf{1}_{\{K_{S_j} \cap U = \varnothing\}} \mathbf{1}_{\{\operatorname{hcap}(K_{S_j}) \le 2t\}} \Bigm| h\Bigr], \qquad j = 0, 1,

is measurable with respect to $Y_U = h - P_U h$. Subtract the second from the first. On $\{\sigma_n \le t\}$ the hulls coincide; on $\{\sigma_n > t\} \cap F$ they again coincide; and on the remaining branch the second capacity indicator is zero. The difference is therefore exactly

$$
\mathbb{P}[F^{c},\ \sigma_n > t,\ K_t \cap U = \varnothing \mid h]. \tag{H1}
$$

<a id="id-H4"></a>
$$
\mathbf{1}_{\{K_\tau \cap U = \varnothing\}} = \lim_{m\to\infty} \lim_{k\to\infty} \mathbf{1}_{\{q_k < T_*,\ K_{q_k} \cap U_m = \varnothing\}}. \tag{H4}

Both limits are monotone in their respective variables, on the probability-one event under discussion.

$$
\mathbb{P}[H,\ K_\tau \cap U = \varnothing \mid h]

is $Y_V$-measurable for every such $V$. Exhausting $U$ by these sets gives measurability with respect to $Y_U$. **Aux. lemma 20** (Lifting a path through its first boundary hit)**.** *Let $D$ be a proper simply connected domain with locally connected boundary in the Riemann sphere. Let $f : \mathbb{D} \to D$ be conformal. Suppose $\alpha : [0, T] \to \overline{D}$ is continuous in the spherical metric and $\alpha([0, T)) \subset D$. Then $f^{-1} \circ \alpha$ extends continuously to $T$ as a path in $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$.*

<a id="aux-20-proof"></a>
*Proof.* The locally connected boundary theorem gives a continuous extension of $f$ to $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$ in the spherical metric. Let $p = \alpha(T)$. If $p \in D$, the assertion follows from continuity of $f^{-1}$ in $D$. Otherwise the cluster set of $f^{-1}(\alpha(t))$ as $t \uparrow T$ is a nonempty compact connected subset of $\partial\mathbb{D}$: it is the intersection of the nested compact connected closures of the terminal subpaths. Continuity of $f$ puts this cluster set inside $f^{-1}(\{p\})$.

No fiber of this boundary map contains a nontrivial circular arc. Indeed, if $f$ were constant and finite on such an arc, the Schwarz reflection principle, after straightening a smaller boundary arc, would extend $f - p$ holomorphically across that arc. The identity theorem would make $f$ constant. If $p = \infty$, apply the same argument to $1/(f - q)$ with a finite $q \notin D$. Thus a connected subset of the fiber contains at most one point. The cluster set is a singleton, proving the assertion. $\square$

## 4 Dubédat’s argument

This section proves nonboundary field determination and duality using the argument of Dubédat [Dub09a, Dub09b]. The independent endpoint input for the nonboundary flow lines is Aux. lemma 7.

When following the illustrations, it will be useful to keep in mind a few definitions and identities:

$$
\lambda := \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\kappa}}, \quad \kappa' := \frac{16}{\kappa}, \quad \lambda' := \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\kappa'}} = \frac{\pi\sqrt{\kappa}}{4} = \frac{\kappa}{4}\lambda < \lambda, \quad \chi := \frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}} - \frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{2} \tag{4.1}
$$

$$
2\pi\chi = 4(\lambda - \lambda'), \quad \lambda' = \lambda - \frac{\pi}{2}\chi \tag{4.2}
$$

$$
2\pi\chi = (4 - \kappa)\lambda = (\kappa' - 4)\lambda'. \tag{4.3}
$$

Throughout, we shall assume that $\kappa \in (0, 4)$ so that $\kappa' \in (4, \infty)$. Each of $\lambda$, $\lambda'$, and $\chi$ is determined by $\kappa$ through (4.1) and is positive. We will frequently go back and forth between these four values using the identities above. To interpret (4.2), recall that an angle change of $\theta$ corresponds to a change of $\theta\chi$ in the value of the field. Thus (4.2) says that the difference $\lambda - \lambda'$ corresponds to “a ninety-degree turn to the left” in the imaginary geometry, which will frequently be useful. (This fact was already illustrated in Figure 1.9.) Recall also from Theorem 1.1 that a force point of weight $\rho$ corresponds to a jump of size $\rho\lambda$ in the boundary values. Thus (4.3) implies that a gap of size $2\pi\chi$ (a “full revolution” gap) corresponds to a $\rho$ value of $(4 - \kappa)$. More generally, a $\theta\chi$ gap in boundary values corresponds to $\rho = \frac{\theta}{2\pi}(4 - \kappa)$.

**Aux. lemma 21** (Finitely many strictly critical upper heights)**.** *Let $k > 0$, $\Lambda = \pi/\sqrt{k}$, and consider a strip force-point evolution in $S = \{0 < \operatorname{Im} z < \pi\}$, started from zero. $$
-\Lambda < a_j < \Lambda \qquad (0 \le j \le m). \tag{UC1}

*The endpoint weights are the ones prescribed by these boundary data and conformal covariance. Then the trace has an actual limit at a finite point of the upper boundary. The conclusion concerns its first upper contact; no continuation through that contact is asserted.*

<a id="aux-21-proof"></a>
*Proof.* Write $s_R, s_L$ for the sums of the lower weights, and $\tau_+, \tau_-$ for the two endpoint weights. The upper force at $p_j + i\pi$ has weight

$$
\omega_j = \frac{a_{j-1} - a_j}{\Lambda}.
$$

The total endpoint charges are

<a id="id-UC2"></a>
$$
q_+ := \tau_+ + s_R = k/2 - 3 + a_m/\Lambda, \qquad q_- := \tau_- + s_L = k/2 - 3 - a_0/\Lambda. \tag{UC2}

These formulas follow from the boundary transformation under $1 - e^{-z}$, as in (HB1); in particular $q_+ + q_- + \sum_j \omega_j = k - 6$. Let $\xi$ be the driver and let $P_j + i\pi$ be the image of the $j$th upper mark. Put $X_j = P_j - \xi$. The upper-coordinate equation is $dP_j = \tanh(X_j/2)\,dt$. Upper marks remain distinct: their differences have positive derivative when positive, because $\tanh$ is increasing.

*The bounded upper contribution.* After replacing each lower coth term by its constant limiting sign, the remaining driver drift is

<a id="id-UC3"></a>
$$
b_t = \frac{q_- - q_+}{2} - \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m} \omega_j \tanh(X_j/2). \tag{UC3}
$$

Set $w_j = (1 - \tanh(X_j/2))/2$. The ordering gives $1 > w_1 > \cdots > w_m > 0$. Substitution in (UC3) gives

<a id="id-UC4"></a>
$$
b_t = -\frac{1}{\Lambda}\left[a_0(1 - w_1) + \sum_{j=1}^{m-1} a_j(w_j - w_{j+1}) + a_m w_m\right]. \tag{UC4}
$$

For $m = 0$ this means simply $b_t = -a_0/\Lambda$. The coefficients in brackets are nonnegative and sum to one. Hence, with $\mu = \max_j |a_j|/\Lambda < 1$,

$$

$$

No monotonicity of the heights is required.

The lower force groups do not collide at a positive time, and the strip evolution exists at every finite strip time. Here are the needed finite-time inputs. For separated lower groups, the noncollision result [Zha10, Lemma 4.2] applies. Adding the finitely many upper marks introduces a bounded driver drift, since $|\tanh| \le 1$, and their passive coordinate equations have bounded Lipschitz coefficients. On every finite time interval, bounded-drift Girsanov therefore transfers that noncollision assertion and finite-time continuity. When deleting the upper marks one can add half their total weight to each endpoint weight; this preserves the endpoint drift and restores the sum $k - 6$. If lower marks start at the seed, use their local entrance construction and noncollision comparison from Section 2, then apply the separated argument at a positive time. Countably many positive-time localizations cover that entrance. The initial continuity

*Linear separation of the lower groups.* We prove that every remaining lower gap grows at least linearly. It suffices to work after a fixed positive time, translating the strip and resetting the clock so that any lower marks are separated from the driver at time zero. When both sides are present, let $r, \ell$ be the nearest right and left coordinates, and put $X = r - \xi > 0$, $Y = \xi - \ell > 0$. $$

If the right list is nonempty, $s_R \ge k/2 - 2 > -2$, so $1 + s_R/2 > 0$. Whenever $X_t \le t$, one has $Y_t \ge t$. Almost surely there is a finite $C$ with $|\sqrt{k}B_t| \le C + \epsilon t$ for all $t$. If $X_u \le u$ throughout a sufficiently late interval $[s, t]$, integration of (UC7) gives

where $K = 2C + \operatorname{TV}(A)$ is finite. An interval starting at $X_s = s$ and ending at $X_t = \alpha t$, with $X_u \le u$ in between, would consequently require

$$

$$

For large $s$ this is impossible. If $X$ eventually stays below the line $t$, (UC8) from one fixed starting time gives $\liminf X_t/t \ge d - \epsilon$. Otherwise use arbitrarily late crossings of that line and the preceding impossibility of a subsequent crossing below $\alpha t$. In every case $\liminf X_t/t \ge \alpha > 0$. $X_t \le t$. Every farther gap is at least the nearest gap, so the assertion holds for all lower marks.

The difference between the true driver drift and $b_t$ is therefore exponentially small at large times: in the original force equation each lower correction is a fixed weight times $\coth(x/2) - \operatorname{sgn}(x)$. *Removing the lower groups on the whole tail.* Start at an integer $n > 0$ after the entrance. Stop when the accumulated squared drift correction, divided by $k$, reaches an integer $M$. Consequently a probability-one assertion for the full reference system holds on its no-cutoff event; no conditional reference law given that event is being asserted. *Every upper mark eventually lies on a fixed side.* For the upper-only system, (UC5) still holds and

**Aux. lemma 23** (Original-hull uniqueness when the finite hull is thin)**.** *Fix $0 < \kappa < 4$ and finite force data. Consider couplings with the original optional hull specification of Theorem 1.1, with their own stopped driving histories retained as local marks. Fix a bounded own-filtration stopping rule $S < T_*$ whose common driving law has a continuous simple trace satisfying*

$$
\eta((0, S]) \subset \mathbb{H}.
$$

*Assume the strict interior dimension bound of **Aux.** lemma 15 for this trace. Then every such coupling has the same joint law of the entire field and the driving history through $S$.*

*Consequently, suppose these stops exhaust a non-boundary-intersecting interval, and suppose one reference coupling on that interval has already been constructed in which the full driving history is a measurable function of the field. Every coupling with the original optional hull specification has the same joint law and the same field-determining function on that interval. A continuous terminal history is included by its measurable limit. The existence and field determination of that reference are inputs to this implication.*

<a id="aux-23-proof"></a>
*Proof.* A finite simple slit whose only real point is its seed does not disconnect an open pocket from infinity. Thus its filled hull and its trace range agree in $\mathbb{H}$:

$$
K_S \cap \mathbb{H} = \eta([0, S]) \cap \mathbb{H}.
$$

The assertion also follows by mapping the complement of the simple boundary-to-interior arc to a half-plane; there is no second boundary endpoint or closed loop to bound a pocket. This equality is the specific geometric hypothesis used below.

Let $d < 2$ be the upper dimension bound supplied by that lemma, and choose $\varepsilon > 0$ so that $d < 2 - 2\varepsilon$. The bound and a countable compact exhaustion give, almost surely,

$$
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(K_S \cap L) < 2 - 2\varepsilon \quad \text{for every compact } L \subset \mathbb{H}.
$$

<!-- page 123 -->
The marginal GFF is locally square-integrable with values in $H^{-\varepsilon}$ by **Aux.** lemma 11. Its regular conditional law given a stopped history is therefore supported on $H^{-\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ for almost every history.

We explain precisely why the restriction off this hull determines a full distribution law. Fix one history for which the preceding statements hold, and write $K$ for its now deterministic hull. If $u, v \in H^{-\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{H})$ have equal restrictions to $\mathbb{H} \setminus K$, their difference is supported on $K \cap \mathbb{H}$. **Aux.** lemma 12, applied after compact localization, gives $u - v = 0$. The restriction map is thus injective on $H^{-\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{H})$.

This is also a measurable uniqueness statement. Record distributions by a countable determining family of smooth test pairings and use the local Sobolev norm to specify the Borel subset $H^{-\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Restriction to the fixed open set $\mathbb{H} \setminus K$ is a Borel injection between standard Borel spaces. Its inverse on its image is Borel by the measurable-inverse theorem for such injections. Consequently two probability measures supported on $H^{-\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ with the same restricted distribution law have the same full distribution law. One can choose the exceptional histories using a countable family of rational balls and test functions. No jointly chosen inverse for every possible hull is required for this pointwise identification of conditional kernels.

The stopped driving marginal is the same in the two couplings by uniqueness of the integrated SDE and the common stopping rule. Conditional on a stopped history, the original specification gives the same Gaussian law on $\mathbb{H} \setminus K_S$: its mean is the prescribed function of that history and its covariance is the same Dirichlet Green kernel. The measurable uniqueness just proved identifies the entire conditional field laws. Integrating over the common history law proves equality of the full stopped joint laws.

Apply this conclusion along a countable increasing exhaustion of the interval, retaining the earlier stopped histories in each later record. Equality of all these finite joint laws gives equality of the field and full-history laws on the countable sequence space. The continuous driving process, and any continuous finite endpoint, are measurable limits of these records. Hence the same full joint law is obtained in every coupling. If the reference law is concentrated on $X = \Phi(h)$, equality of joint laws gives this identity in each other coupling as well. $\square$

*Scope of this non-boundary implication.* This argument transfers an already constructed field-determined *simple non-boundary flow-line* coupling to the original optional hull class. It does not construct the reference or reprove its field determination. It does not apply to nonsimple counterflows. If a flow line hits the original boundary and disconnects an open pocket, its filled hull is no longer its thin range. The original outside-hull kernel then omits an open set, and the support-removal step above alone is unavailable. The broader original-coupling upgrade at paragraph 7.1 therefore uses a separate pocket-birth and freezing argument before applying support removal. The present lemma itself remains limited to finite hulls that equal their thin ranges.

The ambient strip counterflow in this section is explicitly $\eta' = \operatorname{CF}_{\mathrm{d}}(h)$. **Aux.** lemma 1 gives its actual initial force word. has a positive lower bound for $x \ge 0$ and $\rho \in J$; here $\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{x,\rho}$ is the one-force-point law with weight $\rho$ and initial position $x$. $$
V_t^{x,\rho} = x + \frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\int_0^t \frac{ds}{R_s^{x,\rho}}, \qquad W_t^{x,\rho} = V_t^{x,\rho} - \sqrt{\kappa}R_t^{x,\rho}.
$$

The Brownian motion in the Bessel equation has the opposite sign to the Brownian motion driving $W$. These formulas give the one-force-point SLE, including $x = 0$, since the reciprocal integral is finite for Bessel dimension greater than one.

We record continuous dependence on both parameters. Squared Bessel processes are ordered when both their initial values and dimensions are ordered. If $Y^+ \ge Y^-$ are such a pair, driven by the same Brownian motion, their difference is a nonnegative submartingale and

$$
\mathbb{E}[Y_T^+ - Y_T^-] = Y_0^+ - Y_0^- + (\delta_+ - \delta_-)T.
$$

The maximal inequality for nonnegative submartingales, followed by a coordinatewise monotone sandwich, proves convergence in probability uniformly on $[0, 1]$ when $(x, \rho)$ varies continuously. The same holds for their square roots. All these Bessel processes are bounded below by the process with initial value zero and dimension $\delta_* := \min_{\rho\in J} \delta(\rho) > 1$. Its reciprocal is integrable on $[0, 1]$. Along any almost surely uniformly convergent subsequence, dominated convergence therefore gives convergence of the reciprocal integrals uniformly in their upper limits. Consequently $(x, \rho) \mapsto \mathcal{L}(W^{x,\rho})$ is weakly continuous in $C([0, 1])$. Since the event $\{\sup |W| < 1\}$ is open, $p$ is lower semicontinuous.

For every fixed $(x, \rho)$, $p(x, \rho) > 0$. Continuity at time zero gives a deterministic $s \in (0, 1)$ such that $\{\sup_{t\le s} |W_t| < 1/4\}$ has positive probability. At this deterministic time the force-point gap is positive almost surely. Conditional on the state at $s$ on that event, choose an interval centered at $W_s$, of radius less than both $1/4$ and $(V_s - W_s)/2$. Until the driver leaves this interval its distance from the nondecreasing force point is bounded below by a positive constant. Bounded-drift Girsanov comparison with Brownian motion gives positive conditional probability of staying in that interval until time one. Integrating proves $p(x, \rho) > 0$. A positive lower semicontinuous function on the compact set $[0, 2] \times J$ has a positive minimum. This proves the required uniform bound and, together with the preceding Cauchy–Schwarz estimate, the lemma. In particular, its constant may be chosen uniformly for $\rho^{1,R}$ in any prescribed compact subset of $(-2, \infty)$. $\square$
