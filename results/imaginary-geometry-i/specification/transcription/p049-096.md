### 3.2 Local sets

The theory of local sets, developed in [SS13], extends the Markovian structure of the field (Proposition 3.1) to the setting of conditioning on the values it takes on a *random set* $A \subseteq D$. In this section, we will give an overview of local sets which is very closely based on the treatment given in [SS13, Section 3.3]. We will cite some of the results in [SS13] in the proofs of the statements below.

Throughout, we shall assume for convenience that $D \subseteq \mathbf{C}$ is a bounded domain and $h$ is a GFF on $D$. Let $\Gamma$ be the collection of all nonempty, closed subsets of $\overline{D}$ which contain $\partial D$. We view $\Gamma$ as a metric space endowed with the **Hausdorff** distance. That is, the distance between sets $S_1, S_2 \in \Gamma$ is

$$ d_{\mathrm{HAUS}}(S_1, S_2) := \max\Big\{ \sup_{x\in S_1} \operatorname{dist}(x, S_2), \sup_{y\in S_2} \operatorname{dist}(y, S_1) \Big\}. $$

It is well known (and the reader may easily verify) that $\Gamma$ is a compact metric space. (In order to treat the case that $D$ is unbounded, we can conformally map to the sphere and use the induced Euclidean metric on the sphere.) Let $\mathcal{G}$ be the Borel $\sigma$-algebra on $\Gamma$ induced by this metric.

Suppose that $(A, h)$ is a coupling of a GFF $h$ on $D$ and a random variable $A$ taking values in $\Gamma$. Then $A$ is said to be a **local set** of $h$ if there exists a law on pairs $(A, h_1)$ where $h_1$ is a distribution on $D$ whose restriction to $D \setminus A$ is harmonic, such that a sample with the law $(A, h)$ can be produced by

1. choosing the pair $(h_1, A)$,
2. then sampling an instance $h_2$ of the zero boundary GFF on $D \setminus A$ and setting $h = h_1 + h_2$.

Whenever we use the phrase "$A$ is a local set of $h$," we will always assume that $\partial D \subseteq A$ even though we may not say this explicitly.

<!-- page 50 -->
Deterministic closed sets are local. By Theorem 1.1, the filled hull $K_\tau$ of an $\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa(\underline{\rho})$ process is local for every stopping time strictly before its continuation threshold. These are motivating examples for the theory. Endpoint assertions and assertions about the range of a nonsimple trace require the corresponding additional arguments.

Given $A \in \Gamma$, let $A_\delta$ denote the closed set containing all points in $D$ whose distance from $A$ is at most $\delta$. Let $\mathcal{A}_\delta$ be the smallest $\sigma$-algebra in which $A$ and the restriction of $h$ (as a distribution) to the interior of $A_\delta$ are measurable. Let $\mathcal{A} = \bigcap_{\delta>0} \mathcal{A}_\delta$. Intuitively, this is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra in which $A$ and the values of $h$ in an infinitesimal neighborhood of $A$ are measurable. Suppose that $A$ is local for $h$. We let $\mathcal{C}_A$ be the conditional expectation of $h$ given $\mathcal{A}$. We note that we can view $\mathcal{C}_A$ as a random variable which takes values in the space of distributions and we can identify $\mathcal{C}_A$ with the harmonic function $h_1$, and this is the perspective that we will take. Indeed, as we will explain carefully just below, conditionally on $(A, h_1)$, the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the values of $h_2$ in $A_\delta$ becomes trivial as $\delta \to 0$, i.e., $\mathcal{A} = \sigma(A, h_1)$. Hence, we can write $(\mathcal{C}_A, \phi) = \mathbf{E}[(h, \phi) \,|\, \mathcal{A}] = \mathbf{E}[(h_1, \phi) \,|\, \mathcal{A}] = (h_1, \phi)$.

Let us justify the germ identification $\mathcal{A} = \sigma(A, h_1)$, with completed sigma-algebras. This also proves that the harmonic distribution in the two-step definition is canonical. Choose nested dyadic square covers $C_n$ of $A$ such that each $C_n$ has only finitely many possible values,

$$ A_{2^{-n}} \subseteq C_n \subseteq A_{(1+\sqrt{2})2^{-n}}, \qquad C_n \downarrow A, \qquad \Omega_n := D \setminus C_n \uparrow D \setminus A. $$

The covers include $\partial D$. For every deterministic possible value $C$ of a cover, let $Q_C h$ be the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(D \setminus C)$. Proposition 3.2 identifies its sigma-algebra with the germ of $h$ on $C$. There are only countably many such $C$, so all these identifications can be used on one probability-one event.

On $\{C_n = C\}$, the function $h_1$ is harmonic on $D \setminus C$. Its Dirichlet pairings with compactly supported smooth functions there vanish. Computing the projection onto $H(D \setminus C)$ in a basis of such functions consequently gives

$$ Q_{C_n} h = h_1 + R_n, \qquad R_n = h_2 - P_{\Omega_n} h_2. $$

Conditionally on $(A, h_1)$, $R_n$ is centered Gaussian with covariance $G_{D\setminus A} - G_{\Omega_n}$, extended by zero to $D$. Write $\sigma_O^2(\phi) = \iint \phi(z) G_O(z, w) \phi(w)\, dz\, dw$ for the zero-boundary GFF variance in an open set $O \subseteq D$. For every deterministic smooth test $\phi$,

$$ \mathbb{E}\big[ |(R_n, \phi)|^2 \mid A, h_1 \big] = \sigma^2_{D\setminus A}(\phi) - \sigma^2_{\Omega_n}(\phi) \longrightarrow 0. $$

Monotone Dirichlet-space approximation gives the convergence, and the variances are bounded by the deterministic $\sigma_D^2(\phi)$. Hence $(Q_{C_n} h, \phi) \to (h_1, \phi)$ in $L^2$. The random projection $Q_{C_n} h$ is measurable from $A$ and the field germ on $C_n$, and therefore from $A$ and the field in every sufficiently slightly larger neighborhood of $C_n$. The shrinking-cover

<!-- page 51 -->
bounds, an almost surely convergent subsequence, and a countable determining test family prove that $h_1$ is $\mathcal{A}$-measurable.

It remains to rule out additional germ information. Put

$$ \mathcal{B}_n = \sigma(A, h_1, Q_{C_n} h) = \sigma(A, h_1, R_n). $$

These sigma-algebras decrease. Conditionally on $(A, h_1)$, this is the decreasing family of Gaussian complement subspaces for $H(\Omega_n) \uparrow H(D \setminus A)$; its limiting complement is zero. More explicitly, Gaussian orthogonal decomposition gives

$$ \mathbb{E}\big[ e^{i(h_2,\phi)} \mid \mathcal{B}_n \big] = \exp\big( i(R_n, \phi) - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2_{\Omega_n}(\phi) \big) \longrightarrow \exp\big( -\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2_{D\setminus A}(\phi) \big) $$

in $L^1$. Reverse martingale convergence, applied also to rational linear combinations of a determining test family, shows that $\bigcap_n \mathcal{B}_n$ contains no information about $h_2$ beyond $(A, h_1)$. Since every $\mathcal{B}_n$ is contained in $\sigma(A, h_1, h_2)$, this proves $\bigcap_n \mathcal{B}_n = \sigma(A, h_1)$ modulo completion. Finally $\mathcal{A} \subseteq \mathcal{B}_n$ for every $n$, because $C_n$ contains a positive neighborhood of $A$ and $Q_{C_n} h$ determines the field germ there. Together with the measurability proved above, this gives $\mathcal{A} = \sigma(A, h_1)$.

We note that in the case that $A$ is a deterministic, closed set then Proposition 3.2 implies that $\mathcal{A}$ is the same as the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(D \setminus A)$ so that $\mathcal{C}_A$ is the same as the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(D \setminus A)$. The proof of Lemma 3.6 given just below implies that $\mathcal{A}$ is equal to the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $(h_1, A)$ as in the definition of a local set given above.

In many places in this article, we will consider conditional expectations where we condition on $A$ and $h|_A$ where $A$ is a local set for $h$. By this, we mean that we consider the conditional expectation $\mathcal{C}_A$ of $h$ given the $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{A}$ defined just above.

There are several other characterizations of local sets which are given in the following restatement of [SS13, Lemma 3.9].

**Lemma 3.6.** *Suppose that $(A, h)$ is a random variable which is a coupling of an instance $h$ of the GFF on $D$ with a random element $A$ of $\Gamma$. Then the following are equivalent:*

*(i) For each deterministic open $U \subseteq D$, we have that* given *the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(U)$, the event $A \cap U = \emptyset$ is independent of the projection of $h$ onto $H(U)$. In other words, the conditional probability that $A \cap U = \emptyset$ given $h$ is a measurable function of the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(U)$.*

*(ii) For each deterministic open $U \subseteq D$, we let $S$ be the event that $A$ intersects $U$ and let*

$$ \widetilde{A} = \begin{cases} A & \text{on } S^c, \\ \emptyset & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases} $$

*Then we have that* given *the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(U)$, the pair $(S, \widetilde{A})$ is independent of the projection of $h$ onto $H(U)$.*

*(iii) Conditioned on $\mathcal{A}$, (a regular version of) the conditional law of $h$ is that of $h_1 + h_2$ where $h_2$ is the GFF with zero boundary values on $D \setminus A$ (extended to all of $D$) and $h_1$ is an $\mathcal{A}$-measurable random distribution (i.e., as a distribution-valued function on the space of distribution-set pairs $(A, h)$, $h_1$ is $\mathcal{A}$-measurable) which is almost surely harmonic on $D \setminus A$.*

*(iv) $A$ is a local set for $h$. That is, a sample with the law of $(A, h)$ can be produced as follows. First choose the pair $(A, h_1)$ according to some law where $h_1$ is almost surely harmonic on $D \setminus A$. Then sample an instance $h_2$ of the GFF on $D \setminus A$ and set $h = h_1 + h_2$.*

Next, suppose $A$ satisfies (i). We will show that (iii) holds. For each $\delta > 0$, let $\mathcal{D}_\delta$ be the collection of sets which can be written as $\overline{D} \cap S$ where $S$ is a closed square in $\mathbf{C}$ of side length $\delta$ with corners in the grid $\delta\mathbf{Z}^2$ and let $\widehat{A}_\delta = \cup\{S \in \mathcal{D}_\delta : S \cap A_\delta \ne \emptyset\}$. Then $\widehat{A}_\delta$ intersects $U$ if and only if $A_\delta$ intersects the intersection of $D$ and the closure of $\widehat{U}_\delta$. Equivalently, $A_\delta$ intersects the intersection of $D$ and the closure of $\widehat{U}_\delta$ if and only if $A$ intersects $\cap_{\delta'>\delta} \widetilde{U}_{\delta,\delta'}$. This is in turn a measurable function of the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(U)$ (since $U \subseteq \widetilde{U}_{\delta,\delta'}$). Therefore the conditional probability given $h$ of the event $\{\widehat{A}_\delta \cap U \ne \emptyset\} = \cap_{\delta'>\delta}\{A \cap \widetilde{U}_{\delta,\delta'} \ne \emptyset\}$ is a measurable function of the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(U)$ (note that we can represent the intersection as a countable intersection so that the event in question is measurable). Note that there are only finitely many possible choices for $\widehat{A}_\delta$ since we assumed $D$ to be bounded.

Fix $C \in \mathcal{D}_\delta^\cup$ and assume that $\mathbf{P}[\widehat{A}_\delta \subseteq C] > 0$. Since $\widehat{A}_\delta$ satisfies (i), we have that the conditional law of $h$ in $D \setminus C$ given both $\widehat{A}_\delta \subseteq C$ and the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(D \setminus C)$ is the same as the conditional law of $h$ in $D \setminus C$ given just the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(D \setminus C)$. That $\widehat{A}_\delta$ satisfies (iii) thus follows from Proposition 3.1.

We now pass from the square approximations to $A$. Take $\delta_j = 2^{-j}$ and write $C_j = \widehat{A}_{\delta_j}$. These covers are decreasing, contain $A$, and are contained in the $(1+\sqrt{2})\delta_j$-neighborhood of $A$. For a deterministic possible coarse cover $C$, write $Q_C$ for the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(D \setminus C)$.

Here is the finite conditioning argument in detail. Fix finitely many cover scales, all at least as fine as the scale defining $C$, and specify their values, with the coarse value equal to $C$. Their joint pattern is a finite Boolean combination of conditions saying that $A$ meets a prescribed closed neighborhood of a grid square. Inclusion and exclusion expresses its indicator as a finite linear combination of avoidance indicators. In every such term, all the conditions excluding coarse squares outside $C$ are retained. Consequently each avoidance term says $A \subset F$ for a deterministic relatively open set $F \subset C$. A term is identically zero unless $\partial D \subset F$. In the other terms choose the closed inner exhaustion below to contain $\partial D$ (adjoin this compact set to each member). Since $A$ is compact, the event is the increasing union of the events $A \subset F_m$, where $F_m$ are closed subsets of $F$ increasing to $F$ and eventually containing every compact subset of $F$. Each event $A \subset F_m$ is avoidance of the open set $D \setminus F_m$. By (i), its conditional probability given $h$ is measurable with respect to the projection onto $H^\perp(D \setminus F_m)$, hence with respect to $Q_C$, because $F_m \subset C$. Conditional monotone convergence and the finite inclusion–exclusion identity show that the conditional probability of the entire prescribed pattern given $h$ is $Q_C$-measurable.

By the deterministic Markov property, the projection of $h$ onto $H(D \setminus C)$ is independent of $Q_C$. The preceding measurability therefore shows that conditioning additionally on any such finite pattern leaves this projection with its unchanged zero-boundary GFF law. This proves both the finite-cover assertion used above and its version with any finite collection of finer covers recorded. In particular, with

$$ \mathcal{G}_k = \sigma\big( \widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\delta_j}, C_j, C_{j+1}, \dots, C_k \big), \qquad k \ge j, $$

conditioning on $\mathcal{G}_k$ gives the same conditional law of $h$ as conditioning on $\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\delta_j}$. The intersection of the covers is $A$, so these sigma-algebras increase to $\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\delta_j} := \sigma(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\delta_j}, A)$. Increasing martingale convergence gives the same conditional-law identity for $\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\delta_j}$. These latter sigma-algebras decrease with $j$, and their intersection is $\mathcal{A}$: this follows by sandwiching the covers between $A_{\delta_j}$ and $A_{(1+\sqrt{2})\delta_j}$ and using the definition of the field germ. This argument adds the finer set information by an increasing join; it does not interchange an intersection with a join of sigma-algebras.

Since the $\sigma$-algebras $\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{2^{-j}}$ are decreasing, the conditional law of $h$ given $\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{2^{-j}}$ is the same as the conditional law of $h$ given $\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{2^{-j}}$, and $\cap_{j=1}^\infty \widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{2^{-j}} = \mathcal{A}$, the reverse martingale convergence theorem implies the almost sure convergence $\mathcal{C}_{\widehat{A}_{2^{-j}}}$ as $j \to \infty$ in the weak sense, i.e., for each fixed $\phi$, we have a.s. that

$$ (\mathcal{C}_{\widehat{A}_{2^{-j}}}, \phi) \to \mathbf{E}[(h, \phi) \,|\, \mathcal{A}] \quad \text{as} \quad j \to \infty. \tag{3.4} $$

In order to finish the proof that (iii) holds for $A$, we need to show that $\mathbf{E}[(h, \phi) \,|\, \mathcal{A}]$ defines a distribution on $D$ and that the conditional law of $\phi \mapsto (h, \phi) - \mathbf{E}[(h, \phi) \,|\, \mathcal{A}]$ given $\mathcal{A}$ is that of a GFF on $D \setminus A$ with zero boundary conditions. Since (iii) holds for each $\widehat{A}_\delta$, we have that

$$ \mathbf{E}[\exp(i\theta(h, \phi)) \,|\, \widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{2^{-j}}] = \exp\Big( i\theta(\mathcal{C}_{\widehat{A}_{2^{-j}}}, \phi) - \theta^2 \sigma^2_{D\setminus \widehat{A}_{2^{-j}}}(\phi)/2 \Big) \tag{3.5} $$

where $\sigma^2_{D\setminus\widehat{A}_{2^{-j}}}(\phi) = \iint \phi(x) G_{D\setminus\widehat{A}_{2^{-j}}}(x, y) \phi(y)\, dx\, dy$ and $G_{D\setminus\widehat{A}_{2^{-j}}}$ is the Green's function for $\Delta$ on $D \setminus \widehat{A}_{2^{-j}}$. Thus sending $j \to \infty$, by combining (3.4) and (3.5) we have a.s. that

$$ \mathbf{E}[\exp(i\theta(h, \phi)) \,|\, \mathcal{A}] = \exp\big( i\theta\mathbf{E}[(h, \phi) \,|\, \mathcal{A}] - \theta^2 \sigma^2_{D\setminus A}(\phi)/2 \big) $$

Here $\sigma^2_{D\setminus A}(\phi)$ is the covariance pairing with the zero-extended Green kernel of $D \setminus A$. We justify that the conditional expectations in this formula define one random distribution. Choose a smooth cutoff $\zeta$ supported in $D$. The compactly supported field $\zeta h$, viewed on a containing rectangle, is square-integrable with values in $H^{-s}$ for any $s > 1$. Indeed, its Green covariance operator is bounded on $L^2$ on that compact set, and summing the Fourier-coordinate variances against $(1 + |n|^2)^{-s}$ is finite. Hilbert-space conditional expectation therefore defines $\mathbb{E}[\zeta h \mid \mathcal{A}]$ as an $H^{-s}$-valued random variable. For a countable exhaustion by such cutoffs these conditional expectations agree on overlaps, since their pairings with every member of a countable determining test family agree. They define a random distribution $M$ on $D$ with $(M, \phi) = \mathbb{E}[(h, \phi) \mid \mathcal{A}]$.

The conditional characteristic-function identity just proved, applied also to rational linear combinations of tests, identifies the conditional law of the distribution $h - M$ given $\mathcal{A}$ with the zero-boundary GFF on $D \setminus A$, extended by zero to $D$. The harmonicity of $M$ on $D \setminus A$ follows from the coarse harmonic projections on each compact set disjoint from $A$, or equivalently by testing their distributional limit against Laplacians of smooth functions supported there. Thus $M = \mathcal{C}_A$ supplies the distribution and conditional law required in (iii).

Now (iv) is immediate from (iii) when we set $h_1 = \mathcal{C}_A$.

To prove (ii) from (iv), it is enough to use orthogonal projections; no interchange of intersections and joins of sigma-algebras is needed. Subtract the deterministic harmonic mean of the original field, if present. Fix a deterministic open set $U \subseteq D$ and write

$$ X = P_U h, \qquad Y = h - P_U h, \qquad E = \{A \cap U = \varnothing\}. $$

Here $P_U$ is the GFF projection onto $H(U)$. It may be defined by choosing a Dirichlet-orthonormal basis obtained from a countable dense subset of $C_c^\infty(U)$ and taking the distributional limit of the corresponding Gaussian series. Denote the fixed law of $X$ by $\mu_U$. The ordinary Markov property says that $X$ and $Y$ are independent.

In the two-step construction, condition first on $(A, h_1)$. On $E$, $H(U) \subseteq H(D \setminus A)$. Since $h_1$ is harmonic on $U$, its Dirichlet pairing with every test in $C_c^\infty(U)$ is zero. Thus $P_U h = P_U h_2$ on this event. The orthogonal decomposition of the conditional zero-boundary field $h_2$ gives

$$ h_2 = P_U h_2 + (h_2 - P_U h_2), $$

where the first summand has law $\mu_U$ and is independent of the second. Its law is independent of $(A, h_1)$. Consequently, for bounded measurable $f$ and $g$,

$$ \mathbf{E}[f(X) g(A, Y) \mathbf{1}_E] = \mu_U(f) \mathbf{E}[g(A, Y) \mathbf{1}_E]. \tag{3.6} $$

One first checks this identity for cylinder functions of the distributional coordinates and then uses the monotone class theorem. This proves the required factorization on the avoidance event, including the information carried by the whole set $A$ there.

On $E^c$, the recorded set in (ii) is the fixed empty set. For every bounded measurable $g_0$ of $Y$, subtracting the avoidance identity from the unconditional Markov factorization yields

$$ \mathbf{E}[f(X) g_0(Y) \mathbf{1}_{E^c}] = \mu_U(f) \mathbf{E}[g_0(Y) \mathbf{1}_{E^c}]. $$

Together these two identities say that $X$ is independent of $(Y, \mathbf{1}_E, A\mathbf{1}_E)$, where $A\mathbf{1}_E$ denotes the recorded set with a cemetery value off $E$. In particular, conditional on $Y$, the event and recorded set are independent of $X$. This is precisely (ii), and completes the proof. $\square$

This gives a definition of $(h_1, \phi)_\nabla$ for all $\phi \in H(\mathbf{H})$ (which, as in the case of the GFF, is defined for each $\phi$ up to a set of measure zero which *a priori* depends on $\phi$). $$ \begin{aligned} \exp((h, \phi)_\nabla) &= \exp((\widetilde{h} \circ f_\tau + h_1, \phi)_\nabla) \\ &= \exp((\widetilde{h}, \phi \circ f_\tau^{-1})_\nabla) \times \exp((h_1, \phi)_\nabla). \end{aligned} $$

Note that $(\widetilde{h}, \phi \circ f_\tau^{-1})_\nabla$ is equal to the $(\cdot,\cdot)_\nabla$-inner product of $\widetilde{h}$ and the $(\cdot,\cdot)_\nabla$-orthogonal projection $\widetilde{\phi}_\tau$ of $\phi \circ f_\tau^{-1}$ onto $H(\mathbf{H})$ as the series expansion for $\widetilde{h}$ is given in terms of an orthonormal basis of $H(\mathbf{H})$. Equivalently, $\widetilde{\phi}_\tau$ is given by subtracting from $\phi \circ f_\tau^{-1}$ the harmonic extension of its values from $\partial\mathbf{H}$ to $\mathbf{H}$. For $x \in \partial\mathbf{H}$, we note that $\phi \circ f_\tau^{-1}(x)$ can only be non-zero provided $x \in f_\tau(\eta_U([0,\tau]))$ as $\phi \in H(\mathbf{H})$. Therefore the conditional

<!-- page 57 -->
law of $\widetilde{h}$ given $f_\tau, h_1$ under the weighted law is equal to that of a zero-boundary GFF on $\mathbf{H}$ plus $\widetilde{\phi}_\tau$. This implies that under the weighted law we have that

$$ h = \widehat{h} \circ f_\tau + \widetilde{\phi}_\tau \circ f_\tau + h_1 \tag{3.7} $$

where $\widehat{h}$ has the law of a zero-boundary GFF on $\mathbf{H}$ given $f_\tau$, $\widetilde{\phi}_\tau$, and $h_1$.

Suppose that $\mathfrak{h}$ is a function which is harmonic in $\mathbf{H}$ with zero boundary values on a neighborhood of $\partial U \cap \partial\mathbf{H}$. We then take $\widehat{\phi} \in C^\infty(\mathbf{H})$ so that $\widehat{\phi} \equiv 1$ on $U$ and $\widehat{\phi} \equiv 0$ outside a sufficiently small larger neighborhood of $U$ so that $\phi = \mathfrak{h}\widehat{\phi}$ vanishes on $\partial\mathbf{H}$. Thus $\phi \in H(\mathbf{H})$ so that we may apply the above for this choice of $\phi$. As mentioned earlier, we have that $h_1 = \mathfrak{h}_\tau$ in $\mathbf{H} \setminus K_\tau$. Observe that $\widetilde{\phi}_\tau \circ f_\tau + \mathfrak{h}_\tau$ (for this choice of $\phi$) restricted to $U$ is equal to the restriction to $U$ of the function $\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_\tau$ which is harmonic in $\mathbf{H} \setminus K_\tau$ with boundary values given by those of $\mathfrak{h}_\tau$ on $\mathbf{H} \cap \partial K_\tau$ and those of $\mathfrak{h}_\tau + \mathfrak{h}$ on $\partial\mathbf{H} \setminus K_\tau$. That is, we have that

$$ h = \widehat{h} \circ f_\tau + \widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_\tau \tag{3.8} $$

on $U \setminus K_\tau$. This proves the claimed Markov property because we can always transform the law of the field restricted to $U$ to the law of the field restricted to $U$ with boundary values which differ outside of $U$ by adding such a function to the field.

One important property of local sets is that given local sets $A_1$ and $A_2$, the *conditionally independent union* $A_1 \widetilde{\cup} A_2$ (defined in the proposition statement below) is also local. This result is contained in the following restatement of [SS13, Lemma 3.10]; see also Figure 3.1.

<a id="prop-3.7"></a>
**Proposition 3.7.** *Suppose $h$ is a GFF on $D$, $A_1, A_2$ are random variables taking values in $\Gamma$, and that $(A_1, h)$ and $(A_2, h)$ are couplings for which $A_1$ and $A_2$ are local. Let $A = A_1 \widetilde{\cup} A_2$ denote the random variable taking values in $\Gamma$ which is given by first sampling $h$, then sampling $A_1, A_2$ independently from their conditional laws given $h$, and then taking the union of $A_1$ and $A_2$. Then $A$ is also a local set of $h$. Moreover, given $\mathcal{A}$ and the pair $(A_1, A_2)$, the conditional law of $h$ is given by the sum of $\mathcal{C}_A$ plus an instance of the GFF on $D \setminus A$.*

<a id="prop-3.7-proof"></a>
*Proof.* Fix a deterministic open set $U \subseteq D$, and write $X = P_U h$ and $Y = h - P_U h$. Let $E_i = \{A_i \cap U = \varnothing\}$ and let $R_i$ record $(\mathbf{1}_{E_i}, A_i)$ on $E_i$, with a fixed cemetery set on $E_i^c$. By Lemma 3.6(ii), the conditional law of $R_i$ given $h$ is a measurable kernel depending only on $Y$. Conditional independence of $A_1, A_2$ given $h$ implies that the conditional law of $(R_1, R_2)$ given $h$ is the product of those two kernels. It also depends only on $Y$. Since $X$ and $Y$ are independent, $X$ has its unchanged zero-boundary GFF law after conditioning on $(Y, R_1, R_2)$. In particular, this proves locality of $A_1 \cup A_2$ and the avoidance criterion with both set labels retained.

We give the last passage to the stated conditional law explicitly. Use the decreasing finite square covers $C_n$ of $A = A_1 \cup A_2$ in the proof of Lemma 3.6. For a deterministic

<!-- page 58 -->
value $C$ of $C_n$, apply the preceding marked criterion with $U = D \setminus C$. On $\{C_n = C\}$ both sets avoid $U$, so $R_1, R_2$ record the full pair $(A_1, A_2)$. The event $\{C_n = C\}$ is a function of that pair. Thus the residual field $P_{D\setminus C} h$ has its unchanged zero-boundary GFF law conditional on the pair, $Q_C h$, and this event. Because $C_n$ takes only finitely many deterministic values, this proves

$$ \mathcal{L}(h \mid \mathcal{F}_n) = \mathcal{L}(Q_{C_n} h + h^{D\setminus C_n} \mid \mathcal{F}_n), \qquad \mathcal{F}_n = \sigma(A_1, A_2, Q_{C_n} h). $$

The sigma-algebras $\mathcal{F}_n$ decrease: the pair determines every cover, and for nested deterministic covers the smaller complement projection is determined by the larger one. Their conditional mean is $Q_{C_n} h$ and their covariance is $G_{D\setminus C_n}$. The locality of the unmarked union, already proved, and its germ identification give

$$ (Q_{C_n} h, \phi) \longrightarrow (\mathcal{C}_A, \phi) \quad \text{in } L^2 $$

for each test $\phi$; monotone Dirichlet-space approximation gives $G_{D\setminus C_n} \to G_{D\setminus A}$ in test pairings. Reverse martingale convergence applied to conditional characteristic functions now gives the Gaussian kernel with mean $\mathcal{C}_A$ and covariance $G_{D\setminus A}$ on $\bigcap_n \mathcal{F}_n$. This sigma-algebra also contains the two individual germs $\mathcal{A}_1$ and $\mathcal{A}_2$. To verify this without interchanging joins and intersections, fix $n$ and let $D^i_m$ be the shrinking square cover of $A_i$ used in the germ-identification proof. For all sufficiently large deterministic $m$,

$$ D^i_m \subseteq (A_i)_{(1+\sqrt{2})2^{-m}} \subseteq A_{2^{-n}} \subseteq C_n. $$

The pair $(A_1, A_2)$ determines these covers. On each of their finitely many possible values, nesting of the orthogonal complement spaces implies $Q_{D^i_m} h = Q_{D^i_m}(Q_{C_n} h)$, interpreted through the countable distributional projection coordinates. Hence $Q_{D^i_m} h$ is $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable. The germ proof gives its $L^2$ limit $\mathcal{C}_{A_i}$ in every test pairing, so $\mathcal{A}_i = \sigma(A_i, \mathcal{C}_{A_i}) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_n$, with completion. This holds for every $n$.

The limiting Gaussian kernel is $\mathcal{A}$-measurable, and

$$ \mathcal{J} := \sigma(A_1, A_2, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{A}_1, \mathcal{A}_2) \subseteq \bigcap_n \mathcal{F}_n. $$

Taking conditional expectations therefore gives the Gaussian law with mean $\mathcal{C}_A$ and covariance $G_{D\setminus A}$ conditional on $\mathcal{J}$. Forgetting the two individual germs gives exactly the claimed law conditional on $(A_1, A_2, \mathcal{A})$. The stronger version with $\mathcal{J}$ will also be used below. $\square$

We say that a local set $A$ of $h$ is *almost surely determined* by $h$ if there exists a modification of $A$ which is $\sigma(h)$-measurable. Many of the local sets we will work with in this article will be almost surely determined by the corresponding GFF, in which case the conditionally independent union is almost surely the same as an ordinary union.

The following proposition (see [SS13, Lemma 3.11]) allows us to estimate $\mathcal{C}_{A_1 \widetilde{\cup} A_2}$ near connected components of $A_2 \setminus A_1$ and $A_1 \cap A_2$ which consist of more than a single point in terms of $\mathcal{C}_{A_2}$.

<a id="prop-3.8"></a>
**Proposition 3.8.** *Assume that $D$ is a bounded, simply connected domain. Let $A_1, A_2$ be connected local sets. Then $\mathcal{C}_{A_1 \widetilde{\cup} A_2} - \mathcal{C}_{A_2}$ is almost surely a harmonic function in $D \setminus (A_1 \widetilde{\cup} A_2)$ that tends to zero on all sequences of points in $D \setminus (A_1 \widetilde{\cup} A_2)$ that tend to a limit in either:*

*(i) a connected component of $A_2 \setminus A_1$ (consisting of more than a single point) or*

*(ii) a connected component of $A_1 \cap A_2$ (consisting of more than a single point) at a point that has positive distance from $(A_1 \setminus A_2) \cup (A_2 \setminus A_1)$.*

<a id="prop-3.8-proof"></a>
*Proof.* We are going to give the argument for (i); the argument for (ii) is analogous.

By Proposition 3.7, the union $A_1 \widetilde{\cup} A_2$ is itself a local set, so $\mathcal{C}_{A_1 \widetilde{\cup} A_2}$ is well defined. Now, conditioned on $\mathcal{A}_2$ the law of the field in $D \setminus A_2$ is given by a GFF in $D \setminus A_2$ plus $\mathcal{C}_{A_2}$. We next claim that $\overline{A_1 \setminus A_2}$ is a local subset of $D \setminus A_2$, with respect to this GFF on $D \setminus A_2$. To see this, note that characterization (iii) for locality from Lemma 3.6 follows from the latter statement in Proposition 3.7.

By replacing $D$ with $D \setminus A_2$ and subtracting $\mathcal{C}_{A_2}$, we may thus reduce to the case that $A_2$ has no interior part (it consists only of the ambient boundary) and $\mathcal{C}_{A_2} = 0$. What remains to show is that if $A$ is any local set on $D$ then $\mathcal{C}_A$ (when viewed as a harmonic function on $D \setminus A$) tends to zero almost surely along all sequences of points in $D \setminus A$ that approach a point $x$ that lies in a connected component of $\partial D \setminus \widetilde{A}$, $\widetilde{A}$ given by the closure of $A \cap D$, that consists of more than a single point.

We spell out the localization in the last step; the local-set mean is not generally a Gaussian function. Work first with a zero-boundary GFF $g$ on a deterministic proper simply connected domain $O$, and a local set $B$ of $g$. Let $U$ be a bounded open patch and let $U_0$ have closure compactly contained in $U$. Put

$$ E = \{B \cap U \cap O = \varnothing\}, \quad g = g_U + q, $$

where $g_U$ is the projection onto $H(U \cap O)$ and $q$ is its orthogonal complement. Conditional on the full local-set information $\mathcal{B}$, on $E$ the two-step law writes $g = \mathcal{C}_B + g^B$, with $\mathcal{C}_B$ harmonic on $U \cap O$ and $g^B$ a zero-boundary GFF in $O \setminus B$. Its projection onto $H(U \cap O)$ has a centered law independent of $\mathcal{B}$. Thus $\mathbf{E}[g_U \mid \mathcal{B}] = 0$ on $E$. It follows, as an identity of harmonic distributions on $U \cap O$, that on $E$

$$ \mathcal{C}_B = \mathbf{E}[q \mid \mathcal{B}]. $$

The identity can first be proved on patches with closure contained in $U$ and then exhausted; all pairings involved are square-integrable.

Let $Q$ be a sufficiently small Whitney square of $O$ meeting $U_0$, with side length $\delta$ and fixed enlargement still inside $U$. The proof of Proposition 3.3, applied to the deterministic removed set $O \setminus U$, gives for every $p \ge 1$

$$ \mathbf{E}\Big[ \sup_Q |q|^p \Big] \le C_p \delta^{p/4}. $$

<!-- page 60 -->
Here the separation between $U_0$ and $O \setminus U$ fixes the constant. Conditional Jensen's inequality, first on a countable dense subset of $Q$ and then using harmonic continuity, gives

$$ \mathbf{E}\Big[ \mathbf{1}_E \sup_Q |\mathcal{C}_B|^p \Big] \le \mathbf{E}\Big[ \mathbf{1}_E \sup_Q |q|^p \Big] \le C_p \delta^{p/4}. $$

There are at most $C\delta^{-2}$ such squares at scale $\delta$. For $0 < \beta < 1/4$ and $p(1/4 - \beta) > 2$, Markov's inequality and Borel–Cantelli show that on $E$, simultaneously on all sufficiently small squares, $\sup_Q |\mathcal{C}_B| \le \delta^\beta$. Thus $\mathcal{C}_B$ tends uniformly to zero on this boundary patch. A countable collection of rational patches proves the assertion at all boundary points a positive distance from the ambient closure of $B \cap O$.

We justify the conditional-local-set reduction before applying this estimate. Put $A = A_1 \cup A_2$. The strengthened marked-union law proved above gives, conditional on $\mathcal{J} = \sigma(A_1, A_2, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{A}_1, \mathcal{A}_2)$,

$$ h - \mathcal{C}_{A_2} = (\mathcal{C}_A - \mathcal{C}_{A_2}) + h^{D\setminus A}, $$

where the residual field has the zero-boundary GFF law on $D \setminus A$. Conditional on $\mathcal{A}_2$, the field on the left is itself a zero-boundary GFF on $O = D \setminus A_2$. The displayed identity is a two-step local-set construction in $O$ for the remaining set $B = A_1 \cap O$, with the ambient boundary adjoined: its proposed mean is harmonic on $O \setminus B$, and after the finer information is forgotten the residual law depends only on $B$ and the fixed conditional domain $O$. Canonicality of the two-step mean therefore identifies its local-set mean with $\mathcal{C}_A - \mathcal{C}_{A_2}$. This argument uses the explicit retention of the two individual germs, not an assumed sigma-algebra distributivity rule.

Apply the preceding boundary estimate conditionally on $\mathcal{A}_2$, in each component of $O$, to this remaining local set. These components are simply connected: $A_2$ is connected and includes the ambient boundary. Select them by rational interior points. In case (i), the limit point has positive distance from the closed set $A_1$, so the required separation holds. In case (ii), it is exactly the positive-distance hypothesis from both set differences that supplies this separation. For each fixed conditional domain the preceding proof applies; regular conditional probabilities and Fubini's theorem give the assertion almost surely in the original joint law. This establishes both stated boundary limits without transferring a pointwise limit through an unjustified conditional expectation. $\square$

by reflection in this collar, gives continuity of the reflected maps and their derivatives in time, locally about each such time. The image prefix is again a continuous simple interior slit, so its capacity time and driving function are continuous on this lifetime as well.

*Proof.* We first justify the domains, rather than assuming that removal of an additional obstacle preserves the active component. Adjoin the closed lower half-plane and the point at infinity to $A$ and to $K_t$. The intersection of the resulting two closed sets in the sphere is exactly the compactified closed lower half-plane, by (HD1), and hence is connected. Each of their complements is connected. Janiszewski's separation theorem therefore shows that $\mathbb{H} \setminus (A \cup K_t)$ is connected. Its complement in the sphere is connected as well, so it is simply connected. This application of the planar separation theorem is independent of imaginary geometry; the form used here is stated, for example, in [Yua22, Theorem 2.4].

The maps $g_t$ and $\phi$ extend analytically by reflection through all real neighborhoods needed to map $A$ and $K_t$, respectively. Their images $E_t = g_t(A)$ and $\phi(K_t)$ are compact half-plane hulls: their complementary domains are the conformal images of $\mathbb{H} \setminus (A \cup K_t)$. The same observation shows that $\phi(K_t)$ is exactly the filling of $\phi(\gamma([0,t]))$. Indeed the components cut off by the original trace are disjoint from $A$, and $\phi$ maps each such component and the common active component conformally. Equivalently, a point outside $A \cup K_t$ is connected to infinity in their complement, whereas every point of $K_t \setminus \gamma([0,t])$ is separated from infinity by the trace already in $\mathbb{H}$.

$$ d\mathfrak{h}_t(z) = \operatorname{Im}\left( \frac{2}{f_t(z)} \right) dB_t. \tag{3.15} $$

See [She10, Remark 4.1 and Remark 4.2] for some additional interpretation.

Since $\mathfrak{h}_t(z)$ is a continuous local martingale for each fixed $z$, it is thus a Brownian motion under the quadratic variation parameterization, which we can give explicitly:

$$ d\langle \mathfrak{h}_t(z), \mathfrak{h}_t(z) \rangle = -dC_t(z) \quad \text{and} \quad C_t(z) := \log \operatorname{Im}(f_t(z)) - \operatorname{Re}(\log f_t'(z)). $$

If $z$ is a point in a simply connected domain $D$, and $\phi$ conformally maps the unit disk to $D$, with $\phi(0) = z$, then we refer to the quantity $|\phi'(0)|$ as the *conformal radius* of $D$ viewed from $z$. If, in the above definition of conformal radius, we replaced the unit disk with $\mathbf{H}$ and $0$ with $i$, this would only change the definition by a multiplicative constant. Thus, $C_t(z)$ is (up to an additive constant) the log of the conformal radius of $\mathbf{H} \setminus K_t$ viewed from $z$. Under the time parameter $C_0(z) - C_t(z)$, the process $\mathfrak{h}_t(z) - \mathfrak{h}_0(z)$ is a standard Brownian motion up to its terminal quadratic variation (with an independent continuation if needed). The fact that $d\langle \mathfrak{h}_t(z), \mathfrak{h}_t(z) \rangle = -dC_t(z)$ may be computed directly via Itô's formula but it is also easy to see by taking $y \to z$ in the formulas for $\langle \mathfrak{h}_t(y), \mathfrak{h}_t(z) \rangle$ and the formulas we will give below.

We will now show that weighted averages of $\mathfrak{h}_t$ over multiple points in $\mathbf{H}$ are also continuous local martingales (and hence Brownian motions when properly parameterized). The calculation will make use of the function

$$ G(y, z) := \log|y - \bar{z}| - \log|y - z|, $$

which is the Dirichlet Green's function for $\Delta$ on $\mathbf{H}$. That is, $G$ is the distributional solution of $\Delta G(y, \cdot) = -2\pi\delta_y(\cdot)$ with zero boundary conditions where $\delta_y$ denotes the Dirac mass at $y$.

We let $G_t(y, z) = G(f_t(y), f_t(z))$ when $y$ and $z$ are both in the infinite component of $\mathbf{H} \setminus K_t$; it is otherwise equal to zero. Observe for $t < \tau(y) \wedge \tau(z)$ that

$$ dG_t(y, z) = -\operatorname{Im}\left( \frac{2}{f_t(y)} \right) \operatorname{Im}\left( \frac{2}{f_t(z)} \right) dt \quad \text{and} \tag{3.16} $$

$$ d\langle \mathfrak{h}_t(y), \mathfrak{h}_t(z) \rangle = -dG_t(y, z). \tag{3.17} $$

The details of (3.16) in the case of $\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa$ (with no $\rho$ values) is worked out explicitly in [She10, Section 4]. The case with $\rho$ values follows from a similar calculation. It is then immediate from (3.15) that (3.17) holds.

Now we let $\phi$ be a smooth compactly supported function on $\mathbf{H}$, fix $U \subseteq \mathbf{H}$ open which contains the support $\operatorname{supp}(\phi)$ of $\phi$, and let

$$ \tau_U = \inf\{t \ge 0 : K_t \cap U \ne \emptyset\}. \tag{3.18} $$

<!-- page 75 -->
We let

$$ E_t(\phi) := \iint_{\mathbf{H}\times\mathbf{H}} \phi(y) G_t(y, z) \phi(z)\, dy\, dz $$

and we will show that

$$ d\langle (\mathfrak{h}_t, \phi), (\mathfrak{h}_t, \phi) \rangle = -dE_t(\phi) \quad \text{for} \quad t < \tau_U. \tag{3.19} $$

Here is a localization which justifies the integration. On a bounded time interval strictly before $T_* \wedge \tau_U$, localize so that $\inf_{z\in\operatorname{supp}\phi} \operatorname{Im} g_t(z)$ is bounded below by a positive constant and the relevant continuous driving quantities are bounded. Stochastic Fubini then gives

$$ d(\mathfrak{h}_t, \phi) = \left( \int \phi(z) \operatorname{Im} \frac{2}{f_t(z)}\, dz \right) dB_t. $$

The same localization permits ordinary differentiation and integration of (3.16). The logarithmic diagonal singularity of the Green function is integrable against the smooth compactly supported test functions. Therefore (3.19) holds, and

$$ (\mathfrak{h}_t, \phi)^2 + E_t(\phi) \quad \text{is a continuous local martingale for } t < T_* \wedge \tau_U. \tag{3.20} $$

These localizations exhaust every compact time interval on which the test support remains outside the hull. Since $E_t(\phi) \ge 0$, the bracket increment is bounded by $E_0(\phi)$. In particular the stopped field average is a true square-integrable martingale at any finite protected-domain stop strictly before $T_* \wedge \tau_U$. The characteristic-function martingale $\exp(i\theta(\mathfrak{h}_t, \phi) - \theta^2 E_t(\phi)/2)$ is bounded in modulus by one. Optional stopping at these protected-domain stops is therefore valid, without taking a value on a swallowed test domain.

The value at a first swallowing time requires care: a hull can disconnect an open set without its trace entering that set. In the coupling lemmas below, we therefore use relatively compact open sets $U \Subset \mathbf{H}$ and finite stopping times $\tau$ satisfying $\tau < \tau_U$ almost surely. All martingale identities are evaluated while the test support remains in the unbounded component. No assertion about a restriction of $\mathfrak{h}_\tau$ to a swallowed set is needed.

Write $T_*$ for the continuation threshold. We use a bounded increasing announcing sequence $T_n < T_*$ with $T_n \uparrow T_*$ and $T_n \le n$, in the usual augmentation of the natural driving filtration. Here and below the assertion is vacuous if the initial state is already at the threshold. Such a sequence exists without a strong-solution assumption. Indeed, after amalgamating initially coincident force points, the first collision time of each initially separated force point with $W$ is predictable: its continuous positive gap is announced by its first passage below $1/m$. Force points that have merged remain merged, by the gap equation and the ordering. Thus the weight of the group which collides with $W$ can change only at one of these finitely many first collision times. The

<!-- page 76 -->
threshold is one of those times, or infinity. At each such predictable time, the entire colliding group, including simultaneous collisions, is measurable just before the time because the stopped driving coordinates are continuous. The event that this is the first group with weight at most $-2$ is therefore measurable just before that time. A selection from finitely many predictable times by these events is predictable. An announcing sequence for $T_*$, also capped at $n$, gives the asserted $T_n$. This argument uses only the continuous stopped SDE state at its collision time; it assigns no endpoint value to a field or a Loewner map on a swallowed set.

Here is a countable family of stops which suffices. For a finite union $U$ of rational balls with $\overline{U} \subset \mathbf{H}$, put

$$ s_{U,n} = T_n \wedge \inf\left\{ t \ge 0 : \inf_{z\in\overline{U}} \operatorname{Im} g_t(z) \le n^{-1} \right\}, \qquad n \in \mathbf{N}. $$

The infimum is taken before the first swallowing of a point of $\overline{U}$; if the initial infimum is already at most $n^{-1}$, the stop is zero. In particular $s_{U,n} \le T_n < T_*$, so every coupling identity below is evaluated before the continuation threshold.

The stops $s_{U,n}$ increase with $n$. They occur before the first swallowing of $U$: a point whose Loewner image is swallowed has imaginary part tending to zero, so it crosses a positive threshold first. In particular $s_{U,n} < \tau_U$. If $t < T_*$ and $\overline{U} \cap K_t = \varnothing$, compactness and continuity give $\inf_{z\in\overline{U}} \operatorname{Im} g_t(z) > 0$, and consequently $s_{U,n} > t$ for all sufficiently large $n$.

We will now combine the above calculations to establish the following intermediate step in the proof of Theorem 1.1.

<a id="lem-3.11"></a>
**Lemma 3.11.** *Fix $U \Subset \mathbf{H}$ open, let $\tau_U$ be as in (3.18), and let $\tau$ be a finite $\mathcal{F}_t$-stopping time such that $\mathbf{P}[\tau < \tau_U \wedge T_*] = 1$. Consider the random field $h_{U,\tau}$ on $U$ which is generated by:*

1. *Sampling the driving history $(W_s, V_s^{i,q})_{0\le s\le\tau}$, including $\tau$,*
2. *Sampling a zero boundary GFF $h^0$ on $\mathbf{H} \setminus K_\tau$ and adding it to $\mathfrak{h}_\tau$, and then*
3. *Restricting the sum to $U$.*

*Then $h_{U,\tau} \stackrel{d}{=} h|_U$ where $h = \widetilde{h} + \mathfrak{h}_0$ and $\widetilde{h}$ is a zero-boundary GFF on $\mathbf{H}$.*

<a id="lem-3.11-proof"></a>
*Proof.* Since $h|_U$ is a Gaussian field, it suffices to show that $(h_{U,\tau}, \phi) \stackrel{d}{=} (h, \phi)$ for each $\phi \in C_0^\infty(\mathbf{H})$ with $\operatorname{supp}(\phi) \subseteq U$. Note that $\mathfrak{h}_\tau$ is measurable with respect to $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ and that $\operatorname{Var}((h^0, \phi)) = E_\tau(\phi)$. Consequently, for each $\theta \in \mathbf{R}$, we have that

$$ \mathbf{E}\big[ \exp\left( i\theta(h_{U,\tau}, \phi) \right) \big] = \mathbf{E}\big[ \mathbf{E}\big[ \exp\left( i\theta(h^0, \phi) \right) \,\big|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau \big] \exp\left( i\theta(\mathfrak{h}_\tau, \phi) \right) \big] $$

<!-- page 77 -->
$$ \begin{aligned} &= \mathbf{E}\bigg[ \exp\bigg( i\theta(\mathfrak{h}_\tau, \phi) - \frac{\theta^2 E_\tau(\phi)}{2} \bigg) \bigg] \\ &= \mathbf{E}\bigg[ \exp\bigg( i\theta(\mathfrak{h}_0, \phi) - \frac{\theta^2 E_0(\phi)}{2} \bigg) \bigg] \quad \text{(by (3.19))} \\ &= \mathbf{E}\big[ \exp\left( i\theta(h, \phi) \right) \big]. \end{aligned} $$

This proves the lemma. $\square$

In order to work towards completing the proof of Theorem 1.1, we will explain how to generalize Lemma 3.11 to the setting in which it holds for multiple stopping times and open sets. We begin with the setting of a finite number of stopping times.

<a id="lem-3.12"></a>
**Lemma 3.12.** *Assume that we have the setup of Lemma 3.11 for a given open set $U \subseteq \mathbf{H}$. Assume that $n \in \mathbf{N}$ and $\tau_1, \dots, \tau_n$ are finite $\mathcal{F}_t$-stopping times with $\mathbf{P}[\tau_i < \tau_U \wedge T_*] = 1$ for each $1 \le i \le n$. For each $1 \le i \le n$, let $h_{U,\tau_i}$ have the law as defined in Lemma 3.11. There exists a coupling of the laws $h_{U,\tau_i}$ for $1 \le i \le n$ so that they are all generated using the same instance $(W, V^{i,q})$ of the $\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa(\underline{\rho})$ driving process such that for $h = \widetilde{h} + \mathfrak{h}_0$, $\widetilde{h}$ a zero-boundary GFF on $\mathbf{H}$, we almost surely have that $h_{U,\tau_i} = h|_U$. Moreover, we have that the conditional law of the projection of $h$ onto $H(U)$ given $\mathcal{F}_{\tau_i}$ and the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(U)$ is that of a zero-boundary GFF on $U$ for each $1 \le i \le n$.*

<a id="lem-3.12-proof"></a>
*Proof.* For simplicity, we will first explain the proof in the case that $n = 2$ and then later explain how to generalize it to arbitrary values of $n \in \mathbf{N}$. Lemma 3.11 implies that there exists a coupling of the law of the pair $(h_{U,\tau}, (W, V^{i,q}))$, with the driving process stopped at its continuation threshold, with $h$ such that $h_{U,\tau} = h|_U$ almost surely.

Suppose that $\tau_1, \tau_2$ are finite $\mathcal{F}_t$-stopping times with $\mathbf{P}[\tau_i < \tau_U \wedge T_*] = 1$ for $i = 1, 2$. By applying the above first with the stopping time $\sigma_1 = \tau_1 \wedge \tau_2$ and then with $\sigma_2 = \tau_1 \vee \tau_2$ (using the conformal Markov property of $\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa(\underline{\rho})$), it is easy to see that we can construct a coupling of the law of the triple $(h_{U,\sigma_1}, h_{U,\sigma_2}, (W, V^{i,q}))$ with $h$ such that $h_{U,\sigma_i} = h|_U$ almost surely for $i = 1, 2$. In particular, on the event that $\tau_1 = \sigma_1$, we have almost surely that

$$ h_{U,\tau_1} = h_{U,\sigma_1} = h|_U. \tag{3.21} $$

On the event that $\tau_1 = \sigma_2$, we almost surely have that

$$ h_{U,\tau_1} = h_{U,\sigma_2} = h|_U. \tag{3.22} $$

Since one of the two events $\tau_1 = \sigma_1$ or $\tau_1 = \sigma_2$ must hold, we conclude from (3.21) and (3.22) that $h_{U,\tau_1} = h|_U$ almost surely. Similarly, we have that $h_{U,\tau_2} = h|_U$ almost surely.

By construction, we have that

$$ h_{U,\sigma_i} = (h^{\sigma_i} \circ f_{\sigma_i} + \mathfrak{h}_{\sigma_i})|_U $$

<!-- page 78 -->
where $h^{\sigma_i}$ for $i = 1, 2$ has the law of a zero-boundary GFF on $\mathbf{H}$ given $\mathcal{F}_{\sigma_i}$. We let

$$ h^{\tau_i} = h^{\sigma_1}\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma_1=\tau_i\}} + h^{\sigma_2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_i>\sigma_1\}}. $$

Then we have that

$$ h_{U,\tau_i} = (h^{\tau_i} \circ f_{\tau_i} + \mathfrak{h}_{\tau_i})|_U $$

To finish the proof of the first assertion of the lemma for $n = 2$, we need to show that $h^{\tau_i}$ for $i = 1, 2$ has the law of a zero-boundary GFF on $\mathbf{H}$ given $\mathcal{F}_{\tau_i}$. We will explain the proof for $i = 1$ (as the proof for $i = 2$ is analogous). Fix a test function $\phi \in C_0^\infty(\mathbf{H})$ and $\theta \in \mathbf{R}$. Using that both of the events $\{\sigma_1 = \tau_1\}$ and $\{\tau_1 > \sigma_1\}$ are $\mathcal{F}_{\tau_1}$-measurable, we have that

$$ \begin{aligned} &\mathbf{E}[\exp(i\theta(h^{\tau_1}, \phi)) \,|\, \mathcal{F}_{\tau_1}] \\ =&\mathbf{E}[\exp(i\theta(h^{\tau_1}, \phi)) \,|\, \mathcal{F}_{\tau_1}]\, \mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma_1=\tau_1\}} + \mathbf{E}[\exp(i\theta(h^{\tau_1}, \phi)) \,|\, \mathcal{F}_{\tau_1}]\, \mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_1>\sigma_1\}} \\ =&\mathbf{E}[\exp(i\theta(h^{\sigma_1}, \phi)) \,|\, \mathcal{F}_{\sigma_1}]\, \mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma_1=\tau_1\}} + \mathbf{E}[\exp(i\theta(h^{\sigma_2}, \phi)) \,|\, \mathcal{F}_{\sigma_2}]\, \mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_1>\sigma_1\}} \\ =&\exp\bigg( -\frac{\theta^2 E_0(\phi)}{2} \bigg) \end{aligned} $$

where the last equality follows because we know that $h^{\sigma_i}$ has the law of a zero-boundary GFF on $\mathbf{H}$ given $\mathcal{F}_{\sigma_i}$ for $i = 1, 2$.

Suppose now that $n \in \mathbf{N}$. We can iterate the same argument above with a finite collection of stopping times $\tau_1, \dots, \tau_n$ with $\mathbf{P}[\tau_i < \tau_U \wedge T_*] = 1$ for all $i = 1, \dots, n$ to obtain a coupling such that $h_{U,\tau_i} = h|_U$ almost surely for all $i = 1, \dots, n$. Indeed, to do so for each $1 \le j \le n$ we let $\sigma_j$ be the first time $t$ that at least $j$ of the stopping times $\tau_i$ have occurred at or before time $t$. Then each $\sigma_j$ is a stopping time and we have that $\sigma_1 \le \sigma_2 \le \dots \le \sigma_n$. Thus we can construct the coupling iteratively as described above in the case that $n = 2$. Under this coupling, we have that $h_{U,\sigma_j} = h|_U$ almost surely for each $1 \le j \le n$. Since for each $1 \le i \le n$ there exists $1 \le j \le n$ such that $\tau_i = \sigma_j$, we then have that $h_{U,\tau_i} = h|_U$ almost surely for each $1 \le i \le n$. The result follows because a simple elaboration of the argument given just above implies that we can write $h_{U,\tau_i}$ as $(h^{\tau_i} \circ f_{\tau_i} + \mathfrak{h}_{\tau_i})|_U$ where $h^{\tau_i}$ has the law of a zero-boundary GFF on $\mathbf{H}$ given $\mathcal{F}_{\tau_i}$.

In the construction of the coupling given above, it is not immediately clear that the conditional law of the projection of $h$ onto $H(U)$ given *both* its projection onto $H^\perp(U)$ and $\mathcal{F}_{\tau_i}$ is that of a zero-boundary GFF in $U$ for each $1 \le i \le n$ even though it is immediate from the construction that this holds if we condition on either the projection onto $H^\perp(U)$ or $\mathcal{F}_{\tau_i}$. We can modify the coupling so that it has this property by, given both the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(U)$ and $\mathcal{F}_{\tau_1\vee\cdots\vee\tau_n}$, resampling the projection of $h$ onto $H(U)$ from the law of a zero-boundary GFF on $U$. Note that performing this resampling operation leaves the marginal law of $h$ unchanged (since the law of the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(U)$ is unchanged as is the conditional law of the projection of $h$ onto $H(U)$ given its projection onto $H^\perp(U)$). This resampling operation also preserves the conditional law of $h|_U$ given $\mathcal{F}_{\tau_i}$ for each $1 \le i \le n$. Indeed, this follows because

this resampling operation preserves the restriction of the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(U)$ to $U$. Moreover, by the Markov property for $h_{U,\tau_i}$ given $\mathcal{F}_{\tau_i}$ (which is the restriction of a GFF on $\mathbf{H} \setminus K_{\tau_i}$ to $U$), we know that the conditional law of the projection of $h$ onto $H(U)$ given both $\mathcal{F}_{\tau_i}$ and the restriction of the projection of $h$ onto $H^\perp(U)$ to $U$ is that of a zero boundary GFF on $U$. This proves the claim. $\square$

**Lemma 3.13.** *Suppose that $n \in \mathbf{N}$, $U_1, \dots, U_n \Subset \mathbf{H}$ are open sets, and $\tau_1, \dots, \tau_n$ are finite $\mathcal{F}_t$-stopping times such that $\mathbf{P}[\tau_i < \tau_{U_i} \wedge T_*] = 1$ for each $1 \le i \le n$. <a id="lem-3.13-proof"></a>
*Proof.* As in the proof of Lemma 3.12, we begin with the argument in the case of two open sets $U_1, U_2 \subseteq \mathbf{H}$; define $\tau_{U_1}, \tau_{U_2}$ accordingly and suppose that $\mathbf{P}[\tau_i < \tau_{U_i} \wedge T_*] = 1$ for $i = 1, 2$. In summary, at this point we have a coupling such that the following hold:

- $h|_{U_j} = h_{U_j,\tau_j}$ for $j = 1, 2$ and $h|_{V_1} = h_{V_1,\sigma_1}$ almost surely.

<a id="lem-3.14-proof"></a>
*Proof.* For each $n$, use Lemma 3.13 to obtain a joint law $\mu_n$ of $h$, the driving process, and the first $n$ fields $h_{U_i,\tau_i}$ with the stated properties. We explain the choice of coordinates in the compactness argument, since stopping times and conditional independence do not in general pass to a weak limit without this precaution.

Represent every distribution by its evaluations on a countable family of smooth compactly supported test functions which is dense in the test-function topology on each compact subset. Include the test families for all the $U_i$ in this choice. Such a family determines a distribution; mere density in $L^2$ would not suffice for this purpose. For each $i$, also record countable Gaussian coordinates of the projections of $h$ onto $H(U_i)$ and $H^\perp(U_i)$. Denote these two coordinate sequences by $P_i$ and $Q_i$. Finally, record $\tau_i$, the driving path stopped at $\tau_i$, and a countable family of bounded random variables which generates $\mathcal{F}_{\tau_i}$ up to null sets; denote these coordinates by $R_i$. Such a family exists because the driving-path probability space is standard and its measure algebra is separable. Each variable can be chosen as a measurable function of the full canonical path. Including these generators explicitly also accommodates the usual right-continuous augmentation without assuming that the unaugmented stopped-path sigma-algebra is already right-continuous. The Brownian driver, when included in the filtration, is determined by the integrated SLE equation and can also be included among the recorded path coordinates.

The joint marginal of all the added field coordinates together with $h$ is fixed, independently of $n$, and the analogous enlarged driving-path marginal is fixed as well. Each of the finitely or countably many other coordinates has a fixed marginal once it is present. These facts give tightness on each finite truncation of the countable product of coordinate spaces. Passing to a diagonal subsequence gives consistent finite-coordinate limits, and hence a joint limiting law by the Kolmogorov extension theorem. The marginal of the enlarged field coordinates is still its fixed law; therefore those coordinates retain their prescribed measurable dependence on $h$ in the limit. The same observation applies to every $R_i$ and the driving path, even when the stopping-time functional is not continuous in the uniform topology.

For each fixed $i$, the pair $(h_{U_i,\tau_i}, R_i)$ has its prescribed fixed joint law for all $n \ge i$, so this remains true in the limit. Equality of $h_{U_i,\tau_i}$ with $h|_{U_i}$ is equality of the common test-function coordinates and is closed under joint weak convergence. Finally the conditional-law assertion in the lemma is precisely that $P_i$ has its fixed centered Gaussian law and is independent of $(Q_i, R_i)$. Independence is closed under joint weak convergence: apply the defining factorization to bounded continuous functions of finitely many coordinates and then use the monotone class theorem. Consequently this independence also holds in the limiting law. These observations prove every assertion of the lemma simultaneously for all $i \in \mathbf{N}$. $\square$

**Aux. lemma 11** (Local Sobolev regularity of a field and its conditional means)**.** *Let $h$ be a planar GFF with a deterministic harmonic mean on a proper domain $D$, with finite Green covariance on compact subsets. For every $0 < \varepsilon < 1$ and $\zeta \in C_c^\infty(D)$,*

<a id="id-SR1"></a>
$$ \mathbb{E}\|\zeta h\|^2_{H^{-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^2)} < \infty. \tag{SR1} $$

*The same holds after a deterministic Cameron–Martin shift. For any sigma-algebra $\mathcal{F}$, the distributional conditional mean $M = \mathbb{E}[h \mid \mathcal{F}]$ has a version with $\zeta M \in H^{-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^2)$ almost surely. All these assertions may be imposed simultaneously on a countable family of cutoffs and exponents.*

<a id="aux-11-proof"></a>
*Proof.* By a finite smooth partition of unity on the support of the given cutoff, it suffices to use cutoffs supported in a rectangle $Q \Subset D$. The deterministic Markov decomposition on $Q$ writes $h = h^Q + u$, where $h^Q$ is a zero-boundary GFF in $Q$ and $u$ is harmonic there. On a compact subset of $Q$, the harmonic remainder has finite pointwise second moments bounded uniformly on that compact set: its covariance is the difference of the Green kernels of $D$ and $Q$, whose diagonal logarithmic singularities cancel. Thus $\mathbb{E}\|\zeta u\|^2_{L^2} < \infty$. The deterministic mean contributes a smooth function locally.

Here is a direct Fourier bound for the zero-boundary part, so no boundary regularity assertion about a random revealed set is needed. For $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^2$, put $\phi_\xi(x) = \zeta(x) e^{-i\xi\cdot x}$. The Green covariance identity gives, up to the fixed normalization of the Dirichlet inner product,

<a id="id-SR2"></a>
$$ \mathbb{E}\left| \widehat{\zeta h^Q}(\xi) \right|^2 = c\|\phi_\xi\|^2_{H_0^{-1}(Q)} \le \frac{C_\zeta}{1 + |\xi|^2}. \tag{SR2} $$

<!-- page 84 -->
Here $H_0^{-1}(Q)$ denotes the dual norm of the Dirichlet norm on $H_0^1(Q)$. For $|\xi| \ge 1$, the last inequality follows by integrating one derivative in the direction $\xi/|\xi|$ against a test $v \in C_c^\infty(Q)$:

$$ \left| \int_Q \zeta(x) e^{-i\xi\cdot x} v(x)\, d^2x \right| \le \frac{1}{|\xi|} \left( \|\zeta\|_{L^2} \|\nabla v\|_{L^2} + \|\nabla\zeta\|_{L^2} \|v\|_{L^2} \right) \le \frac{C_\zeta}{|\xi|} \|\nabla v\|_{L^2}. $$

The last step is the Poincaré inequality on the fixed rectangle; it also follows by integrating $v$ from one of its sides and applying Cauchy–Schwarz. For bounded $\xi$ use the same Poincaré inequality without integration by parts.

By the Fourier definition of the inhomogeneous Sobolev norm and Tonelli's theorem, (SR2) gives

$$ \mathbb{E}\|\zeta h^Q\|^2_{H^{-\varepsilon}} \le C_\zeta \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} (1 + |\xi|^2)^{-1-\varepsilon}\, d^2\xi < \infty. $$

This proves (SR1). A Cameron–Martin shift is locally in $H^1$, hence locally in $H^{-\varepsilon}$, and does not alter the covariance estimate.

Take the Bochner conditional expectation of the square-integrable $H^{-\varepsilon}$-valued random variable $\zeta h$. Conditional expectation is a contraction in this Hilbert space, so it supplies $\zeta M$ with the asserted regularity. A countable test family verifies the compatibility of these versions on overlapping cutoffs. A partition of unity then gives the claim for every compact subset of $D$. $\square$

**Aux. lemma 12** (A small-dimensional set supports no such distribution)**.** *Let $0 < \varepsilon < 1$, let $E$ be a relatively closed subset of an open set $U \subset \mathbb{R}^2$, and suppose*

<a id="id-SR3"></a>
$$ \dim_{\mathrm{H}}(E \cap K) < 2 - 2\varepsilon \quad \text{for every compact } K \Subset U. \tag{SR3} $$

*If a distribution $T \in H^{-\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ is supported on $E$, then $T = 0$.*

<a id="aux-12-proof"></a>
*Proof.* We give the cutoff argument. For $0 < \varepsilon < 1$, the norm $\|v\|^2_{H^\varepsilon}$ is comparable, with deterministic constants depending only on $\varepsilon$, to

<a id="id-SR4"></a>
$$ \|v\|^2_{L^2} + \iint_{\mathbb{R}^2\times\mathbb{R}^2} \frac{|v(x) - v(y)|^2}{|x - y|^{2+2\varepsilon}}\, d^2x\, d^2y. \tag{SR4} $$

For smooth compactly supported $v$, this follows directly by Fourier transform and Fubini: integrating $|e^{i\xi\cdot z} - 1|^2 |z|^{-2-2\varepsilon}$ in $z$ gives a finite positive constant times $|\xi|^{2\varepsilon}$, by rotation and scaling. Completion gives (SR4) for the fractional Sobolev space.

Let $E_0 \Subset U$ be compact with $\dim_{\mathrm{H}} E_0 < 2 - 2\varepsilon$. Given $\delta > 0$, cover $E_0$ by finitely many balls $B(x_j, r_j)$, with $0 < r_j < 1$, so that $\sum_j r_j^{2-2\varepsilon} < \delta$ and all doubled balls lie in a fixed compact subset of $U$. The existence of a countable such cover is the definition of zero $(2 - 2\varepsilon)$-dimensional Hausdorff measure; compactness permits a finite subcover without

<!-- page 85 -->
increasing the sum. Choose smooth bumps $v_j$, equal to one on these balls, with values in $[0, 1]$ and support in $B(x_j, 2r_j)$. Scaling in (SR4) gives

$$ \|v_j\|^2_{L^2} + [v_j]^2_\varepsilon \le C_\varepsilon r_j^{2-2\varepsilon}. $$

For $v = \max_j v_j$, pointwise

<a id="id-SR5"></a>
$$ v(x)^2 \le \sum_j v_j(x)^2, \qquad |v(x) - v(y)|^2 \le \sum_j |v_j(x) - v_j(y)|^2. \tag{SR5} $$

To check the second inequality, suppose $v(x) \ge v(y)$ and choose an index attaining the maximum at $x$; then $0 \le v(x) - v(y) \le v_j(x) - v_j(y)$. The opposite order is symmetric. Hence $\|v\|^2_{H^\varepsilon} \le C\delta$. The function $v$ equals one in an open neighborhood of $E_0$. Convolve with a nonnegative smooth unit-mass mollifier of radius smaller than half the distance of $E_0$ from the complement of that neighborhood. The resulting $v_\delta \in C_c^\infty(U)$ still equals one near $E_0$, and Jensen's inequality in (SR4) shows that its norm has the same bound. Its support can be kept in the fixed compact neighborhood already chosen.

Multiplication by a fixed smooth compactly supported function $\phi$ is bounded on $H^\varepsilon$. Indeed, in (SR4) use

$$ |\phi(x)v(x) - \phi(y)v(y)|^2 \le 2\|\phi\|^2_\infty |v(x) - v(y)|^2 + 2|v(y)|^2 |\phi(x) - \phi(y)|^2. $$

The second term is integrable with a bound $C_\phi\|v\|_2^2$, because $|\phi(x) - \phi(y)|$ is bounded both by a constant and by $C|x - y|$, and $0 < \varepsilon < 1$.

Now fix $\phi \in C_c^\infty(U)$ and choose a compact neighborhood $K \Subset U$ of its support. Apply the preceding construction to $E_0 = E \cap K$. Since $v_\delta = 1$ near $E_0$ and $T$ is supported on $E$,

$$ T(\phi) = T(\phi v_\delta), \qquad |T(\phi v_\delta)| \le C\|\phi v_\delta\|_{H^\varepsilon} \le C_\phi \delta^{1/2}. $$

The first bound uses a fixed compact cutoff of $T$ belonging to $H^{-\varepsilon}$. Letting $\delta \downarrow 0$ proves $T(\phi) = 0$ for every test, as required. $\square$

**Aux. lemma 14** (A direct strict dimension bound below parameter eight)**.** *For each $0 < \kappa < 8$, there is a deterministic $d_\kappa < 2$ such that an ordinary chordal $\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa$ trace has, almost surely, interior Hausdorff dimension at most $d_\kappa$. On every compact $K \Subset \mathbb{H}$, the number $N_\delta$ of dyadic squares of side $\delta$ meeting the trace in $K$ satisfies $N_\delta \le C_K \delta^{-d_\kappa}$ for every sufficiently small dyadic $\delta$, with a finite random $C_K$. The proof uses the ordinary-SLE continuous-trace theorem, but neither simplicity nor an exact SLE dimension theorem.*

<a id="aux-14-proof"></a>
*Proof.* Fix $z \in \mathbb{H}$. Until its swallowing time, write

$$ Z_t = g_t(z) - W_t = X_t + iY_t, \qquad \Upsilon_t = \frac{Y_t}{|g_t'(z)|}, \qquad \Theta_t = \arg Z_t \in (0, \pi). $$

Twice $\Upsilon_t$ is the conformal radius of the active domain from $z$. The Loewner equation and Itô's formula give

<a id="id-DU1"></a>
$$ d\log Z_t = (2 - \kappa/2) Z_t^{-2}\, dt - \sqrt{\kappa} Z_t^{-1}\, dB_t, \qquad d\log\Upsilon_t = -\frac{4Y_t^2}{|Z_t|^4}\, dt. \tag{DU1} $$

<!-- page 87 -->
Set

$$ s(t) = \log\frac{\operatorname{Im} z}{\Upsilon_t} = \int_0^t \frac{4Y_u^2}{|Z_u|^4}\, du. $$

In this increasing clock, the angle satisfies

<a id="id-DU2"></a>
$$ d\theta_s = \frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{2} d\widetilde{B}_s + \frac{\kappa - 4}{4} \cot\theta_s\, ds, \qquad 0 < \theta_s < \pi. \tag{DU2} $$

Indeed, its original drift and quadratic-variation rates are $(\kappa - 4) X_t Y_t / |Z_t|^4$ and $\kappa Y_t^2 / |Z_t|^4$, respectively. Division by the clock rate proves both coefficients. The Brownian motion in this clock is obtained from the continuous local martingale by the quadratic-variation time change; if its terminal quadratic variation is finite, append an independent Brownian continuation there. Up to the end of its original clock, the angle agrees with the unique autonomous diffusion (DU2) killed at an endpoint. If that clock stops while the angle is still interior, continue the autonomous diffusion with the appended Brownian increments. Let $\tau_\partial$ be the endpoint-hitting lifetime of this diffusion.

We establish an exponential bound for its lifetime uniformly in its initial angle. Put $p = 2 - 8/\kappa < 1$. For $0 \le \theta \le \pi/2$, define

<a id="id-DU3"></a>
$$ u(\theta) = \frac{8}{\kappa} \int_0^\theta (\sin v)^{-p} \left( \int_v^{\pi/2} (\sin y)^p\, dy \right) dv, \qquad u(\pi - \theta) = u(\theta). \tag{DU3} $$

The integral is finite. Near zero, its integrand is $O(v^{-p})$ if $p > -1$, $O(v\log(1/v))$ if $p = -1$, and $O(v)$ if $p < -1$. Each of these is integrable because $p < 1$. Thus $u$ is nonnegative and continuous on $[0, \pi]$, vanishes at its endpoints, and has a finite positive maximum $M_\kappa = u(\pi/2)$. Its derivative at $\pi/2$ is zero, so the reflection in (DU3) makes it twice continuously differentiable throughout $(0, \pi)$. Direct differentiation gives

<a id="id-DU4"></a>
$$ u'' + p\cot\theta\, u' = -\frac{8}{\kappa}, \qquad \left( \frac{\kappa}{8}\frac{d^2}{d\theta^2} + \frac{\kappa - 4}{4}\cot\theta \frac{d}{d\theta} \right) u = -1. \tag{DU4} $$

Stop first on exiting $(\epsilon, \pi - \epsilon)$ and at a deterministic time $T$. Itô's formula, with bounded coefficients on this compact interval, gives

$$ \mathbb{E}_\theta[T \wedge \tau_\epsilon] = u(\theta) - \mathbb{E}_\theta[u(\theta_{T\wedge\tau_\epsilon})] \le M_\kappa. $$

Send $T \to \infty$ and then $\epsilon \downarrow 0$. Monotone convergence proves $\sup_\theta \mathbb{E}_\theta \tau_\partial \le M_\kappa$. For completeness, the finite increasing limit of the exit times really is an endpoint hit: an interior accumulation point would allow local Lipschitz continuation, while oscillation between neighborhoods of the two endpoints would require infinitely many crossings of a fixed middle interval in finite time. The drift is bounded on that interval, and Brownian uniform continuity excludes those crossings. The limit is therefore one endpoint.

<!-- page 88 -->
Markov's inequality and the strong Markov property yield

<a id="id-DU5"></a>
$$ \sup_{\theta\in(0,\pi)} \mathbb{P}_\theta[\tau_\partial > s] \le 2\exp\left( -\frac{\log 2}{2M_\kappa} s \right) \le 2e^{-\beta_\kappa s}, \qquad \beta_\kappa = \min\left\{ 1, \frac{\log 2}{2M_\kappa} \right\} > 0. \tag{DU5} $$

One applies the first inequality at successive blocks of length $2M_\kappa$; the prefactor 2 covers the remaining fraction of a block. All the stopping and Markov arguments here concern the one-dimensional diffusion before killing.

Koebe's distance estimate gives $\Upsilon_t \le 2\operatorname{dist}(z, \partial D_t)$. We explain why eventual approach by the trace is detected before $z$ is swallowed, even when the trace is nonsimple. Suppose $0 < r < \operatorname{Im} z/4$. If $z$ is swallowed at a finite time $\sigma$ without being hit and the closed prefix has distance greater than $r$ from $z$, then the connected disk $B(z, r)$ is trace-free and lies wholly in the bounded complementary component containing $z$. A future Loewner trace cannot enter an open component already disconnected from the active domain. In fact every point of $B(z, r)$ is thereafter excluded. Thus an eventual approach to distance less than $r$ either occurs before swallowing, or the trace at the swallowing time already has distance at most $r$ from $z$. In the latter case, continuity of the finite prefix and of its increasing compact initial ranges gives pre-swallow approaches to distance $r + \epsilon$ for every $\epsilon > 0$. If $z$ itself is hit, the same statement holds for arbitrarily small positive radii. Consequently the approach event requires the angle diffusion to survive up to every clock value strictly below $\log(\operatorname{Im} z/(2r))$. Applying (DU5), and then taking the limit in that clock value, proves

<a id="id-DU6"></a>
$$ \mathbb{P}[\operatorname{dist}(z, \gamma) < r] \le C_\kappa \left( \frac{r}{\operatorname{Im} z} \right)^{\beta_\kappa}, \qquad 0 < r < \tfrac{1}{4}\operatorname{Im} z. \tag{DU6} $$

Increasing the constant covers the closed-radius versions needed for square covers.

For $K \Subset \mathbb{H}$, the relevant dyadic-square centers have a common positive lower bound on their heights, and there are $O_K(\delta^{-2})$ such squares. A square visited by the trace has its center at distance at most $2\delta$ from it. Therefore

$$ \mathbb{E}N_\delta \le C_K \delta^{-2+\beta_\kappa}, \qquad \mathbb{P}[N_\delta > \delta^{-2+\beta_\kappa/2}] \le C_K \delta^{\beta_\kappa/2}. $$

The latter bound is summable over dyadic scales. Borel–Cantelli proves the assertion with $d_\kappa = 2 - \beta_\kappa/2 < 2$. A countable compact exhaustion gives the stated interior dimension bound. $\square$

**Aux. lemma 15** (Interior dimension for continuous finite-force traces)**.** *Let $0 < \kappa < 8$, and suppose an integrated finite-force $\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa(\underline{\rho})$ has an available continuous trace before its continuation threshold. **Aux. lemma 16** (A coupling that retains the swallowed components)**.** *Suppose that an ordered integrated $\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa(\underline{\rho})$ evolution is already known to be generated by a continuous trace $\eta$, before its continuation threshold $T_*$. Put*

$$ A_t = \eta([0, t]) \cap \mathbf{H}, \qquad \Omega_t = \mathbf{H} \setminus A_t. $$

*Here $A_t$ is relatively closed in $\mathbf{H}$, and* all *components of $\Omega_t$ are retained. The coupling in Theorem 1.1 can be constructed so that, for every stopping time $\tau < T_*$, its conditional law given the entire stopped driving history is*

$$ \mathcal{L}(h \mid \mathcal{F}_\tau) = \mathcal{L}(M_\tau + h^{\Omega_\tau} \mid \mathcal{F}_\tau). $$

*The equality is an equality of laws of distributions on $\mathbf{H}$. The distribution $M_\tau$ is $\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable and harmonic in $\Omega_\tau$; conditionally on $\mathcal{F}_\tau$, $h^{\Omega_\tau}$ is a fresh zero-boundary GFF on $\Omega_\tau$, extended by zero. In the unbounded component, $M_\tau$ agrees with $\mathfrak{h}_\tau$ in Theorem 1.1. In a component cut off earlier, its harmonic mean is the limit at the disconnection time and is thereafter frozen. The residual zero-boundary GFF in that component is independent of subsequent driving, conditionally on the history at disconnection. In particular, the stopped range, together with its driving history, is a local exploration.*

<a id="aux-16-proof"></a>
*Proof.* Write $\mathcal{G}_t$ for the Green kernel of $\Omega_t$, extended by zero outside that open set and between distinct components. This differs from the kernel $G_t$ used earlier: $\mathcal{G}_t$ retains the kernels of swallowed pockets. Set

$$ k_t(z) = \begin{cases} 2\operatorname{Im}(1/f_t(z)), & z \in \mathbf{H} \setminus K_t, \\ 0, & z \notin \mathbf{H} \setminus K_t. \end{cases} $$

The convention at swallowing times does not affect the time integrals. Notice that $k_t \le 0$.

*The Green identity across a disconnection.* The ordinary Loewner calculation (3.16), until either test point is swallowed, extends to

$$ \mathcal{G}_t(z, w) = G_{\mathbf{H}}(z, w) - \int_0^t k_s(z) k_s(w)\, ds, \qquad z \ne w. \tag{3.23} $$

<!-- page 91 -->
Here and below a stopped version is used if $T_* < \infty$. We explain the extension. A later tip is in the closure of the unbounded component of every earlier hull. It therefore cannot enter an open component already disconnected from infinity. Such a component and its Green kernel remain unchanged. Just before a disconnection, monotone Green-kernel convergence gives the kernel of the resulting component when both points lie in it, and zero when they lie in different components. If a point is actually hit by the trace, the Green function with a different fixed pole tends to zero. These convergence statements can also be seen using killed planar Brownian motion: the increasing compact initial trace segments exhaust the segment at the limiting time, except for its single terminal point, and a single point is polar. Boundary regularity at a point of a nontrivial connected planar boundary gives the assertion for a point hit by the trace. Thus there is no missing downward jump in $\mathcal{G}_t$ when a pocket is closed. Apply (3.16) before the first disconnection of either point and freeze the resulting kernel afterward; this proves (3.23). The same argument, from the right and from the left, gives continuity of the Green pairings against compactly supported smooth tests.

For real $\phi, \psi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbf{H})$, write

$$ \mathcal{E}_t(\phi, \psi) = \iint \phi(z) \mathcal{G}_t(z, w) \psi(w)\, dz\, dw. $$

For $\phi \ge 0$, Tonelli's theorem, the sign of $k$, and (3.23) give

$$ \int_0^t \left( \int \phi(z) k_s(z)\, dz \right)^2 ds = \mathcal{E}_0(\phi, \phi) - \mathcal{E}_t(\phi, \phi) \le \mathcal{E}_0(\phi, \phi). $$

In particular the spatial integral is finite for almost every time. The same integrability for a signed smooth test follows by domination by a nonnegative smooth compactly supported majorant of $|\phi|$. Fubini and polarization then give the corresponding bilinear identity.

*A distribution-valued mean martingale.* Define

$$ M_t(\phi) = (\mathfrak{h}_0, \phi) + \int_0^t \left( \int \phi(z) k_s(z)\, dz \right) dB_s. $$

It is a square-integrable continuous martingale on every bounded localization, and

$$ [M(\phi), M(\psi)]_t = \mathcal{E}_0(\phi, \psi) - \mathcal{E}_t(\phi, \psi). \tag{3.24} $$

These random linear functionals have simultaneous distribution-valued versions. Here is one useful justification. On each compact subset of $\mathbf{H}$, the integral operator with kernel $G_{\mathbf{H}}$ is bounded on $L^2$: its logarithmic diagonal singularity is uniformly integrable there. Consequently

$$ \mathbb{E}|M_t(\phi) - (\mathfrak{h}_0, \phi)|^2 \le c_K \|\phi\|^2_{L^2} $$

<!-- page 92 -->
for tests supported in a fixed compact neighborhood $K$. Apply this estimate and Doob's inequality to a smooth cutoff times the Fourier basis of a containing rectangle. Summing with Sobolev weights $(1 + |n|^2)^{-s}$, $s > 1$, gives convergence of the resulting martingale series in $L^2$ of the supremum norm with values in $H^{-s}$. A countable exhaustion therefore yields a continuous distribution-valued version $M_t$.

For a deterministic rational ball, stop at its first intersection with the trace. Before that stop and before the ball is disconnected, $k_s$ is harmonic on it; after disconnection $k_s$ is identically zero there. Testing this stopped stochastic integral against $\Delta\psi$ supported in the ball shows that $M_t(\Delta\psi) = 0$ as long as the ball is avoided. Thus no independence is being inferred by conditioning a stochastic integral on a future avoidance event. Use a countable collection of balls and tests and the distributional mean-value characterization of harmonic functions. It follows that $M_t$ is harmonic on every component of $\Omega_t$. Before a compact test support is swallowed, stochastic Fubini and (3.15) identify $M_t$ there with $\mathfrak{h}_t$. For a swallowed component the distributional limit from before disconnection is harmonic and agrees with $M_t$ thereafter. Distributional convergence of harmonic functions implies locally uniform convergence of every derivative, so this is also an ordinary harmonic-function limit on compact subsets of the pocket.

*Consistency of the conditional Gaussian laws.* For every real test $\phi$, Itô's formula and (3.24) show that

$$ Z_t(\phi) = \exp\big( iM_t(\phi) - \tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}_t(\phi, \phi) \big) $$

is a complex local martingale. Its modulus is at most one, because $\mathcal{E}_t$ is a nonnegative covariance form. Hence it is a true bounded martingale and optional sampling applies. Let $Q_t$ be the Gaussian probability kernel on distributions with mean $M_t$ and covariance $\mathcal{G}_t$; equivalently it is the law of $M_t$ plus an independent, zero-extended GFF in $\Omega_t$. Existence and measurability of this kernel follow either from that GFF construction, or from its characteristic functions on a countable dense test family and the same local Sobolev bounds. For stopping times $S \le T < T_*$,

$$ \mathbb{E}\left[ \int e^{i(u,\phi)} Q_T(du) \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_S \right] = \int e^{i(u,\phi)} Q_S(du). $$

Applying the identity to linear combinations of tests proves consistency of the entire Gaussian kernels, not just of individual marginals.

Use the bounded announcing sequence $T_n \uparrow T_*$ introduced above. The joint laws of a history stopped at $T_n$ and a field sampled from $Q_{T_n}$ are projectively consistent. Represent each stopped sigma-algebra by the stopped canonical path and a countable family generating $\mathcal{F}_{T_n}$ modulo null sets, retaining the families for earlier indices as well. Such families exist because the canonical path law is a standard probability space. The recorded histories and the distribution space are standard Borel spaces. Their countable projective extension constructs one field $h$ jointly with the entire driving history before $T_*$. The driving marginal remains the prescribed SLE law, and $Q_0$ is the GFF with mean $\mathfrak{h}_0$. Optional sampling and localization give $\mathcal{L}(h \mid \mathcal{F}_\tau) = Q_\tau$ for every $\tau < T_*$.

The continuity of $M$ and of the Green pairings also makes this statement valid for the usual completed, right-continuous filtration.

The displayed conditional law is the decorated version of the two-step local-set construction in Lemma 3.6. For clarity, on the event that a deterministic open set $U$ avoids $A_\tau$, $M_\tau$ is harmonic on $U$. Conditional on the stopped history, the projection of $h^{\Omega_\tau}$ onto $H(U)$ is a zero-boundary GFF on $U$, independent of its orthogonal complement. Its law does not depend on that history. The marginal law $Q_0$ also gives the unconditional GFF Markov independence of $h_U$ and $h_{U^c}$. Combining this with the conditional factorization on the avoidance event shows that, conditionally on $h_{U^c}$, the avoidance event and the stopped history retained on that event are independent of $h_U$. This is the history-sensitive locality used in Lemma 6.2.

Forgetting the finer information gives a two-step construction from $(K_\tau, H_\tau^K)$ and this remaining GFF. Hence $K_\tau$ is local. The covariance between $C$ and every other component is zero. The conditional Gaussian kernels therefore factor, with a factor on $C$ already measurable at $S$. Components may be selected by rational points, so a countable argument establishes the statement simultaneously for all pockets. $\square$

**Aux. lemma 17** (The full range-kernel change of mean)**.** *Use the retained-range coupling of* ***Aux.*** *lemma 16, with initial harmonic mean $m$ and driving Brownian motion $B$. Write $\Omega_t = \mathbb{H} \setminus \eta([0, t])$, retaining every component, and write $P_t$ for Dirichlet-space projection onto $H(\Omega_t)$. Let $f \in H(\mathbb{H})$ be deterministic and let*

$$ Z = \exp\{ (h - m, f)_\nabla - \tfrac{1}{2}\|f\|_\nabla^2 \} $$

*be its Cameron–Martin density. The pairing is the isonormal Gaussian pairing, defined by Dirichlet-space approximation. There is a predictable real process $a^f$ such that*

<a id="id-CM1"></a>
$$ \begin{aligned} \int_0^t (a_s^f)^2\, ds &= \|f\|_\nabla^2 - \|P_t f\|_\nabla^2, \\ \mathbb{E}[Z \mid \mathcal{F}_t] &= \exp\left\{ \int_0^t a_s^f\, dB_s - \frac{1}{2}\int_0^t (a_s^f)^2\, ds \right\}. \end{aligned} \tag{CM1} $$

<!-- page 94 -->
*Under the measure with density $Z$, put $B_t^f = B_t - \int_0^t a_s^f\, ds$. Then $B^f$ is Brownian motion in the retained driving filtration, and the conditional law of the entire field, as a distribution, is*

<a id="id-CM2"></a>
$$ \mathcal{L}_f(h \mid \mathcal{F}_t) = \mathcal{L}_f(M_t + P_t f + h^{\Omega_t} \mid \mathcal{F}_t). \tag{CM2} $$

*For every smooth compactly supported test $\phi$,*

<a id="id-CM3"></a>
$$ (M_t + P_t f, \phi) = (m + f, \phi) + \int_0^t k_s(\phi)\, dB_s^f, \qquad k_s(\phi) = \int \phi(z) k_s(z)\, dz, \tag{CM3} $$

*with the same $k$ as in the retained-range construction. These identities hold at every pre-threshold time. For increasing retained stopping times $T_n \uparrow T$, their limiting form is read on $\mathcal{F}_{T-} := \sigma(\bigcup_n \mathcal{F}_{T_n})$, with*

$$ P_{T-} f := \lim_{n\to\infty} P_{T_n} f \quad \text{in Dirichlet norm.} $$

*This is projection onto $\bigcap_n H(\Omega_{T_n})$. The limiting mean and covariance are the corresponding conditional-kernel limits; no closed Euclidean terminal range is being postulated. If the trace has a continuous extension to $T$,* ***Aux.*** *lemma 9 identifies this covariance with the Green covariance of the complement of the actual terminal range, retaining all its components. In particular (CM1) has a strictly positive terminal density on the full pre-threshold history, whether or not a terminal trace limit has been established.*

<a id="aux-17-proof"></a>
*Proof.* First suppose $f \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{H})$ and put $v_f = -\Delta f/(2\pi)$. The Dirichlet normalization in this paper gives $G_{\mathbb{H}} v_f = f$ and $G_{\Omega_t} v_f = P_t f$. Define $a_t^f = k_t(v_f)$. The retained Green identity, integrated against $v_f$ twice, gives

<a id="id-CM4"></a>
$$ \int_0^t (a_s^f)^2\, ds = (v_f, G_{\mathbb{H}} v_f) - (v_f, G_{\Omega_t} v_f) = \|f\|_\nabla^2 - \|P_t f\|_\nabla^2. \tag{CM4} $$

Here the last equality uses orthogonal projection: $(v_f, P_t f) = (f, P_t f)_\nabla = \|P_t f\|_\nabla^2$. The same Green identity integrated against $\phi$ and $v_f$ gives the identity of finite-variation processes

<a id="id-CM5"></a>
$$ (P_t f, \phi) = (f, \phi) - \int_0^t k_s(\phi) a_s^f\, ds. \tag{CM5} $$

The integral is absolutely convergent, by Cauchy–Schwarz and the two nonnegative Green-energy bounds.

For general $f \in H(\mathbb{H})$ choose $f_n \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{H})$ converging in Dirichlet norm. Equation (CM4), applied to $f_n - f_j$, shows pathwise that

$$ \int_0^t |a_s^{f_n} - a_s^{f_j}|^2\, ds \le \|f_n - f_j\|_\nabla^2. $$

<!-- page 95 -->
It defines a predictable limit $a^f$, consistently on every bounded localization. The same bound holds over the entire lifetime by monotone convergence. Orthogonal projections are contractions, so (CM4) passes to the limit. Since

$$ |(f_n - f, \phi)| \le \|f_n - f\|_\nabla\, (\phi, G_{\mathbb{H}}\phi)^{1/2}, $$

and the same estimate holds for their projected differences, (CM5) also passes to the limit, uniformly in $t$. No smoothness of the trace, and no zero-area assumption on its range, has entered these identities.

The mean martingale in the range construction gives

$$ (M_t - m, f)_\nabla = \int_0^t a_s^f\, dB_s, $$

where both sides are first defined for $f_n$ and then by their $L^2$ limits. Conditionally on $\mathcal{F}_t$, the remaining Gaussian contribution to $(h - m, f)_\nabla$ has variance $\|P_t f\|_\nabla^2$. Its exponential moment therefore gives (CM1). Gaussian exponential tilting in that conditional law changes its mean by $P_t f$ and leaves its covariance unchanged. This proves (CM2) for all finite collections of test functions and hence for the distribution-valued field.

The quadratic variation of the stochastic integral in (CM1) is bounded by the deterministic constant $\|f\|_\nabla^2$. The exponential is consequently uniformly integrable: for $p > 1$, its $p$th moment is bounded by $\exp\{p(p-1)\|f\|_\nabla^2/2\}$, by the exponential martingale formula and bounded-bracket localization. Its stochastic integral converges to a finite real value in $L^2$ and almost surely. Thus its terminal value is strictly positive. Girsanov's theorem on each bounded localization, followed by this uniformly integrable limit, proves the Brownian assertion on the whole retained history.

Finally add (CM5) to $(M_t, \phi) = (m, \phi) + \int_0^t k_s(\phi)\, dB_s$ and substitute $dB = dB^f + a^f\, dt$. The finite-variation terms cancel exactly, giving (CM3). The same energy estimates and martingale convergence pass these identities to the stated increasing-history limit. For completeness, the projection limit used there exists strongly: if $n \ge j$, then $H(\Omega_{T_n}) \subseteq H(\Omega_{T_j})$, and

$$ \|P_{T_j} f - P_{T_n} f\|_\nabla^2 = \|P_{T_j} f\|_\nabla^2 - \|P_{T_n} f\|_\nabla^2. $$

The decreasing sequence of squared norms has a limit. The projections are therefore Cauchy. Their limit belongs to every one of the closed subspaces, and its difference from $f$ is orthogonal to their intersection, which proves the asserted projection description. The same argument identifies the limiting Green pairings first for smooth Dirichlet sources and then by approximation. If the trace extends continuously to the finite terminal time, the announced-range lemma identifies these limits with its actual closed range; one first uses a fixed bounded conformal coordinate if necessary. Without that endpoint hypothesis only the preterminal kernel limit is used. If the lifetime is finite, Brownian motions may be completed independently after it; no assertion about the SLE continuation beyond its threshold is made. $\square$
