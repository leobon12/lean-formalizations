Here is the precise renewal argument needed at this endpoint. Fix $0 < a < b < 1$ and use consecutive cycles $a \to b \to a$. Scale-speed Green formulas give, for every nonnegative function $f$, expected occupation in one complete cycle equal to

$$ (s(b) - s(a)) \int_0^1 f(y) m(y)\, dy. $$

Indeed, the killed Green kernel for $a \to b$, reflected at $0$, is $(s(b) - s(a \vee y)) m(y)$ for $y < b$. The kernel for $b \to a$, with the inaccessible recurrent endpoint $1$, is $(s(b \wedge y) - s(a)) m(y)$ for $y > a$. Their sum is $(s(b) - s(a)) m(y)$ throughout $(0, 1)$. The cycle lengths $L_n$ are finite almost surely, independent and identically distributed, with infinite mean; their $g$-rewards $R_n$ have finite mean. The truncated strong law gives $(L_1 + \cdots + L_n)/n \to \infty$, so the number $N(u)$ of completed cycles satisfies $N(u)/u \to 0$. The reward strong law gives $(R_1 + \cdots + R_n)/n \to \mathbb{E} R_1$. Also $R_n/n \to 0$ almost surely: for every $\epsilon > 0$, integrability gives $\sum_n \mathbb{P}(R_n > \epsilon n) < \infty$, and Borel–Cantelli applies. The incomplete-cycle reward is at most $R_{N(u)+1}$, whose ratio to $u$ tends to zero by these two limits. The initial segment before the first visit to $a$ is finite and has finite reward almost surely. Hence

$$ \frac{1}{u} \int_0^u \frac{1 - U_v}{U_v}\, dv \longrightarrow 0, \qquad \frac{\log X_u}{u} \longrightarrow d - c = -2. $$

In each case, $X_u$ decays exponentially and $F(u) = \int_0^u U_v^{-1} dv = O(u)$ almost surely. If $X_u \le C e^{-\epsilon u}$ after a finite random time, integration by parts gives $\int e^{-\epsilon u} dF(u) < \infty$. Local reciprocal integrability treats the initial finite interval. Therefore

$$ \tau = \int_0^\infty X_u^2 du < \infty, \qquad \int_0^\infty \frac{X_u}{U_u} du < \infty. $$

Define $t(u) = \int_0^u X_v^2 dv$, $V_{t(u)} = 2 \int_0^u X_v/U_v\, dv$, $R_{t(u)} = U_u X_u$, and $W_{t(u)} = V_{t(u)} - R_{t(u)}$. The time-changed martingale in these equations is Brownian in physical time. Direct substitution gives the integrated one-seed equations and $g_{t(u)}(1) = W_{t(u)} + X_u$. Their uniqueness identifies this construction with the canonical evolution until the passive point is swallowed. At $t \uparrow \tau$, $V$ and $W$ have finite limits and $X \to 0$. Thus the passive point is swallowed at the finite time $\tau$. $\square$

No trace theorem is imported in LP2. Its one-dimensional endpoint construction is the dimension greater than one instantaneous Bessel convention, and the displayed Green kernels specify the only recurrent-diffusion occupation input.

### 6.3 Terminal reduction from finite continuity

<a id="id-LP3"></a>
**Lemma LP3** (ordinary-SLE steering input). Let $k > 4$, let $a < w < b$, and let $I = [p_-, p_+]$ be a compact interval with $a < p_- < w < p_+ < b$. Ordinary chordal $\mathrm{SLE}_k$, started at $w$, has positive probability of swallowing both endpoints of $I$ at a finite time before swallowing either outer point $a, b$.

<a id="id-LP3-proof"></a>
*Proof.* First consider swallowing one prescribed inner point $p$ while both outer points remain unswallowed. Choose a small $\varepsilon > 0$. In a sufficiently short deterministic time, a smooth driver can move from its current position toward $p$ until the image of $p$ is at distance between $\varepsilon/2$ and $2\varepsilon$ from the driver, keeping both outer gaps bounded below by a fixed $\delta > 0$. For example, if $p > w$, move the driver monotonically from $w$ to $p - \varepsilon$ in time $T \le \varepsilon^2/16$; choose $\varepsilon < (p - w)/2$. The target characteristic is nondecreasing and its gap stays at least $\varepsilon$, so its total displacement is at most $2T/\varepsilon \le \varepsilon/8$. The two outer characteristics move outward while the driver stays in the fixed compact interval $[w, p]$. Reflection treats $p < w$. Continuous dependence of those real characteristics on the driver and Brownian full support give a positive-probability neighborhood of this final configuration.

<!-- page 482 -->
The ordinary-SLE gap at the inner point is a Bessel process of dimension $1 + 4/k \in (1, 2)$ until it hits zero. Its first zero is almost surely finite. Couple all initial gaps $y \in [\varepsilon/2, 2\varepsilon]$ with the same Brownian motion. One-dimensional comparison up to the first zero gives $\tau_y \le \tau_{2\varepsilon}$. By Brownian scaling and the almost sure finiteness of $\tau_{2\varepsilon}$, for a sufficiently large deterministic $K$ the event

$$ \tau_{2\varepsilon} \le K \varepsilon^2, \qquad \sup_{0 \le t \le \tau_{2\varepsilon}} |\sqrt{k} B_t| \le K \varepsilon $$

has a fixed strictly positive probability independent of $\varepsilon$. It controls every initial gap in the indicated interval simultaneously. For $\varepsilon$ small relative to $\delta/K$, the two outer gaps cannot vanish: their real characteristics move outward and the driver remains inside their initial buffered interval. This proves the one-inner-point assertion.

Apply it first to $p_-$, and then, by the strong Markov property, to the surviving image of $p_+$ unless that point has already been swallowed. The two outer points remain protected and the second conditional success probability is strictly positive in every successful first-stage configuration. Integration gives the claimed positive probability. A countable union over deterministic time bounds and strictly positive outer-gap cutoffs yields the same event on at least one bounded-energy Girsanov localization. $\square$

**Proposition LP4** (both-accessible two-seed terminal theorem). Let $k > 4$ and $-2 < r_L, r_R < c$. Suppose the canonical two-seed driving law has a continuous trace on every finite capacity interval. Then this trace tends to infinity almost surely.

<a id="id-LP4-proof"></a>
*Proof.* First obtain a positive-probability finite own stopping time at which the physical leftmost and rightmost swallowed real points straddle $0$ strictly. After the first $D = 1$ level, wait for a right-seed collision using accessibility of the right Jacobi endpoint. The left force is then separated from the driver by $D > 0$. Map out, center, and divide by $D$. In these coordinates insert a passive point $\varepsilon > 0$. Lemma LP2 and scaling imply that its swallowing time and the driver oscillation up to that time are respectively $\varepsilon^2$ and $\varepsilon$ times finite random variables. Thus, for sufficiently small deterministic $\varepsilon$, it is swallowed before the buffered stop $1/64 \wedge \inf\{|W| \ge 1/4\}$ with positive probability. The separated left drift can be deleted through this stop by the bounded-energy change in LP1. Equivalence transfers that positive probability to the actual two-seed future. The inverse real boundary map sends this passive point to a physical real point strictly beyond the previous right swallowed extreme. Hence the new right extreme is positive.

Next wait for a left-seed collision and apply the reflected experiment. It has positive conditional probability of making the left swallowed extreme negative. The first extreme remains positive because swallowed hulls increase. Then wait for the next Jacobi midpoint $U = 1/2$. This is again a finite own stopping time. Call the resulting time $T$ and the positive-probability event just constructed $E_T$. Its defining successes are observed by time $T$.

On $E_T$, let $f_T$ be the inverse hydrodynamic map. Finite trace continuity gives its continuous extension to the spherical closed half-plane. If $0$ is already outside the closure of the active component, the future automatically avoids a neighborhood of $0$. Otherwise its preimage fiber

$$ F = f_T^{-1}(\{0\}) $$

is a compact subset of the open real interval $(V_T^L, V_T^R)$: the inverse values at the two endpoints are the strictly negative and strictly positive physical swallowed extremes, and the exterior real rays map to unswallowed exterior real rays. Also $W_T$ lies strictly between those two force coordinates. Choose, measurably using rational endpoints, an interval

$$ F \cup \{W_T\} \subset (p_-, p_+) \subset [p_-, p_+] \subset (V_T^L, V_T^R). $$

<!-- page 483 -->
Apply LP3 to ordinary SLE in this mapped-out half-plane, taking the two force coordinates as protected outer points. Let $\tau$ be the first time both inner endpoints have been swallowed. For a deterministic integer $m$ larger than the reciprocal initial outer gaps, define the genuine capped stopping time

$$ \tau_m = \tau \wedge m \wedge \inf\{t : \min(W_t - V_t^L, V_t^R - W_t) \le 1/m\}. $$

The positive success event in LP3 is the increasing union, up to harmless adjustment of strict cutoffs, of events $\{\tau < m,\ \inf_{[0,\tau]} \min(W - V^L, V^R - W) > 1/m\}$. At least one such event has positive probability. Both force drifts have bounded energy through $\tau_m$, so Girsanov gives equivalence of the entire capped stopped driving laws.

Apply Zhan's independent ordinary-SLE result [Zha10, Corollary 6.1] at this positive finite stopping time $\tau_m$, before restricting to its success event. It says simultaneously that every real point strictly between the swallowed real extremes has a relative upper-half-plane neighborhood inside the swallowed hull. On success, $\tau_m = \tau$ and this conclusion covers the compact fiber $F$. Both the success event and this stopped hull property transfer under the equivalent capped laws. No successful-future event has been used as if it were a stopping time. Every later relative trace lies in the closure of the remaining component and avoids an open neighborhood of $F$. By compactness and continuity of $f_T$ on the spherical closure, its physical image is bounded away from $0$. We have established positive probability that $0$ is absent from the terminal cluster set.

Let

$$ Q = \bigcap_{n \ge 1} \overline{\eta([n, \infty))}^{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}. $$

The event $0 \notin Q$ is a measurable dilation-invariant event of the full canonical driving law. Aux. lemma 128 therefore makes its probability one. The compact nonempty set $Q$ contains $\infty$: otherwise all sufficiently late trace pieces would be bounded, and the whole trace would generate a bounded hull of finite half-plane capacity, contrary to infinite capacity lifetime. Set

$$ Z = \inf\{|z| : z \in Q \cap \mathbb{C}\}, \qquad \inf \varnothing = \infty. $$

The absence of $0$ and compactness of $Q$ give $Z > 0$. Exact dilation invariance of the trace law gives $Z \overset{\text{law}}{=} aZ$ for every $a > 0$. A random variable in $(0, \infty]$ with this property equals $\infty$ almost surely: $\mathbb{P}(Z \le x)$ is constant over $x > 0$ and tends to zero as $x \downarrow 0$. Thus $Q = \{\infty\}$ and $\eta(t) \to \infty$. $\square$

### 6.4 Retargeting two marks on the same side

<a id="id-LP6"></a>
**Proposition LP6** (same-side two-mark reduction). Fix $k > 0$. Assume that every canonical two-seed law with both weights greater than $-2$ has a continuous trace on every finite interval and is transient. Assume the integrated target-change identity, used only before the old and new targets separate. Then every law with two same-side force groups has a continuous trace up to and including its finite continuation threshold and is transient on infinite lifetime. The first group may start at the seed if its weight is admissible. The two weights otherwise need not satisfy any inequality.

<a id="id-LP6-proof"></a>
*Proof.* Reflect so that the positions are $0 \le x_1 < x_2$, with $x_1 = 0^+$ allowed. Write the weights as $r_1, r_2$ and $S = r_1 + r_2$. If the initial seed group is inadmissible, the lifetime is zero and there is nothing to prove. Target the actual second mark using

$$ \psi(z) = \frac{z}{x_2 - z}, \qquad \psi^{-1}(w) = \frac{x_2 w}{1 + w}. $$

The transformed finite force points are the old target $-1$, with weight

$$ q = k - 6 - S, $$

and $\psi(x_1) \ge 0$, with weight $r_1$. There are exactly two groups, one on each side. By Aux. lemma 106, whose hypotheses are precisely the two-seed theorem assumed here and the independent one-force first-swallow input, this transformed evolution has a continuous trace through every finite continuation endpoint and is transient if its lifetime is infinite. This continues through all admissible collisions of its right group; no individual collision excursions are sewn together.

Use the target-change identification only until the first separation of the old target $-1$ from the new target at infinity, or an earlier threshold. The geometric force endpoint identities imply that the first swallowing of $-1$, if finite, has trace endpoint $u \le -1$. If $u < -1$, its inverse image is a finite real point strictly larger than $x_2$. The inverse image of the whole preceding trace is compact and continuous, because this stopped trace has not visited the pole $-1$. It therefore has finite original capacity. At its endpoint both original marks have been swallowed and amalgamate into one group of weight $S$. If $S \le -2$, this is the original threshold; otherwise the one-seed theorem supplies the complete continuous transient continuation. The inverse map of the finite continuous original prefix joins that continuation at its tip and fixes infinity.

If instead the first swallowed-old-target endpoint is $u = -1$, continuity of the transformed trace gives convergence of the inverse trace to infinity. There is no earlier original finite threshold on this branch: any such threshold before target separation would have been preserved by the target change. The integrated original nonexplosion theorem excludes an unbounded trace at finite original capacity, using the deterministic Loewner hull bound. Thus this branch has infinite original lifetime and is transient.

An earlier transformed continuation threshold can only occur at the right group, with $r_1 \le -2$. Its endpoint belongs to $[\psi(x_1), \infty)$ on the real boundary. The inverse endpoint is in $[x_1, x_2)$, and the inverse stopped trace is compact and continuous, so this is exactly the original first-group threshold at finite capacity. An inadmissible left group can trigger a threshold only when $-1$ is swallowed, which was already treated.

Finally suppose there is no finite threshold and $-1$ is never swallowed. The transformed trace has infinite lifetime and tends to infinity. Its completed spherical range is compact and does not contain $-1$: finite-time continuity excludes a missing finite-time visit, and its terminal limit is infinity. Its inverse image is consequently a bounded continuous completed trace ending at $x_2$, with finite original capacity. Both original groups merge there. Apply the same threshold or admissible one-seed continuation argument according to $S \le -2$ or $S > -2$.

These alternatives exhaust the complete transformed law before target separation. Every time change is the Loewner capacity clock on that pre-separation segment. Each finite original joining time is the first two-group merger or continuation threshold, hence is an own driving stopping time; Aux. lemma 3 gives its integrated coordinate limits even if it arose from infinite transformed capacity. The continuation after a finite original merger is identified by the original integrated strong Markov property, not by continuing the transformed process toward a different target. This proves the proposition. $\square$

### 6.5 Closing the low-total many-force induction

**Lemma LP8** (the singleton extension of Aux. lemma 113). Fix $k > 0$, $N \ge 3$, and assume the complete continuity and transience theorem for every configuration with at most $N - 1$ finite force groups, together with the complete two-seed theorem. Besides the three alternatives stated in Aux. lemma 113, its conclusion holds if one side is a single admissible group and the total weight of the other side exceeds $d = k/2 - 4$.

*Proof.* Suppose the right side is the singleton, of weight $r > -2$, and the left total is $s > d$. The strengthened experiment in Aux. lemma 111 requires exactly these inequalities, not the stronger inequality $r > d$. Indeed, if $d < s < c$, its reflected dipolar reference swallows the whole left side at a finite continuous endpoint while avoiding the right singleton. If $s \ge c$, its reference is the full opposite-side two-mark law with weights $(s; r)$. The assumed two-seed theorem and Aux. lemma 106 supply its full continuity and transience. The left reference mark cannot collide, by the localized nonhitting Bessel argument; the right singleton may have all its admissible collisions. Thus the completed reference trace avoids the left marked interval by a positive random distance. Choosing a deterministic smaller clearance with positive probability and applying the whole-history buffered Gaussian comparison gives the uniformly positive singleton trial of Aux. lemma 111.

Now repeat Aux. lemma 113's adapted midpoint trials with this experiment. There are at least two groups on the left because $N \ge 3$. A finite successful left swallow therefore decreases the number of groups, unless it is the continuation threshold. Swallowing the admissible singleton alone is not a success and does not end the comparison. An infinite successful reference branch is transient. The same buffer half-rectangle gives no finite accumulation of failed trials. Every infinite one-sided phase has finite angular clock by Aux. lemma 101, so deleting its opposite group on bounded energy localizations transfers the smaller-count transience theorem on that entire phase. If there are infinitely many midpoint trials, the uniformly positive success probability excludes failure of all of them. The finite-time continuity and finite-threshold endpoint assertion are already Aux. lemma 103. These are all parts of Aux. lemma 113's proof, with the strengthened singleton experiment replacing its two-total experiment. Reflection proves the other orientation. $\square$

**Lemma LP9** (retargeting with a strict group-count decrease). Suppose a configuration has $N$ designated finite force groups and at least two groups on its right. In right-side order let their positions be $x_1 < \cdots < x_n$ and their weights have partial sums $S_j = \sum_{i=1}^{j} r_i$. A first position $0^+$ is allowed. For an index $j \ge 2$, target the actual mark $x_j$ by $\psi(z) = z/(x_j - z)$. The transformed word has at most $N$ finite groups. Its right total is $S_{j-1}$ and its left total is

$$ k - 6 - S_j. \tag{LP9.1} $$

If the complete theorem is available for that transformed word and for all words with at most $N - 1$ groups, then it holds for the original word.

<a id="id-LP9-proof"></a>
*Proof.* For counting, a designated finite force group may have weight zero. Auxiliary passive characteristics, such as trial barriers, are deterministically recovered from the driver and do not enter this induction count. The target mark is removed from the finite list and the old target becomes the finite mark $-1$, of weight $k - 6$ minus the original total weight. Retaining this mark even if its weight is zero makes the count exactly $N$. The right transformed marks are exactly the original right marks preceding $x_j$. The left list consists, in order away from the seed, of all original left marks, the old target $-1$, and the original right marks after $x_j$ in reversed order. Adding their weights proves (LP9.1).

Use the complete transformed law until the earliest of: a merger of at least two designated finite groups on the same side; a continuation threshold; or swallowing the old target $-1$. At simultaneous events give old-target swallowing priority, then a continuation threshold, then an admissible merger. Admissible collisions involving just one group do not stop this rule. Before the old target is swallowed, every merger involves only original groups on the same side, since all transformed groups beyond $-1$ are protected by that unswallowed mark. Such a merger decreases the original group count. An earlier threshold has exactly the same cumulative original weight, because the changed old-target weight has not participated. The continuous finite transformed prefix avoids $-1$ and hence pulls back to a bounded continuous original prefix of finite capacity. Apply the original strong Markov property and the smaller-count theorem at an admissible merger.

If the old target is swallowed first, the geometric endpoint is a real $u \le -1$. A merger or threshold involving that old target is treated by this branch as well. If $u < -1$, then $\psi^{-1}(u) > x_j$: at least the first $j \ge 2$ original right groups are swallowed. Their amalgamation either is a threshold or decreases the original count. The inverse prefix is bounded and continuous, so the smaller-count theorem applies as above. If $u = -1$, the original trace tends to infinity; integrated nonexplosion and the deterministic Loewner hull bound imply that the original capacity lifetime is infinite, as in LP6.

**Theorem LP10** (complete large-parameter finite-force induction). Fix $k > 4$. Assume the full two-seed continuity and transience theorem for every admissible pair, the independent one-force first-swallow theorem, and the whole-history buffered comparisons of Aux. 108–111. Then every integrated finite-force law has a continuous trace up to and including its finite continuation threshold, and its trace tends to infinity on infinite lifetime. *Proof.* Amalgamate initial coincident groups on each side. An inadmissible group at the seed gives an immediate threshold. Count the remaining designated finite force groups, which may include a designated zero-weight group. Do not count auxiliary passive characteristics recovered from the driver; such characteristics may still define trial or stopping rules. With zero groups use ordinary SLE. With one group use Aux. lemma 99 through its first swallow and the one-seed theorem for every admissible continuation. For two opposite-side groups use Aux. lemma 106, and for two same-side groups use LP6.

These give the full theorem for at most two groups.

Suppose the full theorem holds for at most $N - 1$ groups, where $N \ge 3$. First establish the following good sectors for $N$ groups on both sides:

$$ s_L \le -2 \quad \text{or} \quad s_R \le -2 \quad \text{or} \quad (s_L > d \text{ and } s_R > d), \tag{LP10.1} $$

or one side is a singleton of weight greater than $-2$ and the other total exceeds $d$. Aux. lemma 113 proves (LP10.1) and LP8 proves the singleton extension. Both proofs use only the smaller-count induction hypothesis and the already established two-group bases.

Consider any remaining $N$-group configuration with groups on both sides. Both total side weights exceed $-2$, and at least one total is at most $d$. ### 7.1 A common-density, two-graph transfer principle

<a id="id-G1"></a>
**Lemma G1** (simultaneous transfer). Let $X, Y_1, Y_2$ take values in standard Borel spaces. Suppose that $P$ is their joint law and $Z > 0$ almost surely, with $E_P Z = 1$. Put $dQ = Z\, dP$. For each $i = 1, 2$, suppose the $(X, Y_i)$ marginal under $Q$ equals a reference marginal concentrated on

$$ Y_i = F_i(X) $$

for a measurable function $F_i$. Suppose the two reference marginals have the same $X$ marginal $\mu$. Then under $Q$ the entire triple has law

$$ (\mathrm{id}, F_1, F_2)_* \mu. $$

In particular, its law under $P$ is mutually absolutely continuous with this joint reference law. Thus every probability-one relation between $F_1(X)$ and $F_2(X)$ in the reference holds for $(Y_1, Y_2)$ under $P$.

<a id="id-G1-proof"></a>
*Proof.* For each $i$, marginal equality gives $Q[Y_i = F_i(X)] = 1$. Intersect these two events. On their intersection the triple is the displayed measurable function of $X$, whose marginal is $\mu$. This identifies its joint law. The measures $P$ and $Q$ have the same null sets because $Z$ is finite and strictly positive almost surely. Taking their pushforwards preserves mutual absolute continuity. No assertion of conditional independence of the paths is used. $\square$

The same assertion applies after conditioning on one retained initial datum. It suffices to prove it for almost every fixed datum. The final probability-one statement is measurable in the original field and histories, so it can then be integrated. The argument does not require a measurably chosen auxiliary family of full couplings indexed by every possible datum.

### 7.2 An explicit ordered reference with two distinct current tips

**Elementary filling fact.** Suppose two injective compact arcs in the closed upper half-plane have distinct real seeds $x_2 < x_1$ and meet only at their common final point. ### 7.9 Exact original marks and continuation thresholds

<a id="id-G11"></a>
**Lemma G11** (seed force and a separated mark through the marked-point threshold). Let $0 < \kappa < 4$, put $d_* = \kappa/2 - 4$, and consider the finite-force path with right weights $r$ at $0^+$ and $\rho$ at $1$, where

### 7.11 The terminal original-real branch of a matched gap

<a id="id-G14"></a>
**Lemma G14** (no new gap entry at a terminal real meeting). ### 8.5 A protected high tail after arbitrary admissible partial sums

<a id="id-GB5"></a>
**Lemma GB5** (general protected-tail estimate). Let $k > 0$, $c = k/2 - 2$, and let a one-sided integrated force evolution have right coordinates $0 \le V_1(0) \le \cdots \le V_N(0)$, with the first coordinate allowed to be a seed and coincident labels interpreted as one merged group. Write $S_i = \sum_{\ell \le i} \rho_\ell$. Suppose, for some $j \ge 2$, that

$$ S_i > -2 \quad (1 \le i < j), \qquad S_i \ge c \quad (j \le i \le N). $$

Assume $V_1(0) < V_j(0)$; one may always compress initially coincident groups before imposing the displayed conditions. Then $X_j = V_j - W$ never vanishes at a finite time. The same conclusion holds for $j = 1$ by the usual noncolliding partial-sum comparison. Inner admissible mergers are permitted: original labels are retained in the integrated equations, and no restart after each individual seed collision is used. An additional finite group on the opposite side does not change the finite-time conclusion, up to the original continuation threshold. Reflection gives the left-sided assertion.

<a id="id-GB5-proof"></a>
*Proof.* First omit the opposite group. Put $s = S_{j-1}$ and, before a putative first vanishing of $X_j$, set

$$ U_i = \frac{X_i}{X_j} \quad (i \le j), \qquad U_j = 1, \qquad Z = 1 - U_1 = \frac{V_j - V_1}{X_j}, \qquad du = \frac{dt}{X_j^2}. $$

All formulas below are first localized on bounded physical and new-clock intervals with $X_j > 0$. Zero sets of admissibly colliding inner groups have Lebesgue measure zero. The reciprocal terms are interpreted through the integrated equations; after a merger, equal labels give equal coordinates. Thus no difference of two undefined reciprocal values is assigned a pointwise meaning at a collision.

Define

$$ \begin{aligned} A &= X_j \sum_{i=j}^{N} \frac{\rho_i}{X_i} - (c - s), \\ E &= \sum_{i<j} \rho_i \left(\frac{1}{U_i} - 1\right) = \sum_{i<j} S_i \left(\frac{1}{U_i} - \frac{1}{U_{i+1}}\right), \\ H &= E + 2\left(\frac{1}{U_1} - 1\right) = \sum_{i<j} (S_i + 2) \left(\frac{1}{U_i} - \frac{1}{U_{i+1}}\right). \end{aligned} $$

Summation by parts in the outer group, whose partial sums relative to $s$ are at least $c - s$, gives

$$ 0 \le A \le C := \sum_{i=j}^{N} |\rho_i| + |c - s|. $$

The inner ordering and admissibility give, with $\epsilon = \min_{i<j}(S_i + 2) > 0$,

$$ H \ge \epsilon \left(\frac{1}{U_1} - 1\right) \ge 0, \qquad |E| \le \max_{i<j} |S_i| \left(\frac{1}{U_1} - 1\right). $$

For a Brownian motion $\beta$ in the new clock, Itô's formula yields

$$ \begin{aligned} d \log X_j &= \sqrt{k}\, d\beta + (E + A)\, du, \\ d \log Z &= -\sqrt{k}\, d\beta - (2 + A + H)\, du, \\ dU_1 &= \sqrt{k}(1 - U_1) d\beta + (1 - U_1)(-c + A + H)\, du, \\ d \log(V_j - V_1) &= -\frac{2}{U_1}\, du. \end{aligned} $$

These identities also follow by subtracting the integrated force coordinates and then localizing; they remain valid through any number of admissible inner collisions.

Suppose first that a putative vanishing of $X_j$ occurs at a finite new clock $u_*$. The martingale in the $U_1$ equation has quadratic variation at most $k u_*$. Its other terms, except the nonnegative integral $\int (1 - U_1) H\, du$, have finite limits. Boundedness of $U_1$ forces that nonnegative integral to be finite. Consequently

$$ \int_0^{u_*} \frac{(1 - U_1)^2}{U_1}\, du < \infty, \qquad \int_0^{u_*} \frac{du}{U_1} < \infty, $$

where the second implication uses $U_1^{-1} = (1 - U_1)^2/U_1 + 2 - U_1$. The last displayed differential identity then leaves $V_j - V_1$ strictly positive at the endpoint. This contradicts $0 \le V_j - V_1 \le X_j \to 0$.

On an infinite new-clock interval the second equation and the Brownian strong law give $Z(u) \le e^{-u}$ for all sufficiently large $u$. Thus $U_1 \to 1$ exponentially and $\int_0^\infty |E(u)|\, du < \infty$. Finite initial portions of this integral are covered by the preceding localized $U_1$ argument. Since $A \ge 0$, the first equation gives

$$ \log X_j(u) \ge \sqrt{k}\, \beta_u - C_0 $$

for a finite random $C_0$. Brownian motion has infinite limsup, so $X_j$ cannot tend to zero at this endpoint either. Finite physical endpoints have continuous finite coordinate limits: this follows either from the established integrated nonexplosion theorem or from the integrated identity in Aux. 115, since $S_N \ge c > -2$. The two clock alternatives therefore exclude every finite first vanishing.

Now restore finitely many opposite-side marks. At a putative positive finite right collision, the nearest left/right cross-gap is increasing and strictly positive. On a sufficiently late interval $X_j$ is small, so every

<!-- page 518 -->
opposite-side gap is bounded below by a positive constant. From a rational preceding time, stop at a bounded horizon and at the first violation of this lower bound. The opposite driving drift is bounded on that entire stopped interval, including the proposed right collision endpoint. Finite-horizon Girsanov deletion gives an equivalent one-sided law with the same right weights and passive stopping labels. The proved assertion excludes that endpoint. Countably many rational preceding times, positive lower bounds, and bounded horizons exhaust all putative finite collisions. $\square$

This section proves global ordered proper completion for all finite prefixes strictly before the continuation lifetimes of two constant-angle flows with ordered angles and ordered or common real seeds. **Convention W0** (a measurable completion predicate).
