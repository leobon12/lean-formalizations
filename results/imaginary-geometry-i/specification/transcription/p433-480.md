**The exact compactness statement needed for Euclidean transfer.** Let $f : \overline{\mathbb{D}} \to \overline{D}$ be the continuous boundary extension of a conformal map onto a bounded simply connected domain with locally connected boundary. Thus the active-chart shield for incoming prefixes proves the desired Euclidean conclusion only after every relevant fiber and every previously explored part of the full fan have been accounted for.

**Aux. lemma 136** (Simultaneous singleton active tip fibers). *Suppose the continuous Loewner trace and the local absolute-continuity hypotheses of Aux. lemma 135 hold through an increasing sequence of original-filtration stopping horizons $H_n < T_*$ which exhaust the available lifetime. $$ f_t^{-1}(\{\eta'(t)\}) = \{W_t\}. \tag{SF1} $$

*Proof.* We use the classical optional section theorem in its following precise form: an optional set whose projection on the sample space has positive probability contains the graph, on an event of positive probability, of an original stopping time. The filtration satisfies the usual conditions; this is a named general-process input, as in [RY99].

$$ f_t(W_t + v + i/k), \qquad 0 \leq t \leq H_n, $$

is adapted and continuous: the inverse Loewner maps depend continuously on time locally in the open half-plane, and their argument here stays at positive height. Extend it constantly after $H_n$. It is therefore optional. For every realized continuous trace, the active inverse maps extend continuously to the boundary at every time. Taking the vertical limit $k \to \infty$ proves that

$$ t \longmapsto f_t(W_t + v) $$

is optional through $H_n$ as well. Pointwise limits of optional measurable functions are optional. The tip process is continuous and adapted.

$$ I_m = [-m, -1/m] \cup [1/m, m], \qquad X^{n,m}_t = \inf_{v \in I_m \cap \mathbb{Q}} d_{\mathrm{sph}}\bigl(f_{t \wedge H_n}(W_{t \wedge H_n} + v), \eta'(t \wedge H_n)\bigr). \tag{SF2} $$

This is an optional process, being a countable infimum of such processes. For each fixed time the inverse map is continuous on the compact real set in (SF2). Assume the two selected open subarcs from $x_0$ to $y_0$ are disjoint and bound a Jordan domain $C$ contained in the upper half-plane, with $\alpha_L$ on its left and $\alpha_R$ on its right, when oriented from $x_0$ to $y_0$. Every other portion of either path is outside $C$. The terminal point $y_0$ may be $\infty$, and $x_0$ may be zero. All boundary values below use the incident prime end selected from $C$.

For every finite own prefix, use its hydrodynamically normalized mapping-out map $g_i$ and the analytic logarithm $L_i = \log g_i'$ tending to zero at infinity. Let $\phi : C \to \mathbb{H}$ send $x_0$ to zero and $y_0$ to infinity, with the left side sent to the negative half-line. Then one analytic logarithm $L_\phi = \log \phi'$ has the following property simultaneously on both sides. If a compact subarc $J$ of a selected open side has been passed by a finite own prefix on that path, ending no later than $y_0$, then on its incident collar

$$ \operatorname{Im}(L_\phi - L_i)(z) \longrightarrow 0 \quad \text{continuously as } z \text{ approaches } J. \tag{2} $$

The logarithm with this property is unique. Multiplying $\phi$ by a positive constant does not change the imaginary normalization.

**Untouched-interval identity used in the proof.** If $K$ is a bounded half-plane hull and $g_K$ its hydrodynamically normalized map, then the logarithm $\log g_K'$ tending to zero at infinity has imaginary part exactly zero on every real interval locally separated from $K$. For a hull generated by a simple crosscut this follows from

$$ \log g_t'(x) = -\int_0^t \frac{2\, ds}{(g_s(x) - W_s)^2}. $$

Finite unions of disjoint-base Jordan hulls follow by successive removal and the chain rule. A general hull is approximated from outside by filled finite unions of small disks, with boundaries in general position. Kernel convergence and reflection on a fixed untouched collar give convergence of maps and derivatives there. Continuing the logarithms from infinity along a fixed interior path preserves the zero lift. This is the independent conformal identity proved as Aux. 57 in the source manuscript. It does not use a conditional-mean theorem.

### 2.6 Staging the determination clause of Lemma 5.6

### 6.1 Extraction of one accessible seed

<a id="id-LP1"></a>
**Lemma LP1** (continuous one-seed extraction). Let $k > 0$, $-2 < \rho < c$, and $q > -2$. Suppose the canonical two-seed $\mathrm{SLE}_k(q; \rho)$ driving law has a continuous trace on every finite capacity interval. Then the canonical single-right-seed $\mathrm{SLE}_k(\rho)$ driving law has a continuous trace on every finite capacity interval,

<!-- page 479 -->
including intervals containing arbitrarily many admissible collisions. Reflection gives the corresponding left-seed assertion. This is a statement about driving and trace laws; it does not transfer a Gaussian field graph.

<a id="id-LP1-proof"></a>
**Proof.** Write $D = V^R - V^L$ and $U = (W - V^L)/D$. The integrated two-seed construction and its logarithmic separation clock identify $U$ as the Jacobi diffusion with parameters $2 + q$ and $2 + \rho$. Start after the first level $D = 1$. Since $2 + \rho < k/2$, the endpoint $1$ is accessible; recurrence of this compact Jacobi diffusion implies that its first subsequent visit to $1$ occurs at a finite logarithmic clock time. The reconstruction of physical time then makes this a finite own stopping time $T$. At $T$,

$$ W_T = V^R_T, \qquad V^L_T = W_T - D_T, \qquad 0 < D_T < \infty. $$

The original finite trace is continuous. The theorem that mapping out a finite prefix of a continuous Loewner trace preserves continuity of the future trace ([Yua22, Corollary 1.3], also used in Aux. lemma 129) therefore gives a continuous relative future. The strong Markov property of the integrated equations identifies its conditional driving law as the law started with driver $0$, left mark $-D_T$ of weight $q$, and right seed $0^+$ of weight $\rho$. Disintegration consequently gives at least one deterministic $a > 0$ for which this entire relative law is continuous on every finite interval with probability one. Exact dilation reduces to $a = 1$.

For this law set

$$ S = \frac{1}{64} \wedge \inf\{t \geq 0 : |W_t| \geq 1/4\}. $$

The left coordinate is nonincreasing and starts at $-1$. Thus $W_t - V^L_t \geq 3/4$ for $0 \leq t \leq S$. If

$$ a_t = \frac{q}{\sqrt{k}(W_t - V^L_t)} \mathbf{1}_{\{t \leq S\}}, $$

then $\int_0^S a_t^2 dt \leq q^2/(36k)$. The positive exponential density

$$ Z = \exp\left(-\int_0^S a_t\, dB_t - \frac{1}{2}\int_0^S a_t^2 dt\right) $$

defines an equivalent probability measure. Under that measure $B^Q = B + \int a_t dt$ is Brownian and the left-force drift cancels from the driving equation. The integrated right-seed equation is unchanged. Uniqueness of the stopped integrated equations identifies the resulting driver with the canonical single-right-seed law stopped at the same driver-measurable time $S$; the deleted left coordinate may be retained as passive data. Equivalence transfers continuity through the closed endpoint $S$.

For $R > 0$ the exact single-seed scaling law gives the same conclusion through

$$ S_R = \frac{R^2}{64} \wedge \inf\{t \geq 0 : |W_t| \geq R/4\}. $$

Take $R = 2^n$. These times increase to infinity, pathwise, because the integrated single-seed driver is continuous and nonexplosive on finite intervals. The trace tips obtained from the same driver agree on overlaps, so the countable union of these closed stopped traces is continuous on every finite interval. This proves the lemma without sewing individual Bessel excursions. $\square$

### 6.2 A driving-only passive-point swallowing calculation

<a id="id-LP2"></a>
**Lemma LP2** (finite swallowing of a passive point). Suppose $k > 4$ and $-2 < \rho < c$. In the canonical single-right-seed driving law, every fixed passive point $x > 0$ is swallowed at a finite time almost surely. This assertion uses only the integrated driving equations and does not presuppose the existence of a continuous trace.

<a id="id-LP2-proof"></a>
**Proof calculation.** It is enough to take $x = 1$. Before its swallowing, put $R = V - W$, $X = g_t(1) - W$, $U = R/X$, and use $du = dt/X^2$. With a suitably signed Brownian motion $\beta$ in this clock, Itô's formula gives

$$ dU_u = \sqrt{k}(1 - U_u)d\beta_u + (1 - U_u)\left(\frac{\rho + 2}{U_u} + 2 - k\right) du, \tag{3} $$

$$ d\log X_u = \sqrt{k}\, d\beta_u + \left(2 - \frac{k}{2} + \frac{\rho}{U_u}\right) du, \tag{4} $$

$$ d\log(1 - U_u) = -\sqrt{k}\, d\beta_u - \left[(\rho - d) + (\rho + 2)\frac{1 - U_u}{U_u}\right] du. \tag{5} $$

The instantaneous reflecting boundary convention at $U = 0$ has no local-time term; its local Bessel dimension is $1 + 2(\rho + 2)/k > 1$, so the reciprocal drift is locally integrable. More explicitly, a smooth change of coordinate making the diffusion coefficient constant gives, near zero, the corresponding dimension greater than one Bessel equation plus a bounded drift. Localized Girsanov and the integrated Bessel convention therefore construct this endpoint and give $\int_0^u U_v^{-1} dv < \infty$ on every finite $u$ interval. Construct this one-dimensional diffusion first, with this reflecting endpoint $0$ and inaccessible endpoint $1$, and reconstruct the original equations below. The multiplicative equation (5), localized below $1$, excludes reaching $1$ in finite clock time. Its scale and speed densities, up to positive constant factors, are

$$ s'(y) = y^{-\alpha}(1 - y)^{\beta_0 - 2}, \qquad m(y) = \frac{2}{k} y^\alpha (1 - y)^{-\beta_0}, \qquad \alpha = \frac{2(\rho + 2)}{k} \in (0, 1), \qquad \beta_0 = \frac{2(\rho + 4)}{k}. $$

If $\rho < d$, the speed measure is finite. The ergodic occupation law, or the renewal proof with two interior levels, yields

$$ \lim_{u \to \infty} \frac{1}{u} \int_0^u \frac{dv}{U_v} = \frac{B(\alpha, 1 - \beta_0)}{B(\alpha + 1, 1 - \beta_0)} = \frac{k - 4}{2(\rho + 2)}. $$

Equation (4) and the Brownian strong law then give

$$ \lim_{u \to \infty} \frac{\log X_u}{u} = -\frac{k - 4}{\rho + 2} < 0. $$

If $\rho > d$, equation (5) gives

$$ \limsup_{u \to \infty} \frac{\log(1 - U_u)}{u} \leq -(\rho - d) < 0. $$

Thus $U_u \to 1$, $\int_0^\infty (1 - U_u)/U_u\, du < \infty$, and

$$ \lim_{u \to \infty} \frac{\log X_u}{u} = \rho - c < 0. $$

At $\rho = d$ the diffusion is recurrent with infinite speed measure. The function $g(y) = (1 - y)/y$ is speed-integrable:

$$ \int_0^1 g(y) m(y) dy = \frac{2}{k} \int^1 y^{\alpha - 1} dy < \infty, \qquad \int_0^1 m(y) dy = \infty. $$
